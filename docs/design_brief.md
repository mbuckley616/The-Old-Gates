# The Old Gates — design brief

Michael's words, gathered by Claude on 27 Sep 2026 from the playtest of build s162. This page is the team's north star: every proposal in `docs/design/` and every session argues from it. Michael edits it; the agents do not.

## What this is
A lightweight, browser-playable open-world RPG that is the marriage of the best of Daggerfall, Morrowind, Oblivion and Skyrim — set in its own world (see `docs/lore_canon.md`). The ambition is stated plainly: build Michael's idealised Elder Scrolls before Bethesda ships theirs. The early versions took basic forms of every system while we learned what could be built; that constraint is gone. Blow the doors off.

## The three things it must feel like
1. **Real decisions with tangible consequences.** Choices change the world you can see — towns, factions, who trusts you, what is open to you.
2. **A challenging, rewarding progression.** Skills that grow by use, perks that change how you play, a world that pushes back.
3. **Fluid movement and weighty combat.** Timing, dodge, parry, counters — Elden Ring's shape at Dragonwilds' difficulty. Movement good enough to build platforming on.

## Look
As polished and realistic as a browser can carry without becoming heavyweight: stylised smooth, built in code. Interfaces read as parchment and scroll in Oblivion's manner, never digital or corporate. The build must stay playable on a laptop in a browser.

## Keep the door open for
Co-op (a friend joins your world with their character) and perhaps a PvP arena. Survival skills (mining, woodcutting, cooking, alchemy) as part of the progression. A Morrowind-style journal with an in-world calendar.

## Not this
A heavyweight download. A punishing Souls-like. Systems that reduce to *buy more/better*. Placeholder plainness kept because it was there first.

## Open questions (Michael to answer)
- Who is the player? A solo adult who loves the Elder Scrolls; anyone else?
- A session of play: twenty minutes on a laptop, or evenings?
- Death: what does it cost?
- How much of the story is authored versus emergent?
- Difficulty: one setting, or a choice?
- Scale: one continent deep, or the whole map wide?
