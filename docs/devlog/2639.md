**The item.** Michael's A on #1983 (24 Nov) is *"by what each serves, windmill and lighthouse first"*. The question's A placed *"a
lighthouse on the nearest point of shore to each port within a kilometre"*. The mill came in at Session 2621. The lighthouse has been a
finished prototype since Session 967 (`gatelandsLighthouse` in `places.js`; OG-046's lighthouses and beacons, OG-058's waystation
lighthouse, after Hook Head). No Gatelands port had one.

**The piece** (`js/38-kit.js`, `PKIT.places.lighthouse` and `PKIT.places.lighthouseLit`). It is the prototype as drawn:
- the limewashed tower, battered from 7.6 m to 6.6 m at the gallery 20 m up, on its tarred plinth, with two tarred bands, the red door
  landward on its step and six windows up the stair's turn;
- the stone gallery on 24 corbels with its iron railing;
- the twelve-sided lantern on its pedestal, with red astragals, the lamp in its lens, the copper dome, the ball and the vane;
- the keeper's cottage (the kit's `PLANS.cottage`, red) 15 m landward, the yard's rubble wall round it, and a path of flags from door to door.

What differs:
- The ground is the game's (`o.ground`). The prototype's headland, its cliff, the rocks along the break and its sea plane are left out.
- The kit returns its solids: the cottage, and the yard wall in eleven runs of four stones.
- The lamp's material is shared, so `lighthouseLit(on)` lights every lamp at once. The lens now glows with the lamp (1.6), so the light
  reads from the sea where there is no bloom.

**In the world** (`js/82-world-structures.js`):
- **Where.** `lighthousesAt(c)` finds a spot for each Gatelands port, read by place (`withPlace` over the port). It searches rings from
  70 m past the pad out to 1,000 m, 10 m apart, 64 bearings a ring, with the bearings turned by the port's own stream (`lighthouse:<site>`).
  The first spot that passes wins, which makes it the nearest shore. A spot passes when:
  - the land is 3 to 40 m over the sea and within 1.5 m across 6 m round;
  - the sea is on at least 4 of 16 bearings 32 m out, and the land under the yard is over 1.5 m;
  - it keeps 60 m from every pad and door, 25 m from every road's line, 30 m from every lake's rim and 50 m from every mill.
  The lantern faces down the middle of the sea's bearings, and the headland is level at the higher of the tower's ground and the yard's.
  My first try drew 96 random spots per port and found 10 of 34: the shore is a thin band, and random spots missed it. A grid probe showed
  30 to 32 of 34 have one, so the search walks rings instead.
- **The headland** (`lighthouseStamps`, the cell's first step with the mills). The tower's pad is level 5 m round, the yard's is level
  11 m round its middle, and both ease over 6 m. There is a 24 m clearing, and open turf to 45 m (`graze`, the mill fields' rule), so the
  tower reads from the sea. The first pictures had one in a wood. The first level was the tower's own spot, and where the land rose inland
  that sank the yard into a dip; the higher of the two now puts it on a rise.
- **The level of detail** (`placeLighthouses`, `lightFar`). Near, the kit as drawn. From 82 m (`LIGHT_LOD` 70 plus 12), the far copy:
  everything but the glazing and the lens baked into one vertex-coloured mesh on `VC_MAT`, plus the lamp on its own material at three
  times its size, so it still shows lit far out. The far copy is 2 draws.
- **Solids.** The tower is two boxes, square and turned 45°, its round to 20 m. The cottage and the yard wall's runs are solid. The path
  between the doors is open.
- **The lamp** burns from 19h to 7h (`tickLighthouses`, called from `tickMills`). It reads the clock and switches the shared material only
  when the hour crosses.
