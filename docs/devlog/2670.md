## v80 — Session 2670 — towns dressed: yards and their walls, the streets paved, the lawns walled into plots, by island
A deep-work side session of 25 Nov 2026, one of three Michael asked for to close the gap between the game's towns and the concept art (P10b,
OG-147). The land's relief and the light and grade are the other two sessions; this one owns the towns' dressing, in
`js/82-world-structures.js` and `genSettlement` in `js/83-world-generator.js`, on `auto/fable-town-dressing` (FREEZE #2042 names the two files).

**What was wrong.** The critic's street shot of 24 Nov (`docs/critic/2026-11-24-street-10h.jpg`) and the before pictures here show it.
The houses are good, but they stand on wide, flat lawn. The streets are pale 2.4 m slabs with the houses set back 2.8 m from them, the square
is grass, and between the houses there is almost nothing: no garden walls, hedges, yards, woodpiles, carts, washing or paving. From the roofs,
Dunmore is a green field with houses on it. The concept art's towns (L5, L9: the Gatelands' dry-stone walls and hedges, the Mark's carved
timber and turf, Aurenne's limewash, tile and courtyards) are dense, layered and plainly in use.

**What changed.** One pass, `townDress(S,o)` (82), runs at the end of `genSettlement` on the new look for every village, town, city, port,
garrison and outpost. It runs once every house, plot, rack, garden, ring and stall stands, just before the bake. Every roll comes from its own
stream keyed by the site and the house (`seededRng(site.id,'dress:'+house)`, and `:streets`, `:infill`, `:ground`). These are drawn after
every draw the town made, so no name, barrel or person moves, and a reload lays the dressing the same (checked: a rebuild hashes the same).
- **Each house's yard.** It goes behind the house, as deep as is clear (scanned back in half-metre rows, kept at 2.2 m to 5 m), and as wide as
  the house and a little. It is walled as its island walls: the Gatelands in dry stone (three courses with "cock and hen" coping) or a hedge
  (more hedge in villages and poor towns); the Mark in slanted-pole fencing or, in rich towns, a stone wall with a turf top; Aurenne in limewash
  under a tile coping, with bougainvillea over it, or cane where it is poor. A gate is left in one side. What is in the yard comes from the
  island's list: washing on a line, a woodpile or a peat stack, beds of greens, a water butt, a coop with hens, an apple tree; logs and a birch
  in the Mark; jars, a lemon, an olive, a cypress in Aurenne. A home you own keeps its yard open, so what you fit in it fits.
- **The frontage.** From each house's front corner to the next house along the row, set back half a metre, a wall of the same kind runs,
  with a 1.3 m gate in it where the gap is over 3.4 m. This closes the street line, as the concept's towns do.
- **Against the walls.** Ivy up a side wall (bougainvillea in Aurenne, little in the Mark), shrubs and flowers along the front clear of the
  door by 1.45 m (foxglove pinks and gorse yellows in the Gatelands, pots of geraniums in Aurenne), a bench by a home's door, a water butt
  at a corner.
- **The trades.** Beside each shop's door, 2.4 m to 3.4 m along its front: crates, a cart and sacks at a general goods; barrels, a table
  with benches and a cart at an inn; a grindstone, a trough and barrels at a smith; pots and beds at an apothecary; timber at a shipwright.
- **The streets.** Carts (with hay, barrels or sacks), barrels, troughs, crates and benches on the verges, more of them in a prosperous town
  (14% to 36% of the places tried).
- **The lawns.** The free ground behind and between the houses is tiled with walled plots, squared to the town's longest street, 5 by 4 m
  to 9 by 7 m. Each is a kitchen garden, an orchard (apples; lemons in Aurenne), a work yard (a shed, a woodpile, a cart), a paddock with a
  rick and a trough, a birch grove in the Mark, or a courtyard with an olive, jars and pots in Aurenne. Each is walled as the island walls,
  with its gate toward the town's middle, up to 0.7 of the town's houses in number.
- **The ground** (as toned down after Michael's look at the first sheets; see below). The square is laid in rings round the well, out to its
  third-nearest building's frontage (9 m to 22 m), with a wandering edge that goes ragged and mossy over its last 1.6 m. It stops at a
  building, a plot (the church yard keeps its grass; the market house's square is paved) or a road. The main streets, the ones through the
  town's middle (within 7 m of it), are laid in small setts 4.3 m wide so they meet the house fronts. So is a road through a paved town
  (`buildRoadRibbons`, `tdrTownAt`), with clean edges across the square. Every other street, the ring lane and the footpaths are worn earth
  tracks, and each door has four flags before it. Strips have a worn verge whose outer edge dips 3 cm to 10 cm under the turf, so where it
  comes up is ragged. Villages are earth throughout, their squares too. All of it is vertex colour on `VC_MAT`'s own geometry, never a tint of
  the material. The old flat ribbon of the paths is removed once the pass has run, and the ground leaves the snow's list with its town
  (`disposeSettlement`).
- **Kept clear.** Nothing solid goes within 2.2 m of a door, its step-out point or a standing townsperson, and nothing soft within 1.25 m.
  Nothing goes on a road, a walk (`S.paths`, the footpaths included), a plot, a rack, a garden, a ring, a stall or the ring lane, or in the sea.
  The walls, sheds, ricks, carts, stacks and trees are solids (`dress:true`; the walls also `bt:'wall'`, so the local map draws them as walls).
  The flowers, ivy, pots, benches and washing are not. The smith's racks and your garden, fitted later, ignore the dressing's solids.
- **Draws.** Every piece is pushed in world space into one list per 60 m of the town and per level, then merged: near (`'hi'`, by
  `houseLod`'s rule through `S.lodMeshes` with `dress:` keys, so nothing else's twin is taken), far (`'lo'`: the walls as one box per run, the
  sheds, ricks and trees as boxes and blobs), and the ground (always drawn, no shadow cast). A town adds about 30 meshes, a third of them in
  view at once.

**Paving toned down** (Michael on the first sheets, carried by the producer's session: "Tone paving down first"). He saw what read as
oversized, even floor tiles laid wall to wall: every street was setts edge to edge, so from the roofs the Gatelands' centre went grey and the
town lost its green. He liked the squares. Now:
- Only the square, the main streets and the doorsteps are paved; the rest is earth with grass at its margins.
- The setts are about 0.3 m (0.7 m before). Each strip is laid in courses of varied length with staggered joints, so it is no longer a grid.
- Each stone's tone varies more, with an odd dark, pale or green one among them.
- Moss sits in the joints, and more toward the edges, where stones go missing and the turf shows.
- The same restraint holds in the Mark and Aurenne, each in its own stone.
- A main street now stops at the square, whose stones lie over it: its domed stones poked through the square's joints.
The hedges are noted, not changed, this round.

**Lots.** I did not tighten the lots. A house's id is its lot's place in the town's layout (S243), and saves key your house, its garden and
its racks by that id, so moving the lots moves every id. The plots and the frontage walls fill the gaps instead. This is raised below.

**Streets that look empty.** Noted for the systems builder; who walks there is unchanged. In Dunmore at 10h the square holds five or six
people, and the streets away from it one or two in view. The Mark's town and Aurenne's show the same.

### Decision
`DECISION: tighten towns' lots, or fill the gaps around them?` (#2053). A house's id is its lot's place in the layout (S243), so tightening
the lots moves every id and an old save's house with it.
- **A (recommended).** Leave the lots where they are. The dressing fills the gaps, and no id moves.
- **B.** Add infill buildings after the existing lots (ids past every lot's, as FARM_N does), in the widest gaps. Any added residents are the
  systems builder's to decide.
- **C.** Re-lay the layout tighter, with a one-time save migration that re-keys owned houses by position.
