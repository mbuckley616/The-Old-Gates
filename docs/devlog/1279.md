The session's work is Michael's playtest note from today (backlog D): "it is spiking in towns … something causing hard lag spikes
in the robes/cloaks of some of the NPCs." Profiled in Dunmore at noon on the new look, in this container's headless Chromium:
- **The build.** A townsperson's builder body is made in one frame. `HB.makePerson` takes 0.3 to 1.3 s and `hbMerge` 0.1 to 0.4 s
  on top. Walking into a town builds one body every 250 ms (`HBODY.gap`), nearest first. The dearest bodies were a cloaked woman
  with a basket (1,270 ms), a man with his hood up (1,174 ms) and a woman in mail (927 ms).
- **The first sight.** The first body drawn in a garment kind compiles that kind's programs on the frame it is drawn. The cloaked
  woman with the basket compiled 13 programs in a 1,913 ms frame, and a girl in apron, bib and braids 9 programs in 1,337 ms. The
  wardrobe is bounded: 36 rolled townsfolk of all four peoples use 61 distinct programs, so this happens once per kind, but each
  time it is a stall. That is Session 1280.

A CPU profile of `makePerson` over 37 bodies put most of the time in two places. The first was posing, which was three's
`getVertexPosition` vertex by vertex. Each weight multiplied its bone's matrix by its inverse again, and `posedBody`/`posedOf` are
called over and over (the hair's drape, the hood, the basket's lift, the fist on a grip, the hands in a skirt). The second was
`surfGrid`'s `near`, which looked in 27 cells of a `Map` for every point of the hair and the hood.
- `skinnedPositions(m, n)` does what `getVertexPosition` does, but makes each bone's matrix once per call and writes out
  `Vector3.applyMatrix4` term for term in three's order. So it gives every digit the same as before. It falls back to three's own
  method for a mesh with morphs or with interleaved or normalized skin attributes.
- `surfGrid` keeps its cells as one block over the points' bounds. Each cell's points sit in a run of one array, in the order they
  came, and their coordinates are in three flat arrays. `near` walks the same 27 cells in the same order with the same strict `<`,
  so it finds the same nearest point. `heightOver` takes the dot product without cloning a vector.

`vendor/people.builder.js` is rebuilt from `people.js` by `scripts/people_bundle.py`.

### Needs eyes
- How a town feels on Michael's machine. A build is still one frame; it is now about a third shorter. Spreading the build over
  several frames would mean making `makePerson` resumable, and that is a bigger change.
- **The first-sight compile, tried and not kept.** I tried `hbWarm`: after a body is built, three's `compileAsync` runs on all its
  parts, inside `LAND_BLOOM.rt` because three keys a program by the target it is drawn into. The body would stand in its driver's
  copy until the programs were ready, at most 1.5 s. Its programs then matched the drawn ones (91 against 89 without it). But this
  container's SwiftShader has no `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`, and the first-sight frames got much worse here: 11.7 s and 18.7 s
  for the two bodies that took 2.0 s and 1.3 s without it, 30.4 s in all against 3.4 s. A warm-up can only be judged on a real GPU
  with the extension (Chrome on Windows or a Mac has it). The other way is a prewarm behind the loading fade, as `prewarmFoes` does
  for the foes, of a few townsfolk dressed to cover the 61 programs. That costs about a second of world entry a body on CI. It is
  owed, with numbers from a real machine.
