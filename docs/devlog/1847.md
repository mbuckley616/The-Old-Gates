**The flags** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/ships.js`, `BANNERS`, `flagTex`):
- **The colours are the canon's.** Lore canon §1.5's national signage gives the Crown crimson-and-gold, the Mark black-and-iron and the
  Compact blue-and-white. These are the game's own `NATIONS[].banner` and `.trim` (`87-world-quests.js`), already used by the forts' banners.
- **The devices are the sails' marks, where an island has one.** OG-004 gives the harp and the sun-star, and its catalogue row says they
  fill the canon's gap, while its lion and wolf are invented heraldry. So the Crown's flag is crimson with a gold harp, and the Compact's is
  blue with a white sun-star. The Mark's sail mark is its sewn stripes, not an emblem, so its flag is black with a band of iron across it,
  split by a black line. Each flag is hemmed in its trim. The harp and the star are the sails' own drawings (`drawHarp`, `drawStar`,
  taken out of `sailTex` and `caravelSail` unchanged, so both painters share them).
- **Your house's** is not drawn. A house has no arms anywhere in the game yet.

**Where it flies** (`banner(S, nation, o)`):
- **On a staff at the stern**, on the centreline, raked 6° aft. On a castle hull the staff stands on the aft castle. The flag flies aft
  on the wind with a ripple and a droop, and turns .35 rad to port by default (`o.yaw`).
- **The hoist is 7% of her length** (0.8 to 2.4 m), and the fly is 1.5 times the hoist: 0.91 × 1.37 m on the sloop, 1.23 × 1.84 m on the
  caravel, 1.54 × 2.31 m on the galleon. The hoist's foot stands 1.2 m over the rail, so it flies over heads, and the fly hangs out over
  the water.
- **A gaff boom overhangs the stern** on the Mark's sloop, cutter, caravel and cog, and on the Gatelands' and the Mark's galleons
  (their mizzen). There the boom would swing through a stern staff. A first picture showed the cutter's boom through the flag. On those
  hulls the banner flies instead from a pole on the head of the mast that carries the boom, as a gaff rig's colours are carried aloft.
  The masthead is found as the highest spar vertex within 0.35 m of the mast's line. `o.at` forces either place.
- **The cost.** Every banner is 388 triangles in 2 draws (the staff with its ball, and the flag of 16 × 8 quads). The flag's material is
  one per nation, shared by every hull. `'none'` removes a banner that is already flying.

`ships.html?banner=gatelands|mark|aurenne|none` (or `banner=1`, her island's) puts it on any hull. `ships-banner-sheet.mjs` draws the sheets.

### Decision
Asked as #1150 (DECISION: a ship’s banner, the three nations’ flags and where they fly):

**The question.** The ship sheet's session 5 (your B on #979) has the yard sell a banner: the three nations', your house's once you own one, or none. Session 1847 (look builder, `auto/look-1847`) drew them in the ship kit. The colours are the canon's national signage (the Crown crimson and gold, the Mark black and iron, the Compact blue and white). The devices are the marks the sails already carry (OG-004's harp and sun-star), and the Mark gets a band of iron, since its sails carry stripes, not an emblem. Each flag flies from a staff at the stern, except where a gaff boom swings over the stern (the Mark's small hulls, and the galleons' mizzen). There it flies from a pole on that masthead. Each banner is 388 triangles in 2 draws. Pictures: [the five classes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mbuckley616/The-Old-Gates/auto/look-1847/docs/prototypes/levelup/ships-banner.jpg), [close views](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mbuckley616/The-Old-Gates/auto/look-1847/docs/prototypes/levelup/ships-banner-close.jpg).

**Options.**
- **A (recommended).** As drawn: the harp, the iron band and the sun-star, on the stern staff or the boom's masthead.
- **B.** As drawn, but the Mark's flag carries OG-147's runic wolf, not the iron band. It is a new emblem, and OG-004's notes call its wolf invented heraldry.
- **C.** As drawn, but every hull flies it at the masthead: one place for all, seen from farther off, and no stern staffs.

**Recommendation.** A: it uses only the canon's colours and the marks the sails already carry. The stern staff keeps the flag in sight from the helm wherever the rig allows.
