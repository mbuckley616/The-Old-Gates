## v80 — Session 2682 — the land's relief and ground: uplands, ridges, scarps and cliff coasts between the places; a painted sward
A deep-work session on `auto/fable-land-relief`, one of three run side by side for the new look (the light and grade on
`auto/fable-light-grade`, the towns' dressing on `auto/fable-town-dressing`). The ask: the open land should read as P10b's landscapes
(OG-147) and not, as the critic's `docs/critic/2026-11-23-storm-inland-13h.jpg` has it, a mown lawn flat to the horizon. This session owned
the terrain's shape in `js/80-world-terrain.js` (`landH`, the far terrain) and the ground's look in `js/89-world-land.js`
(`landTerrainMat` and `landMask`), and nothing of the sky, light or grade. FREEZE #2044 held those two files while it ran. Written as
2668 at first and moved to 2682: main reached 2681 while the work was in hand. The code's notes are tagged S2682 (S2659, the first tag, is
another session's on main).

**What was wrong.** `landH` laid hills of ±5 to 16 m on a 110 m wave over the whole of every island, a continental swell and a 60 m
ridged ripple; above that only the ranges' spines (`ridgeAt`) and the authored peaks stood up. Sampled every 60 m over the land, the
Gatelands' median was 6 m above the sea and nine points in ten under 14 m; the Mark and Aurenne the same within a metre. From the
eye at 1.65 m nothing between the places stood against the sky. The ground was groundColor's biome colour with a fine noise over it, so
what there was read as one even green.

**The relief (`RELIEF`, `reliefAt`, `cliffAt`, in `80-world-terrain.js` just after `ridged`).** Four landforms on top of `landH`'s,
by nation:
- *uplands*: broad patches of high country, a 1.5 km field crossing a level by nation (the Mark has the most);
- *hills and ridges* on them: a ridged field on a 520 m wave with its crests sharpened (`pow 2.4`) and a 240 m swell, up to 46 m in the
  Gatelands, 78 in the Mark, 38 in Aurenne;
- *escarpments*: where a 760 m field crosses two levels in the scarp country (a 2.1 km field), a tableland and its upper shelf with
  faces of 22 to 26 m (Benbulbin's kind; the skyline's mesas in the pictures);
- *cliff coasts*: stretches of shore where the land stands 30 to 44 m to the brink and the sea takes it in a short band (sea .3 to .55
  instead of 0 to 1);
and the ranges' crests broken into crags and summits (a 150 m ridged field over the spine, 42 m at the top), scaled by nation (the Mark
×1.45). The amplitudes are blended between cells' centres, so no border shows a step; the home province is a fifth gentler and a sea
cell's islets under half.

**The calm field.** All of it is held down near what people built and where the water runs. `calmGrid(i,j)` is one 32 m grid a cell,
built once from the 3×3 cells around it: 0 on and round every site's pad (pad + 28 m), every door (`fixedDoorSpot`; a fort's compound
95 m, a cave's or a gate's 40), every road's line (`roadCurve`, which reads only the road's def and its ends), every routed river and
lake (`RV.reaches`, `RV.lakes`) and the home province's authored rivers, lake and inlet, rising to 1 over the next 110 to 190 m. So the
towns, roads and gates keep the ground they had and the rivers run in broad valleys between the high country. The calm field reads
`getCell` (which reads no height) and the routing alone, so the relief is a pure function of the seed and the place, the same whatever
has loaded.

**The rivers are unchanged.** A first cut let the relief into the routing's lattice and the rivers re-routed (the navigable length fell
from 86,141 m to 34,014, the lakes went from 42 to 80, the joins from 88 to 16). `reliefLive()` now returns false while `RV.routing`
is set: the routing reads the land exactly as main's did (205 reaches, 42 lakes, 88 joins, 86,141 m navigable, all as main), and the
valleys are then cut round what it found.

**The ground (`landCol` in `landTerrainMat`).** The sward painted, not mown: broad patches of warm sunlit grass, cool lush grass and dun
(a 170 m and a 64 m field); long soft brush strokes that run one way a patch and coarsen with distance; drier olive and bracken on the
slopes, lusher green on low flat ground, heather browns on the high ground, and sheep tracks along the contours of a hillside within
140 m. Rock breaks through the turf on the steep (slope .3 to .52) and in crags on a hillside (a 16 m field, likelier the steeper), in
warm greys with soft strata, joints, lichen and moss on its ledges; snow keeps to a cliff's gentler parts. The woods channel of
`landMask` now stands only under the scatter's tree line (30 to 44 m) and off the steep, so a bare upland no longer reads as forest from
afar.

**The far terrain.** On the new look the far sheet is drawn by the land's own material (`LAND.farMat`, `landTerrainMat(true)`) with its
mask laid as a chunk's is, so the ridges on the skyline are the same country as the ground at your feet, not a flat lit sheet; it
no longer darkens itself for canopy (the material paints the woods). Its points are 16 or 32 m apart and 3 m under the land, and where the
land folds between them the sheet stood out of the near ground in pale disks (on main too, on the range north of the Gates; see
`before-gatenorth.jpg`). Within the loaded chunks, which cover it anyway, the far copy is let down 30 m in its vertex shader, the
ring's radius read live (`RADIUS*CHUNK`), so a chunk not yet built still shows land there, only lower.

**What the numbers say** (60 m sampling of `landH` over the land, before → after): the Gatelands' mean height 11.7 → 23.9 m, nine in ten
under 14.3 → 51.6 m, mean slope .095 → .202, ground steeper than 1 in 2 1.9 % → 5.0 %; the Mark's mean 12.1 → 37.5 m, highest 127 →
320 m, steep 1.6 % → 8.9 %; Aurenne's mean 11.2 → 27.1 m, nine in ten under 13.3 → 62.4 m. About a quarter of the land is held flat by the
calm field. Of 88 site pads, 23 now have ground more than 12 m off their level 40 m past their blend (16 on main): those pads stand in a
slightly deeper fold than before, and the stamps' blend takes them as it did.
