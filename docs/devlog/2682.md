## v80 — Session 2682 — The light by the hour: a curve, the islands' palettes, haze, painted clouds (deep work, light and grade)
Michael asked on 25 Nov 2026 for side sessions to close the gap between the game and the concept art; this one owns the light,
the sky and the grade (the land's relief and the towns' dressing are two siblings'). The critic's shots on main
(`docs/critic/2026-11-24-street-10h.jpg`, `…-23-storm-inland-13h.jpg`, `…-25-light-night-sea.jpg`) showed why it fell short of P10b
(OG-147): every daytime frame was the same noon. Three causes in the code. The sun's light never sank below an elevation of 0.32
(`atmosphere()` clamped it), and its colour and strength moved only with the night factor, so 7h, 10h and 16h were one light. The
ambient (0.55) and the hemisphere (0.55) together matched the sun (1.15), so a shadow was barely a shade darker than the lit side.
And the haze was the biome's one grey-green FogExp2: the square in its exponent keeps the first 300 m clear and then shuts like a
door, so a ridge at 900 m was simply gone, never blue. At night the sun's light stayed warm at 0.16 from 0.32 up, with the ambient
at 0.16 and the hemisphere at 0.13: walls stayed lit as if floodlit, and the sea stayed bright blue. Stars showed in a sunset sky
(they came up with the night factor, which is 0.5 at 18h).

**The light by the hour** (`89-world-land.js`, `LIGHT_KEYS`, `lightAt`, `landHour`, `landLights`). One curve, keyed at the hours
P10b's lighting panel paints: night (0h to 4.3h and from 20.6h), the blue before dawn (5.2h), sunrise (6h), the early gold (7h),
morning (8.5h), noon (12.5h), afternoon (15.5h), golden hour (17h), sunset (17.85h), dusk (18.5h) and the blue hour (19.3h). Each key
holds the sun's colour and strength, the ambient and the hemisphere (sky and ground colours), the dome's zenith, middle and horizon,
the haze's colour and density, the sun's glow, the exposure, the grade's warmth, saturation and contrast, and the post's bloom and
vignette; between keys the values ease by smoothstep. By day the ambient is about a sixth of the sun (0.24 against 1.6 at noon) and
the hemisphere carries the sky's blue into the shade, so shadows read and are cool; at a low sun the fill rises (0.26 to 0.3 with a
hemisphere of 0.44) so the land under a grazing light is warm and readable, not black. The light itself is the real sun down to an
elevation of 0.1 (golden hour's long shadows), and when the sun is down it turns to the moon, on the moon's own path, scaled by its
phase (0.3 at new to 1 at full) and by whether it is up: night is lit cool and blue from above, and lamps are what is warm. On
top of the curve, the island's palette (P10b's regional palettes): the Gatelands as the curve, a touch of green in the haze; the Mark
colder sun and ambient, a deeper zenith, a bluer, thinner haze (0.75), the grade cooled and a little less saturated; Aurenne an ochre
sun, a warm horizon and haze, the grade warmed and saturated (+0.12), and a turquoise tint on the sea. Each region's island is its
centre cell's nation, and the palettes are blended by `regionWeights`, so a strait between two islands is never a seam; the biome
nudges on top (a forest's and a fen's green, thicker haze, the wastes' and dunes' dust, the tundra's cold). Then the weather: cloud
greys the sky and the haze toward their own luminance, raises the ambient (an overcast's light comes from everywhere), thickens the
haze and flattens the grade; a storm darkens all of it. The weather's FogExp2 (its sight in metres, `WX_SIGHT`, which the foes'
sight shares) is `applyWeather`'s as before.

**The haze** (`OG_U`, three's fog chunks). An exponential haze in distance, over three's own fog: `1 − exp(−d·D)` toward the haze
colour, D from 0.00062 at noon to 0.0013 at sunrise, thinner as you climb, capped at 0.92. The plain exponential lays the planes one
behind another, each a step nearer the sky's colour, where the square shut the view; the biome's own FogExp2 density is cut to 0.45
of what it was on the new look (a forest's closeness stays). The dome's foot is painted in the same haze colour, so far land melts
into the sky with no line. The haze and the grade are uniforms every material shares with no list to keep: three clones a built-in
material's uniforms from `ShaderLib` when it compiles it, and a value that is a plain object (not a Color or Vector) is copied by
reference, so one object reaches every material. They are set only for the open world's own frame (`landPost`'s onBeforeRender,
and around the bloom's composite) and zero otherwise, and zero is the old output exactly, so gates, rooms and the hands are
untouched (the gates stay as dark as they were).

**The grade** (`landGrade`). P10b's S1180 grade (cool shadows, warm highlights, saturation 1.14) now moves with the hour: warmth
pushes the highlights to amber at golden hour and the shadows to blue at night, saturation from −0.12 at night to +0.16 at golden
hour, a little contrast about the middle. Room is left for visual-foundation.md's corruption level: `OG_U.gradeB` drains the colour
toward grey and turns the hue (a torn gate's school), from `LAND.drain` and `LAND.hue`, both 0 until that card sets them.

**The sky** (`landSky`). The sun's glow spreads along the horizon as a band when it is low (the sunset's), a broad slow variation
breaks the flat gradient, and High adds thin cirrus lit by the glow. Stars wait for the blue hour on the new look.

**The clouds** (`landClouds`). Each heap's puffs are shaded together, by the heap's own roundness (out from its centre, up at its
top), so a heap has one lit side and one shadow side with a soft terminator; each puff's edge is eroded by noise, so the outline is
torn rather than a ring of circles; the heap is cut flat at its base (cumulus sit on a level) and darkest there; a sun behind lights
the edge. Lit tops take the sun's colour, the shade the sky's middle: gold and violet at a low sun, grey under an overcast (and
swollen, `uGrow`), dim silver under the moon. Still one draw call; 18 to 28 puffs a heap at Low, 26 to 41 at Middle, 34 to 54 at High.
