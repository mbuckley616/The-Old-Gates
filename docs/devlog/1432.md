## v80 — Session 1432 — First Words: the townsperson's greeting, by who speaks and who listens

The quest writer's build of *First Words* (approved by Michael, 14 Oct 2026), owed to his control-room note of 5 Oct: "There's too much exposition when greeting you ('You're a markmen? those people kill bears at 10!') — should be more subtle intros, like 'Greetings, Markman.' Or 'What is it?'". A stranger's first words were built from three parts: a stock line, half the time *You're a Markman, by the look of you.*, and then one of the speaker's opinions of your people from the canon's §2.1 table. A census and a verdict at the door. The return lines (*Back again?*, *You. Good.*) were one pool for all four peoples, so an Aurennais said *You. Good.*

The greeting getter in `dialogFor` (`87-world-quests.js`) now calls `firstWords(def,site)`, beside `PEOPLES`, and returns one line. A first meeting takes a line of `GREET_FIRST[speaker][listener]`, or one time in three, for anyone with a trade's own pool (any role but Villager), that trade's line (`def._roleGreet`, set in `makeDef`, `83-world-generator.js`). A later meeting takes the speaker's people's `GREET_AGAIN`, or one time in three the temper's line; to an Old Blood player, `GREET_AGAIN_OB`, where *I never could look straight at you, could I.* now lives. An Old Blood player's first meeting carries one of the canon's four descriptions after the address, one time in three (`peopleGreeting`, which keeps only that branch). The tags (*Neighbour.*, *Guildsman.*) are in the speaker's people's words (`GREET_TAG`: a Markman says *Guild-hand.*, an Aurennais *We are neighbours now, I believe.*). Every line is the draft's, word for word. The §2.1 opinions are unchanged and still answer *What do you make of the …?*. Feast days keep their precedence, and the great gates' tool lines still come before the greeting. The pious temper's lines (Finding 18) were already on main. Which line is said is cosmetic (`Math.random`); no new saved key, since `worldState.met` already counts meetings. `PEOPLES[…].greet` is unread now and left in place.
