## v80 — Session 1509 — the knife, the spear and the crossbow carried at their own size
No freeze was open, the last bodies run had landed (#810), and section I holds nothing for this lane. Session 1504 left an item owed: the
third-person crossbow was 1.3 m from stirrup to butt, larger than a real stirrup crossbow. I put that down then to the hand's bones
scaling what they hold about 1.17×.

**What it was.** That reading was wrong, and this is a correction to Session 1504's entry. The hand's bones are not scaled. Every piece a
body carries takes the scale of the body's root: the driver's `personScale` × `PW.BODY`, 1.19–1.35 by stature (the player's 1.35 at
1.87 m). The builder's copy is carried on the driver's piece (`tpHBHold`, `hbHold`), so it takes that scale too. That is intended for
most of the weapon kit (`wpnBuild`), because it was drawn to the old person, 1.233 tall: at ×1.35 a sword is 0.89 m, a longsword 1.17,
a claymore 1.45, an axe 0.76 and a mace 0.68, all about true. Three pieces were drawn later in true metres, and they came out too big.
The knife (0.36 m) was 0.49, the spear (2.20) was 2.97, and the crossbow (1.04, Session 1458) was 1.40.

**What changed.**
- `32-people.js`: `WPN_TRUE` names the three (`dagger`, `spear`, `crossbow`). `wpnTrue(kind, w, holder)` scales such a piece by one
  over its holder's root, so it is carried at world scale 1 whatever the stature. The foes' kit weapon takes it when `buildFoe` hangs it
  on the gear bone. Only the Cultist's and the Goblin's knife are affected: the foes' spear is the builder's own.
- `54-thirdperson.js`: `tpTrue(R)` does the same for the player's piece once the root's scale is known. It also scales what is drawn in
  that piece's frame: an enchantment's motes and the left hand's place on a two-handed grip. This happens before the builder's copy is
  made, so the copy takes it too. First person is untouched: it builds its own piece through `tpWeapon`, and only the third-person
  build scales it.
- The span (`TP_XSPAN.lean`, 1.2→1.0 to 1.35→1.08). The shorter bow stands with its butt at the chest instead of over the head, but its
  string is lower. With the old bow the left wrist fell 0.16 m short of the string at 1 s. The back now bows a little deeper, which
  puts both wrists back on the string. Every other span number holds, because the span reads the piece's own scale (`ku`).
