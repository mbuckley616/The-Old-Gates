## v80 — Session 1527 — the shark's gill slits drawn as lines
Session 1525 left a note under "Needs eyes": at the line-up's distance the reef shark's painted gill bands aliased into a small
chequer. Session 1526's close picture in the game showed the same thing close to. So the shark that had just gone into the game
wore a smudge where its gills should be.

**Why painting could not do it.** The bands were coloured into the hide's vertices. Five slits sit 0.085 head lengths apart, which is
about 1.8 cm on the kit's body at size 1, and each band was 1.6 cm wide. The hide's grid is cut at about the same pitch. So the bands
nearly touched, and the few vertices between them fell on or off a band by chance. That is the chequer
(`creatures-reefshark-gills-before.jpg`). A narrower band would only alias at a finer grain, and spacing them so the grid could show
them would spread five slits over more than a head's length.

**What changed** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/creatures.js`; `vendor/creatures.builder.js` rebuilt from it):
- `gillSlits`: a plan whose `gills` names `slit` (a width) gets its slits as geometry. Each slit is a thin ribbon laid on the grown
  skin. Rays are cast in from each side at six heights along the line the painted band followed (the same `from`, `step` and lean
  back by `tilt`), and the hits are taken from the triangles in the gills' stretch only. The ribbon is `slit` wide along the body
  (0.0035, about 8 mm on the kit's shark and 11 mm on the game's), narrowed at its two ends, and lifted 1.5 mm off the skin along the
  skin's normal. All ten slits are one mesh with 100 triangles and one draw call, in the plan's `gill` tone, double-sided. It hangs
  on the chest bone (the spine's last), which the slits lie over.
- With `slit` set, the painted bands are not drawn.
- `reefshark` sets `slit: .0035`. No other plan uses gills.
In the game the slits are a bit on the shark's body, shown near and at the middle level; only the eyes are dropped at 25 m.

### Needs eyes
- The slits are crisp black lines, darker than a real shark's, which read as a shadowed fold. At play distance that reads as gills;
  close to, a softer gill tone may suit better.
- A ribbon is rigid on the chest bone while the skin under it is skinned to the head and spine. The swim gait bends least there, but
  a hard turn could lift a slit's edge a millimetre off the skin.
