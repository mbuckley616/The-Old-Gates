## v80 — Session 1362 — a crossing takes gaps on the nave's rule (Michael's A on #664)
Session 1358 asked what a failing gate with no nave and no chasm loses: the Vault of the Tide (seed 845, catastrophic: a flooded
hall, a crossing and the stair hall on floor one, a crossing and the ring on floor two) walked exactly as a steady gate. Michael
answered A on 24 Oct: crossings take gaps on the nave's rule.

**What changed.** `gateCondition` (`js/52-dungeon-gen.js`) cuts gaps in a crossing exactly as in a nave. Each crossing gets 1 or 2
cells by 1 or 2, one per 60 m of space and at least one (a crossing is 13 cells, 39 m, so one), and twice that when catastrophic.
The gaps stay 3 cells from a threshold and 4 from another gap, and the way round is kept: no gap may split what you could reach.
On floor one of a two-floor gate a gap goes only over floor two's rock a cell round, and it is a hole in floor one's foothold, as
Session 1358 has it for the naves. The nave's loop became `cutGaps(type)`. The crossings are cut after the chasms' spans, so every
nave gap, gone span and stone a gate had before is where it was; the crossings' draws come after them on the same stream. The pit,
the fall, the minimap's black and a foe's wall all read the map's open air (8), so nothing else needed to change. Nothing about a
gate's condition is saved.

**Flooded halls stay as they are.** Option B, a flooded hall losing its islands, was not chosen. So a gate that holds only flooded
halls still shows nothing on its way. Over 400 seeds a size (220 of them failing or broken), the bare gates go from 8 to 1 medium,
8 to 1 large and 21 to 4 massive, and every one left is flooded halls alone. Tiny and small gates have a single space, and when it is
flooded (38 of 220) they stay bare. The Session 1358 entry's 41 of 185 counted these too, and A does not reach them.
