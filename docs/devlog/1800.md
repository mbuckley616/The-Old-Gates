**The valleys there are.** A probe ran a line across every routed river in Aurenne's 21 land cells, every 20 m along each. It put the
channel's walk at the lower of the two shoulders (the highest ground 15 to 75 m out each side) and ran the arcade between the points where
the ground comes up to it. The deepest crossing per cell is 4.8 to 15.8 m from the walk to the stream's bed, across 36 to 132 m. S1787's
probe measured 10.2 m at most; it took the valley from the ground beside the water, and this one takes it to the bed. So the land has
a few valleys over 12 m (three cells), and most are 7 to 10.

**The kit** (`PKIT.places.aqueduct`, `aurenneAqueduct` in `js/38-kit.js`). This is places.js's `aurenneAqueduct` (Session 907), with the
same plaster over brick, ashlar rings, string course, cornice, blue tile band, and channel with its lining, crust and copings. The
prototype brought its own 20 m valley. This one is handed the world's ground (`o.ground(x)`, the lowest across the arcade's width) and
is laid to it:
- **The tiers.** The valley's depth sets them, as #1072's A said. *One tier* under 12 m: its arches spring 5.4 m under the walk. That is
  as high as they go with the rings still under the tile band (the band is at 1.34 to 1.76 m down, the ring's top at 1.85). *Two tiers*
  at 12 m and deeper: the string course halfway down, the upper arches 0.5 m over it, the lower ones 3.6 m under it. On the drawn
  20 m valley that gives the prototype's -10.5 and -6.5 exactly.
- **The arches.** A bay opens wherever the ground under all of it lies half a metre under its spring; elsewhere the arcade is a wall
  on the slope, its foot 0.6 m into the ground. The piers keep the drawn 2.2 m on a pitch of 8.2 m. They slide along the line in half
  metres over one pitch to open the most arches; of equals, the bay's centre nearest the deepest point wins, so the stream runs
  through an arch.
- **The break.** It is over the two bays either side of the pier nearest the deepest point, where both bays are open. The tier above
  them and the channel are fallen, the ragged ends are the prototype's, and the pier between is a 2.5 m stump. In one tier it stands
  on the ground and the breach is open to walk through; in two, it stands on the string course. Forty blocks lie heaped under the gap.
- **Left out.** The prototype's own ground, stream bed, rocks and cypresses: the world's land and scatter are there instead.
- **Its solids.** The world reads every solid as a box square to its axes, whatever its turn (`solidAt` ignores `c`/`s`; the caves'
  turned boxes are read the same way). So the arcade's solids are a chain of squares as wide as the arcade, a metre apart, each kept
  inside its run. Turned any way, an arch stays open. The channel runs 4 m on into the hill at either end, solid there too.

**The places** (`aqueductAt`, `aqueductStamps`, `placeAqueduct` in `js/82-world-structures.js`, hooked into the cell's first and
statics steps in `81`). One Aurenne cell in three keeps one, by its own stream (`aqueduct:<cell>`). It is read by place (`withPlace`,
the bare land), from the cell's own routed rivers; never a navigable one, so no hull meets a pier. The lines are those of the probe,
kept when:
- 36 to 130 m long;
- 7 m or more deep;
- the walk 3 m or more over the sea;
- 18 m or more of the line lying 6 m under the walk, so at least two arches can open;
- its ends and middle 60 m off every pad, 80 m off every door, 40 m off every road's line, 40 m off every lake's rim, and 150 m off
  the cell's grotto.

A line deep enough for two tiers wins, then the one with the most ground low enough for arches, then the deepest. The first rule only
took the deepest. At `aq_7_5` that opened just the two bays of the break, because its 10.4 m is mostly the river's own cut; the suite
caught it. Stamps that only clear (`noFlat`, the land keeps its lie) take the scatter off the line every 8 m, so no tree stands in an arch. The arcade is built on the
drawn ground (`meshH`). It has no name, no marker and no E: it is found by looking, like the Weaver doors.
