**The pale bands.** The face was a grid of 220 columns by 30 even rows from the top to the foot, about 1.6 m apart. Its beds (7.3 m) step
out of one another by up to 1.6 m (the bed term), but each step fell between two rows, so it was smoothed into a facet tilted up to the sun.
The result was a soft pale smear about 1.6 m high, which faded in and out along the brink as the step grew and shrank. A broad swell (9 m
over 55 m along and 45 m up) also tilted whole stretches of the face upward. Now the rows follow the beds:
- each bed is its own strip of five rows;
- between two beds is a ledge strip 0.3 m high that carries the whole step;
- every strip has its own vertices, so its normals are its own. A bed standing out of the one above shows a thin lit ledge, a bed standing
  back shows a shadowed overhang, and the tone changes at the line;
- the swell is read at the bed's middle, so it too steps at the ledges, and each bed stands near plumb.

A row above the top or under the foot is laid on it, so the strips fit any column. That makes 39 segments a column instead of 30: the face
is 17,160 triangles (was 13,200). The strips are joined into one indexed geometry by hand, because the kit's `mergeGeometries` keeps no
index. The first try went through it and drew the face as triangle soup, slivers and all.

**The lip.** The rim's bed ran flat to the brink and met the face's top row there. The top bed now ends in a lip:

The walk is unchanged: `capAt` still holds you to 0.3 m past the brink, at the bed's height. The rim is 11,612 triangles (was 6,960).
