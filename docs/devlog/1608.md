- **Cloch Ghéar's pillar** (1.0 m across, 1.2 m, or 1.8 overshot): gate stone in three rough drums of eight sides, each turned and
  tipped a little, with dark joints between, a lichened foot, and seven stones of rubble thrown up round it. A sigil is cut four times
  round the top drum, lit amber and unlit by the scene's light: a gate's ring and its point. The first build cut a cross there, which
  read as a church's sign, and the canon's Weaver is not that church (OG-006's note). 916 triangles in 2 draws.
- **Sioc's floe** (3 m on open sea, 0.4 m under the face and 0.1 over): a broken plate of eleven sides, each between 0.96 and 1.06
  of its round, so where you stand on it and where the ice is drawn agree within 8 cm. The sides are deep blue and the top is pale
  with four cracks; four shards drift beside it. 241 triangles in 1 draw.
- **Tonn with Sioc's bridge** (6.2 by 1.6 m, 0.35 deep): a deck of blue-green ice with a pale top. Along both edges the wave's crest
  stands frozen, a lip of uneven height leaning outward with a foam-white head every half metre, all outside the walkable 1.6 m.
  Icicles hang under the deck. About 1,500 triangles in 1 draw at the full length.
- **Séideán's updraft** (1.5 m wide, 3 m drawn): three ribbons of wind, 1.5 turns each, narrowing as they rise and turning about
  the column. A ring of stirred air lies on the ground, and 24 motes are carried up the spiral. It is one additive, unlit
  material that never writes depth and never answers a ray. The motion is read from `performance.now()` in the ribbon's
  `onBeforeRender`, so it is cosmetic and costs nothing when the thing is out of view. 312 triangles and 24 points in 3 draws.

The pillar and the bridge cast shadows; the floe and the updraft do not. `laidEnd` already disposes every geometry under the group.
The four materials are shared and never disposed: the stone and the ice are `VC_MAT`, the sigil, the wind and the motes one each.
