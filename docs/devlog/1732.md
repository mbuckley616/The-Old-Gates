**Her bench's fittings** (`50-travel.js`, `SHIP_BENCH`). The bench page has six new rows, *Build stowage* through *Build arms rack*. Like the patch and the splice, they show only at her own bench (`shipWorkHere`: in her cabin, or at her side with a joiner's kit while she is hove to). The sheet's numbers:
- stowage: 6 planks and 2 spars, Joinery 25
- still: 4 planks and 2 iron ingots, Joinery 25
- galley: 4 planks and 1 iron ingot, Joinery 25
- chart table: 4 planks, Joinery 50
- crew bunks: 8 planks, Joinery 25
- arms rack: 4 planks and 1 iron ingot, Joinery 50

Each fitting is a Joinery use of 12 and takes the forge's half-hour (`FORGE_MIN`, as a bow or a buckler takes). It goes straight into her. The checks run before anything is spent, through `fitShipWhy` (`86-world-crime.js`), which is now split out of `fitShip`. The bench refuses a fitting when:
- her places are full;
- she already has one of that kind;
- the hold's goods leave no room for its weight;
- the yard already keeps one for her (it goes back in there free, so a fitting stays one of a kind).

`fitShip(k,'bench')` fits it for no gold. The sheet sets no time for a fitting. The half-hour is the bench's own precedent for a made piece, so this is the one number chosen here.

**The still and the galley** (`shipFitAtHand`). The fittings have no models yet (the look builder's, per the sheet). Until they do, both count anywhere in her hold:
- With a still fitted, `alchStrength` is ×1.10 there.
- With a galley fitted, `hearthHere` is true there, so `atFire` is true and a fish or a joint cooks below deck at sea.

Once a room records a spot (`house._fits[k] = {x,z}`, in the room's metres), each one reaches the sheet's 2 m. The still still asks for a mortar, as brewing always has. The sheet makes the still a better place to brew, not a second mortar. You can only go below while she is hove to, so "cooking at sea" means hove to off the land.
