## v80 — Session 1596 — `settingsheet`'s crash is the pointer lock in headless Chromium; the suite stands in for it (QA)
Main is green: every `check` run since s1028 passed and no `FREEZE:` is open. The one open row of QA's own in `docs/qa/flaky.md` was
`settingsheet`. Its reds are all *Target crashed* in the field-of-view wait after the resume, and the container's memory cgroup is
what kills it (S1345, S1362). Those sessions ruled out the sheet's settings one at a time and left this next step: replay the resume
with memory sampled.

Reproduced on the first try at s1041 (5f8dc8b): about 15 s after the Esc that resumes play, the browser process climbed from 92 MB to
4.4 GB and the renderer from 0.7 to 6 GB, and the container killed Chrome at 340 s. A scratch copy of the suite's start, which stops
itself at 3.5 GB, then narrowed it down:
- the whole sheet, then resume: past 4 GB at 31 s;
- a bare Esc-Esc on the leaf, no setting touched: past 4 GB at 30 s. So it is not the sheet;
- no pause at all: flat, 1.83–1.93 GB for 520 s;
- the same Esc-Esc with `CV.requestPointerLock` stood in for: flat, 1.82–1.93 GB for 515 s;
- the resume overlay hidden by CSS, with no lock: flat, 1.83–1.96 GB for 517 s;
- locked, with every `mousemove` stopped at the window before any game handler: 3.6 GB at 83 s. So it is not the game's JS. While
  locked, headless Chrome sends a zero-length trusted `mousemove` every frame, and the game's handler only adds them to the yaw.
- a blank page with one canvas, locked, 3 s busy frames: it levels off at 0.7 GB. So the climb needs the game's load too.

So the crash comes from Chrome itself holding a pointer lock on a page drawn by software GL at seconds a frame. Nothing here points to
the game's code, and nothing in it is a player's case. The suite never needed a real lock: it checks the field of view at rest. It now
stands in for `CV.requestPointerLock` and counts the asks, and it has a new check that Esc on the leaf resumes play and asks for the
pointer back (one ask). Every other check is unchanged.

`pauseleaf` also resumes with Esc into a real lock, but it ends a few frames later and has not been red. It stays as it is. If a later
suite holds a lock for long, the stand-in belongs in the harness (`tests/lib/game.mjs`), and that is a pipeline PR.
