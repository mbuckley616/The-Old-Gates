## v80 — Session 1789 — the Wait slip says when it will lose the watch (backlog I, s1180)
The systems builder's run of 3 Nov, 13:47 UTC. No freeze was open, and the lane's last branch (`auto/systems-1784`) was over two and a
half hours old. Section I held one item for this lane, filed from the critic's playtest of 3 Nov: Aoife's watch at Colmán's Rest
(*The Watch*, S1776) lapses on any use of the Wait menu inside the night, with no word first. `erWatchTick` lapses the night when the
clock moves more than 60 minutes between two of its ticks (S1776: "the clock jumping an hour or more inside the night"), and the slip's
smallest step is an hour, so every wait is a moment over 60 and loses it.

The item offers two fixes: the slip says so, or the watch accepts the slip's hour. The second changes a rule of the errand (whether a
waited night counts as a kept one), and the critic's proposal *Keep watch* (`docs/proposals.md`, 3 Nov) asks Michael for a larger
form of it, the clock run fast inside the circle. So this session builds the first, which changes no rule, and leaves the rule to that
proposal.

**What changed.**
- `erWatchJumpLoses(min)` (`87-world-quests.js`, beside `erWatchTick`): the open watch, if any, that a jump of the clock by `min` minutes
  would lapse, read as the tick reads it: the jump ends at or after the night's start, and 60 minutes or more past the later of the
  watch's last tick and the start. The tick lapses at more than 60; the warning takes 60 itself, because the slip's hour always lands a
  moment past the last tick in play (the critic's case), while a test that sets the clock by hand lands on it exactly. A watch already kept, lapsed or turned in is passed over. A wait that ends before 22:00, or one that carries the clock
  up to an hour into the night (an hour's wait from 21:30), does not warn, since the tick does not lapse it.
- The rest slip (`restSlipDraw`, `60-shop.js`), Wait and Sleep alike, adds a last row in the slip's red while that is so:
  ☾ *The night at the hen-house will be lost.* (the fold by the giver's trade: the fold, the hives, the hen-house), with *an hour or more*
  at its right. It is redrawn on every change of the hours, so stepping the slip down to a short wait before the night clears it. The
  wording is the errand's own lapse line (*The night at the {fold} is lost.*) turned to the future; nothing of the quest writer's is changed.
  The slip still waits or sleeps as asked; it warns, it does not refuse.
