are the next one's.

**The fall** (`bridgeFell`, `bridgeDown`, `87-world-quests.js`). A bridge's site (`buildBridges`' `bridge_<cell>_<n>`) falls in a week when
`seededRng('bridge', id+':'+week)` comes under 1 in 30, and is down that week and the next. Nothing is stored for a fall: the clock and the
id say which bridges are down, so a load, or a friend in the host's world, reads the same. The week is the game day ÷ 7.

**The crossing, as first built and as landed.** As first written this session put a solid across the middle of the deck (`bridgeGapSolid`, read
by `solidAt`) and set you off the side if you stood there when it fell, since nothing drew the fall. Before it landed, the look builder's
S2281 landed the broken span: `bridgeGeo(…, down)` draws the middle pier and its two arches fallen, `bridgeFit` splits the rails either
side of the gap, and `BRIDGE_GAPS` (read by `worldH`) takes the road's causeway out of the gap, so you fall from the break into the river and
swim. It reads the state in one function, `bridgeIsDown(id)` (`82-world-structures.js`), left for this session to point at the rule. So the
solid, its line in `solidAt` and the push off the side are gone, and `bridgeIsDown` now asks `bridgeDownId(id)`. `tickBridges` reads the set
of loaded bridges down once a game minute and calls the look's `bridgeRedraw(id)` on each that has fallen or stood again since, so a fall,
a mend coming good and the end of the two weeks all redraw the bridge where it stands. A record's `down` (true or false), which the look's
`bridges` suite sets by hand, is kept as a hand's override that wins over the roll. Coming within 20 m on the road still says once a fall:
*{bridge} is down: its middle span lies in the river. The lord at {town} may see it mended.*

**The news** (`riderNews`). A bridge down within the rider's two cells joins his list, dated from the week it fell: *{bridge} is down. The
middle span is in the river.*, to the journal and pinned on the map at the bridge, as his other news is.

**The mend** (`bridgeMendTopics` in `lordTopics`, `mendBridge`). The nearer lord is the nearest village, town, port or city with a lord in
the bridge's cell and its eight neighbours, read from the cells' data (not what has loaded). He has three topics while one of his bridges is
down and not yet paid for: *The bridge is down.* (when it fell, the masons' price, the planks); *I'll pay the masons.* (250 gold + 2 a metre
of the bridge, rounded; *{n} gold, and you have {m}.* when short); and, at Joinery 25, *I'll mend it myself.* (20 planks from the bag). Either
writes `worldState.bridges[id] = {paid, up, how}` (the world's: in the S242 list, not `SS_CHAR_WS`), and the bridge stands again on the day
`up`, two days after. A fall in a later week is a new fall. The mend gives no favour and no Joinery practice: the page names neither.
