1.5 s for you, 6 s for her hand if he reaches it unhurt."* Session 1815 built your cut and left hers. A black sail wants the fight

The backlog has carried both since then: *naval combat B's systems sessions are done but for a hand at her rig or a line (the

**What the game does today** (read in `85-world-sea.js` and `86-world-crime.js`): a merchantman spawns with `crew: []`, and only a
black sail is given living hands (`crewUp`, three Pirates). `rigHands(o)` reads those bodies, so a merchantman's fire always runs its

1827). Boarded, *"Her crew keep their heads down"*, but nobody stands there.

Building the clause means deciding whether her crew have bodies. That is a new kind of person at sea (neither foe nor townsman)
and a new rule about what striking one costs, so it is asked first, not built.

### Decision
**A merchantman's hands: who cuts her lines and douses her rig? (#1064 B's last clause) — DECISION #1225**

The naval page gives her hand 6 s at a line *"if he reaches it unhurt"*, and Michael's A on #1143 gives a living hand 3 s to douse
her rig. A black sail's hands are bodies, the Pirates on her deck. A merchantman has none, so neither rule can reach her.

- **A (recommended).** Two hands with bodies on every merchantman, unarmed, never hostile (her crew, as a black sail's). Hooked,
  one goes to a cleat and cuts each line in 6 s. Any hit on him starts his 6 s again, as the douse's 3 s are started again. With
  both lines cut she runs. Her rig is doused at 3 s by the same rule as a black sail's. Striking a hand is piracy, as any hit on
  her already is. Killing him is booked once as the same piracy, not as a murder. Boarded, they keep their heads down and stop
  cutting. One systems session, plus a sailor's dress from the bodies builder (a stand-in Pirate body without arms until then).
- **B.** No bodies. Her crew cut one line 6 s after the lashing and the other 6 s later, unless you have crossed to her deck
  first. They douse her rig at 3 s unless you are aboard. Cheaper, but the page's *"unhurt"* has nothing to read: the bow and the
  ballista cannot stop them.
- **C.** Leave it out. A merchantman never cuts free and never douses. The page's clause then applies to nobody, since a black
  sail wants the fight.

**Recommendation: A.** It is the page's rule as written. It keeps Bow and the ballista's heavy arrow (#1120, #1136) useful against a
ship that is running away. Her crew already witness piracy, so giving them bodies adds no new crime.
