After Session 1235's fortress on a pass, OG-034's board still had three of the Mark's settlements: the cliff terraces and rope walkways,
the fjord port, and the place under the aurora. This session builds the fjord port: `markFjordPort` in `docs/prototypes/levelup/places.js`,
at `places.html?place=fjordport&view=fjord|wharf|ship|halls|naust|racks|far`. Cited images: OG-034, the *Fjordhold* panel (a port at a fjord's
head, timber halls on the water, ships alongside) and its harbour strip, and OG-025's timber town by the water. OG-034's catalogue row flags
the board's names as proposals and its *Highhold / Council of Houses* as against the canon. Neither is used: the port has no name and no
government. The look is the canon's for the Mark: timber halls, dark shingle, and "nothing decorative that does not also keep out cold". The
models were the wharf at Bryggen and the boathouses (naust) of the Norwegian west coast. Nothing here needed a decision: it is a place
Michael's board already shows, built in the kit's Markish dress.

- **The land:** a shelf of made ground 1.6 m over the water at the wharf, rising 1 in 30 to the fjord wall's foot 24 m back. The wall is a
  wooded talus at 1 in 1.1 for 10 m, then cliff at 1 in 0.45 and steeper, with buttresses and gullies, rounding over near 150 m. It comes
  down to the water past x ±42, and the fjord widens to ±160 m by 120 m out. The far wall stands 200 m out, where the fjord turns. There is
  shingle east of the wharf and moss on the talus. The rock and the moss are told apart by the land's own slope, not each triangle's, so
  they lie in bands.
- **The wharf:** a timber deck 1.6 m over the water, 40 m long and 6 m out from the shore. Ten log cribs, each 2.6 m square, carry it on
  stringers. Each crib is built of logs crossed in courses from 0.25 m under the bed to the stringers, with stone fill at its top. The deck
  boards run along the face in staggered lengths. Along the edge are a face log, fender logs every 3 m and oak bollards every 6 m.
- **The warehouses:** three of the kit's halls (`markHall`), shingled and carved, gable-on to the wharf. The foot of each one's steps is
  1.4 m behind the wharf. A hoist beam on braces runs out from each front gable, with a block, a rope and a hook 3.3 m up.
- **The boathouses:** two nausts on the beach, their mouths to the water, 3.2 m wide inside and 8.8 m long. The side walls are dry stone,
  0.7 m thick and 1.9 m high, on a straight top from the back down to the mouth, under wall plates and a turf roof at 30°. A planked gable
  stands over the mouth on a lintel log, with a back wall and a planked back gable. In each, a slip log runs from inside the back wall down
  the beach to 0.4 m under the water. On it lies a færing, a four-oared double-ender (6.2 by 1.5 m) with three thwarts and two oars, bow to
  the water.
- **The stockfish racks:** two A-framed racks 14 m long at the wharf's west end, with a ridge pole and two bars a side. The fish hang in
  pairs over the bars by their tails, split and dried flat (the Mark's fishing, from OG-034's *Glassfjord* panel; the fish are cut shapes).
- **The ship:** the kit's Markish cutter (`ships.js` `markCutter`, striped), alongside the wharf's middle and 0.46 m off the fenders. A
  gangplank runs from the deck to her cap rail amidships. Her sail had stood set at the wharf, so `markCutter` takes a new `o.furled`
  (default false, so nothing else changes): the gaff is lowered onto the boom, the main rolled along it in its stripes under five gaskets,
  and the topsail and the headsails stowed.
