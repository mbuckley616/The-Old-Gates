## v80 — Session 2446 — the Forgotten Aqueducts: each fallen block one stone, and the water coloured by its depth (prototype; OG-104)
The run's second session, on `auto/look-2445`. It takes two of the faults Session 2445 left under *Needs eyes*.

**What was wrong.** (1) The vault's fallen blocks on the scree and in the water, and the blocks heaped at the chokes, each showed four
courses of small bricks on every face (`aqueducts-scree.jpg` at S2445). Their boxes keep their own coordinates, 0 to 1 a face, and the kit's
ashlar is two blocks by four courses a tile, so each face drew the whole tile. A fallen voussoir 2 to 5 m long should read as one stone.
(2) The water was one dark colour, and its blue-green came from a point light over it. That light made a hard glint in the hole, and the
shallows over the scree's toe looked as deep as the nave's 6 m.

**What changed** (`ruins.js` `forgottenAqueducts`, `places-aqueducts-view.js`; the prototype only).
- The fallen blocks' material takes the ashlar at a half by a quarter a face, offset a quarter across, so each face is one block of the
  tile with its joints round its edges. The offset is needed because the course a box's face lands on is set half a block over. The
  kit's own fallen stones and the standing arches are unchanged.
- The water is a grid of 70 by 30 (4,200 triangles, from 2) coloured by the depth under each point (`ground`): pale green where it is under
  1.5 m (the scree's toe), dark blue-green over the nave's 6 m. Its roughness is now 0.28, up from 0.2. The light over it is halved twice
  (700 to 350; at dusk 200 to 120), so the colour is the water's own.
- 65,815 triangles in 18 draws (S2445: 61,617).
