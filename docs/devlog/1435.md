## v80 — Session 1435 — No arm through your own cloak (Michael's #274, new look)
The same run as Session 1434. Session 1434 held the townsfolk's arms under a cloak in the walk and left your own body out, because you
swing a blade, raise a guard and draw a bow as you walk, and the hold would have taken those arms. This session takes up that slice.

**What was wrong.** On the new look your cloak is the builder's, skinned whole to the pelvis like the townsfolk's. Walking with a sword
and a wool cloak, 5,371 of 23,627 hand and forearm points under it came out through it, by up to 0.32 m. Standing was no better. The
blade's rest pose (up and forward) put the right fist 18 cm out through the cloak's side: 990 of 2,493 points. Holding the arms alone
still left 480 of 37,455 upper-arm points out by up to 4.7 cm. Taking the torso's turn off in a probe showed the cause. The walk leans
the spine 0.2 rad forward, and the shoulders ride the spine while the cloak rides the pelvis.

**What changed.**
- **`tpPose` (`54-thirdperson.js`) decides, per arm, whether the hold may take it** (`TP.hbHold.L`/`.R`). An arm is held while its pose
  is still the gait's own swing, or, standing, the arm as it hangs or the blade's rest pose. Anything that writes over the arm takes it
  back: a swing, a block, a cast, the bow, a torch, a shield, the two-handed grip, the unarmed guard, sneaking, a jump, the swim, the
  climb, the mantle and death. The hold eases in and out at the arm's own rate (`ka`), so a blow begun mid-stride comes up out of the hold.
- **Under a cloak you walk upright.** While the torso is the gait's, its forward lean is eased off (`TP.hbHold.S`, `.lean`). With no
  cloak, the lean and the arms are as before.
- **`hbPose` and `hbHoldF` (`32-people.js`)** take each held joint's weight from `rig.tpHold` by its side when the rig has one (your body
  only). The townsfolk's rule is unchanged. `tpEnsure` now keeps `hbCloakArms`' set on your body and points `rig.tpHold` at `TP.hbHold`,
  where Session 1434 set it to null.
- What the hands hold follows the builder's hand (`tpHBHold`). Walking or standing in a cloak, the sword hangs at your side in the held
  fist, its angle to the forearm the driver's.
