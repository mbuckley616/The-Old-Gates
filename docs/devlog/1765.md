## v80 — Session 1765 — the sea's wrecks chosen by the land keyed by place (backlog I, s1149)
Backlog section I's open systems item. Session 1752 found that `seaWrecks` (the whole sea's wreck list, `86-world-crime.js`, which the
wreck's maiden and the navigator's pointer read) chose each chunk's wreck spot by `worldH`, whose hills take their amplitude from the
loaded cells' regions: two boots with 22 and 18 regions loaded chose a different spot in 18 chunks, beds up to 2.91 m apart, 753 wrecks
against 756. The look builder's half landed in Session 1758: `placeH(x,z)`, the bare land with the 3×3 cells round the point's own cell
standing in for what is loaded. This is the other half: the choice reads it.

**What changed.** One function, `wreckSpot(cx,cz)` (`85-world-sea.js`, beside `spawnWreck`): the chunk's hash (1 in 16), then the first
of the nine hashed spots whose bed by `placeH` is 2.5 to 8 m under the sea, `{x,z,bed}` or null. `spawnWreck` lays its wreck there and
`seaWrecks` walks it, so the list and the wrecks laid can no longer part (before, the two loops were copies of each other). The hull and its
chest still stand on `worldH`, the drawn sea floor, which is what the chunk is built on. The rules are unchanged: the same hash, the same
nine spots, the same depth band, the maiden in each island's deepest bed.

`yardpanel`'s maiden check compared the deepest wreck's bed with `worldH`; it now compares with `placeH`, the bed's own measure (the same
equality, not loosened), and its logged drift reads `placeH` too.
