FREEZE #470 (F3, the land) is open, so this run worked in the prototypes only. Michael's B on #464 (the row of seven along the
churchyard's long side) is one number in `townPlots` (`83-world-generator.js`): the yard beside the church goes from 2 m to 4.5 m.
That is game code, so it waits for the freeze to lift. It is the look builder's first session after the freeze.

The work taken instead is from L9's *Trades at work* (OG-114). The sheet draws eight workplaces. The kit has the farmstead, the
forge (S929), the guild hall (S964), the warehouse, the mill (S949) and the shipyard. It had no market hall. A Gatelands town holds its
market today as trestle stalls round the well on the square (`plan.stalls`, `stallGeo`), with no building. OG-114's Gatelands panel
draws its market round a stall by a stone bridge, and OG-070's vocabulary (limewash, dressed stone, round arches, slate) is the
Irish market house's own. Whether a town gets one, and which, is a design call. So it is built three ways in `pieces.js`,
`gatelandsMarket({ kind })`, beside the church, the coaching inn and the keep:
- **`house`: the market house.** Two storeys, 15 by 8.6 m, limewashed with dressed quoins, a string course and a cornice, under a
  hipped slate roof (32°). The ground floor is an open arcade of round arches in dressed voussoirs, 2.0 by 3.0 m, five on each long
  side and three (1.7 by 2.8 m) on the west end. It is flagged a step up from the square, under the beams of the floor above (3.0 m
  clear). The room upstairs is 3.0 m floor to floor and lit by nine sashes. It is reached by a solid stone stair up the outside of
  the east end: 18 risers of 0.2 m on 0.25 m treads, 1.1 m wide, with a limewashed parapet, to a landing at its door. On the ridge
  is a wooden cupola with the market bell, a slate pyramid and a vane. A name panel over the middle arch reads MARKET HOUSE.
  10,874 triangles in 21 draws.
- **`shelter`: a market shelter.** One storey, 10 by 6.6 m, open on all four sides through four arches a side and two at each end,
  under a hipped slate roof. 5,312 triangles in 9 draws.
- **`stalls`: no building**, the market as the game holds it now: seven trestle stalls. 1,244 triangles in 4 draws.

In all three, the town's beam and scales hang in the middle: an iron beam with two pans on chains at 1.15 m, from the house's
girder, the shelter's tie, or a timber tripod on the open square. The stalls are boards on trestles with crates and a sack.
`pieces-gatelands.html?view=market&market=house|shelter|stalls|all&cam=square|arcade|stair|high` stands it on a flagged square 34 by
26 m, with three-bay townhouses round three sides.

### Decision
**A town's market on the new look: a market house, a market shelter, or the stalls as today?** (DECISION #475)
OG-114 draws eight workplaces for the trades, among them a market hall. The new kit has all the others but that one. A Gatelands
town holds its market today as trestle stalls on the square, with no building.
- **A. A market house** *(recommended)*. Two storeys, 15 by 8.6 m: an open arcade of round arches on the ground floor for the market,
  a room above reached by a stone stair outside the east end, and a cupola with the market bell. It is the Irish town's own
  building, in OG-070's limewash, dressed stone and slate. It needs a plot on the square like the church's (#433 B), about 17 by 11 m
  with its stair and a margin, and gives a town a second landmark on its square. The room above is a room the quest writer and the
  systems builder can use (a court, a hiring hall, the toll-taker), or not.
- **B. A market shelter.** One storey, 10 by 6.6 m, open on all sides. Smaller, half the triangles, and it needs a plot about
  12 by 9 m. It keeps the stalls dry but is no landmark: from across the square it sits under the townhouses' eaves.
- **C. As today.** Stalls on the open square, the beam on a tripod. No plot, no building.

A and B would apply to towns, ports and cities (not villages), placed on the square after the church and the keep. Pictures:
