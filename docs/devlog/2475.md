## v80 — Session 2475 — ground that pushes back, 2: ice that does not let go
The second session of the systems builder's run of 21 Nov, on `auto/systems-2474`. Michael's A on #1800, the page's session 2
(`docs/design/ground-that-pushes-back.md`): ice's momentum and the roll on it. The page's row: *not slow, but it does not let go: you gain
and lose speed at 6 m/s², so a stop slides 1.2 m (1.8 from a sprint)*, the roll ×1.3 (3.4 m light).

**What changed** (`js/50-travel.js`, `js/90-main.js`):
- **Where ice is** (`iceUnderfoot()`): only the ice the game already has, as the page asks. You must be on the ground and:
  - on a live Sioc floe or a Tonn-and-Sioc bridge (`laidLive`, `laidInside`), within 0.2 m of its top: not on a pillar, not over it;
  - in a gate, on a frozen pool (`dIceLift`), not its water;
  - in an ice cave, on its lake: inside the kit's `lake` rectangle and within 0.15 m of its surface (the bridge over it is not ice).
- **The step** (`iceTick`). The loop's step is handed to it before the move. Off ice it only keeps the velocity you are moving at. On ice
  that velocity eases toward the one you ask for at `ICE.acc`, 6 m/s², by the loop's dt (the dropped-frame rule), and the frame's step is
  that velocity's. So you carry onto the ice the speed you came with, build to the run over 0.64 s, and slide when you let go or turn. A
  roll or an air dash on it moves as before and hands on its own speed, so you come out of a roll sliding. `moving` in the loop is now a
  `let`: a slide with no key held is a step (the footsteps, the bob and the hearing count it as one).
- **The roll** on ice goes ×1.3 as far (`footRollMult`): 3.38 m light, 2.34 heavy.
- **The chip.** On ice the buff bar shows *Ice* (the page's word), its title *it does not let go: you gain and lose speed slowly, and a roll
  goes 130% as far.* It shows in a gate and in a cave too, where the snow and mud chip never does.
- **Not built, and why:** *a stagger knocks you 1.5× as far.* Your stagger today (`playerPostureHit`) stops you where you stand; nothing
  knocks you back, so there is nothing to scale. It waits for a knock-back, if one is ever built. Foes are unchanged, as the page says:
  they still treat a frozen pool as a wall (S1682), and nothing of theirs walks a floe.
- Hobnails (the grip at 20 m/s², the noise) are session 3; `iceAcc()` is the one place they will change.
