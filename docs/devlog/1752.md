## v80 — Session 1752 — the cairn's passage in rough stone, not polished marble
The run's third item, from the critic's report of 2 Nov (build s1144, `docs/critic.md`): the passage cairn that Session 1732 put at the
Gatelands' stone rings is "lined with smooth slabs veined like polished marble, where the prototype and OG-121 have rough orthostats"
(`docs/critic/2026-11-02-cairn-passage-11h.jpg`, at c5_9_s1). Section I held nothing else for the look builder, and F8's under-places still
wait on #1026.

**Why it read as marble.** The cairn's slabs and flags (`cairnMats` in `js/38-kit.js`) are the kit's `rock` surface, the same as places.js's.
`rock` is painted from a broad noise, strata and a crack term (`pow(1 − |fbm − .5|·2, 14)`), which draws thin dark lines wherever the noise
crosses its middle. On a boulder the roughened shape breaks them up; on the passage's orthostats, flat faces 2.6 m high under even daylight at
a UV scale of 0.5 (one tile in 2 m), they run as long, smooth, branching veins: marble. The flags showed the same.

**What changed.** A new surface beside `rock`, `orth` (512 px, normal strength 5): a broad weathering noise, a grain at about 7 cm and fine
pits at about 3 cm, with pale grey-green lichen in small spots, and no strata or crack lines. The cairn's `slab` material takes it
(colour 0x9a978c, fully rough) and so do the passage's and chamber's flags (`floorC`, 0x6a645a). The boulders, the kerb, the facade and the
mound keep theirs. Nothing else in the kit reads `orth`; the abbey's and the tower house's own `rock` slabs are untouched. Same geometry, so
the same 24,228 triangles in 7 draws.

### Verified (headless Chromium)

  flags, with no veins (`docs/prototypes/cairn-slabs-after.jpg`, against the critic's picture above). A first try, at half the grain's
  contrast and wider lichen, read as soft green smears; the picture is the second.
- `cairn` 7/7: 24,228 triangles in 7 draws, 8.25 m high, the passage and chamber open, the slabs solid, the ground 0.24 m under the flags,
  the far copy 376.
