## v80 — Session 1195 — the townsfolk wear the builder's bodies (card F4, part one)
Card F4 asks for the people and creatures of the new look to come from the builder's genome (`character-builder.md`), townsfolk
first. Until now the builder lived only in the prototype pages: `docs/prototypes/levelup/people.js` is an ES module that fetches
MakeHuman's pack (`mh/pack.json` and `pack.bin`, made by `mh_pack.py` and not committed). The game is classic scripts that open
by double-clicking, so neither the module nor the fetch can come in as they are. This session brings the builder into the game
on the new look and dresses Dunmore's people in it; the foes and the wolves are the card's later sessions.

**The builder in the game.** `scripts/people_bundle.py` (new, after `three_bundle.py`) writes two files, never edited by hand:
- `vendor/people.builder.js`: `people.js` inside one function, its exports returned as `window.HB`. Its one fetch is replaced by a
  read of the pack below, so `loadPack` is synchronous; the script refuses a `people.js` that imports anything but three or
  awaits anything but the pack. It is 448 KB. `--check` says whether it is stale against `people.js`.
- `vendor/mh-pack.js`: the pack as `var MH_PACK={ix,b64}`, MakeHuman's CC0 body, its 140 targets and 163-bone skeleton. The
  binary is 10.0 MB (character-builder.md guessed 7), 6.6 MB gzipped; as base64 the file is 13.4 MB. That is inside v2-scope's
  40 MB download budget, and only the new look loads it: the renderer's chooser in `index.html` writes the two tags after
  three.js 0.170's. Decoding it and building the pack's tables takes 110 ms at the first body.

**Townsfolk from the genome** (`32-people.js`, a section at its foot). `personGenome` still decides who someone is (name, role,
sex, age, people, stature), and `buildNPCMesh` still keys them by place and name. On the new look it now also rolls the
builder's genome from that key (`<site>:<name>`, e.g. `dunmore#2:Sinéad`) with the person's people, sex, child and elder flags
(`hbGenome`, `HB.rollGenome`): a seeded roll inside the people's ranges, never `Math.random`, so the same villager is the same
on every load and for everyone. A guard gets the builder's mail, helm and spear over it. Only the body changes: names, roles,
dialogue, schedules and every outcome are as they were.

**Poses on the builder's skeleton.** The builder has no gait library for people yet (B4, the clip library, is not built; its
creatures have gaits, its people stand). Rather than rewrite every pose for a 163-bone rig, the old 17-bone rig stays as the
driver: `tickPeople` poses it exactly as before (idle, walk, run, wave, sleep, the ragdoll, the stride by distance covered), and
`hbPose` copies its sixteen joints onto the builder's bones each frame. Each joint's rotation is taken relative to its rest in the
person's own frame and applied to the builder's bone relative to the builder's standing pose (arms down), so the driver's zero
pose is the builder's stance; the bones between (the spine's four, the clavicles, the twist bones, the fingers) keep their
stance. The hips' bob and sway are carried over at the ratio of the two hip heights. Skinning is kept local to the body (the
bones' matrices less the body's root, in float64), as CLAUDE.md's rule for x, z ≈ 13,000–25,000 has it. The body stands at the
height the game gives the person (SCALE's statures by people, Michael's A on #276).

**Three levels for crowds.** Under 16 m the builder's body whole; to 42 m the same body without its cards (hair, brows, beard,
moustache) and eyes, the scalp shell standing in; beyond, the driver's own distant copy, now tinted from the genome's skin, hair
and clothes so nothing changes colour at the line. A metre of hysteresis each way. The shadow is always the driver's distant
copy, swapped in inside the shadow pass as the townsfolk's shadow-LOD already does, so the builder's meshes cast none.

**Building them.** `makePerson` is heavy: 0.4 to 1.1 s a body on this container's CPU (in the game, a CI-class machine), 50,000
to 270,000 triangles whole (a long loose style's hair cards are 220,000 of the largest), 14 to 27 meshes. So a body is built only
when its person first comes within 46 m of the eye, nearest first, one at a time at most every 250 ms, and never while the
world still has cells to load (the first run built one a frame and starved Hearthwick's load in two suites). Until it is built
the distant copy stands in.

### Needs eyes
- **The budget is nearly spent.** Each body whole is 14 to 27 draw calls; six near the square took Dunmore from 905 to 984. A
  market with a dozen people within 16 m will pass 1,000. The middle level should be one merged mesh, not the whole body less its
  cards, and the near radius may want to come in; that is this card's next session, measured, before the foes.
- **The hitch.** A body built in the street is a stall of a few hundred milliseconds on a laptop, one per quarter second as you
  walk into a town. Two remedies for the next session: `people.js`'s hair-and-cloth surface lookups (`surfGrid`'s string keys,
  `heightOver`, 30% of the build in a profile) can be made several times faster without changing a body, and the bodies of a
  town can be built while its cells load rather than after.
- **The feet.** The builder's legs are not the driver's length; the copied angles keep the stride but a foot can sit about 15 cm
  off the driver's planted ankle mid-stride (0.16 m in the suite), so a standing foot may slide a little. Planted-foot IK on the builder's legs is the
  fix, with the gait library (B4).
- **Faces and colour at conversation distance.** The builder's colours were tuned in the prototype's colour-managed sRGB output;
  the new look keeps r128's colour handling (`05-compat.js`). Skin, cloth and hair read right in the pictures, but a close look
  at a face in the game's light is owed.
- Owed by the card: sitting at work and in beds on the builder's body (the driver's poses reach it; not yet looked at), the foes
  (bandits, the lair's people) and the wolves as creature plans.
