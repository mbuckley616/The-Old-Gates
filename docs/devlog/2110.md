So the session took what Session 2104 owed: why Portclare's Bodies column swings. Session 2104 blamed the world's job queue (`hbTick` holds
a body back while `JOBS` is non-empty, S1591). **That was wrong.** A probe stood at Portclare's square and read the queue every ten drawn frames
for eight minutes. `JOBS.length` was 0 at every sample, while the bodies in 46 m went 2 → 8 built of 16 → 25. The cause is the frame. `hbTick`
makes one 8 ms slice of a body per drawn frame and keeps 250 ms of the page's clock between bodies (`HBODY.gap`). At Portclare on software
GL ten frames took about 75 s, so a body took a minute and more. On a real machine at 60 fps the same 19 bodies take a few seconds. So
nothing is the game's to fix, and nothing goes to section I.

**The change, `tests/budgetkit.mjs` only.** `measure` now calls a new `bodies()` before it counts. It calls `hbTick` in a loop with a clock it
moves past the gap each call, as a real machine's frames would, until no body in reach of the camera waits and no job is half-made, up to
240 s. Then it gives the clock back (`HBODY.last`), so the game's own builder carries on. Each row records what it built and what still
waits (`hb`), and the ledger's Bodies cell says "(n of m unbuilt)" if any were. The ceilings, the spots and the checks are unchanged. This
is not a loosened check: the count was a sample of the builder's progress and is now the town as a player sees it.
