## v80 — Session 1775 — the creature kit's hide and fur share their programs between kinds: the prewarm 31 s to 11 s
The second item of the run, asked of this lane by the systems builder (Session 1769) and carried by the producer: since the islands'
33 animals came into the open country, the world's first entry prewarms 75 kinds and compiled 224 shader programs doing it, 77 more
than before, about 14 s on software GL for every suite that enters the world. "Can the kit share those programs between kinds?"

It can, because nothing in the shaders differed but numbers. The kit's two materials (`creatures.js`) keyed their programs by those
numbers: the hide by its glow colour (`'hide' + JSON.stringify(o.glow)`), though the colour was already a uniform and the shader text
the same for every colour; and each fur shell by its layer and the whole fur plan (`'cfur' + layer + '/' + layers + JSON.stringify(o)`),
because the layer's height and the strand noise's three scales were written into the fragment shader as literals. So a wolf's nine
shells were nine programs, a bear's another nine, and every kind with its own fur numbers a set of its own. Now the layer's height
(`uFL`) and the strands' scale (`uFS`) are uniforms set per material, and the keys are `'hide'` (with `c` for a clear hide and `f` for
a face that thins, which do change the text) and `'cfur'`. three.js still runs each material's `onBeforeCompile` with its own uniforms
and reuses the compiled program by key, so every shell keeps its own numbers. The fish's school (`hcschool`) and the people's
materials are untouched. The game takes it through the bundle (`scripts/creatures_bundle.py`; `--check` matches).
