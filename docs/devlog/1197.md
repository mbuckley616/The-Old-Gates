## v80 — Session 1197 — the foes wear the builder's bodies (card F4, part three)
Sessions 1195 and 1196 dressed the townsfolk of the new look in the builder's bodies; the card's fifth goal is the region's foes
for the vertical slice's tier B, bandits and the lair's people. This session gives every human foe the same treatment. (Written
on `auto/fable-f4-people-2`, cut from PR #497's head while #497's CI ran.)

**Which foes.** The nine kinds of `FOE_DRESS` that are people: Bandit, Bandit Archer, Highwayman, Deserter, Cultist, Rogue Mage,
Pirate, Bandit Captain and the dungeon's Shieldbearer (`hbFoeOk`). The dead, the skeletons, the goblins and kobolds, the trolls
and the ogre, the hag, the gargoyle, the golem and the wraiths keep their own bodies: none of them is a person the builder makes.
A town guard drawn on you (a Bandit on a townsman's genome) is the same person you saw in the street, since its genome already
carries his key.

**Seeded by its id.** A foe is built before it has an id (`keyFoe` gives it one after `buildZoneEnemy` returns), and the builder's
body is built later anyway, when the foe first comes within 46 m. So the genome is rolled then, from `foe:<id>`: the site, job or
dungeon and the index, the co-op rule's place and id, never a position or `Math.random` (`hbFoeGenome`). The same foe is the same
body on every load and for everyone.

**Dressed by kind** (`HB_FOE_DRESS`), over the builder's own roll for the person's people, sex and build, in the kind's
`FOE_DRESS` colour: the bandits and archers in a hooded shoulder cape, the highwayman in a long coat, the deserter in mail and a
kettle helm, the cultists and rogue mages in a robe under a hooded cloak, the pirate in a head-wrap, the captain in mail, helm and
cape, the shieldbearer in mail and helm under a tabard. The builder's own spear, staff, crossbow, basket and shield are taken off
every one: the weapon and the shield are still the game's kit.

**The kit in the fist.** The kit's weapon hangs on the driver's gear bone and its shield on the driver's forearm, and those bones
hang inside the driver's mesh, which is hidden while the builder's body shows: the first try drew a line of empty-handed bandits.
So while the builder's body shows, what the driver holds is carried in the builder's group, following its bone's turn each frame,
with a weapon's grip moved to the middle of the builder's fingers, which are closed on it the way `people.js` closes a hand on a
shield's grip (`hbCloseFist`, `hbHold`). When the driver's own body shows again, or the builder's is dropped, they go back to
their bones as they were (`hbGive`).

**The glows and the corpse.** The tell's glow, the guard-break's flash and the parry's stagger all light `enemyBodyMesh(e)`, which
is still the driver's mesh. `hbGlow` copies its emissive onto every material of the builder's body (the parry's yellow clone reads
as a strong gold), and each foe's middle level gets its own copy of the shared baked material for it. At the kill, `killE` and
`killZoneEnemy` darken a corpse by cloning every material on it, and `Material.copy` leaves `onBeforeCompile` behind, which is
where the builder's garments carry their weave and their cut hems: a cloned shell would have shown the whole body's shell. The
builder's materials now clone with their shaders (`hbClone`).

**Correction to Session 1195: the hips.** `hbPose` copied the driver's hip travel into the builder's root bone at the ratio of the
two hip heights, measured in two different frames: the factor came out 0.23, so the walk's bob was a quarter of what it should
be and a killed foe's builder body stayed standing over its fallen driver (the head 1.0 m up, 1.03 m from the driver's). The
travel is now taken in the person's own frame, across at full measure and up at the ratio of the hip heights, then put into the
builder's frame. The walking foot keeps closer to the driver's ankle for it: 0.121 m against 0.145 to 0.161 before.
