## v80 — Session 2714 — the shore by island and a deeper sea: the Gatelands' coast stops reading tropical
*Written as 2713 and renumbered 2714: the systems builder's 2713 was pushed first.*
The look builder's run of 26 Nov (late), on `auto/look-2714` from origin/main. No `FREEZE:` issue was open; the last look branch (2709)
was three hours old and landed. Section I names nothing open for the look builder.

**The gap.** The Mark upland has had four sessions (2696, 2702, 2705, 2709); what is left there is the relief (F3, #2063) and F3's light.
The street waits on the town dressing (#2058), the storm's sky on 86 and 89, the valley on relief. In the critic's *The look against the
art* (`docs/look/critic-1126/`) the coast is "far … bluer water and a whiter beach; no cliffs". The cliffs are the relief's (#2063 is
still open), but the rest of the frame is this lane's: half of it is a white beach and a pale Caribbean cyan sea, on the Gatelands,
whose palette in P10b (and `LIGHT_PAL`'s note) is moss and soft blue with green-grey water. P10b's *Coastal Cliffs* is dark blue water
under grey rock.

**Why it was so.** Two things, both in `js/80-world-terrain.js`:
- `groundColor` laid one sand, `0xb8ac88`, on every shore of every island. On the new look `landCol` keeps a pale ground's brightness
  (×0.82, not the country's ×0.5), so the beach rendered near white, and the shallows showed it through the water.
- The sea's material is a Lambert of `0x2f7fb2` with an emissive of `0x0a2a40`. Under the hour's sun (1.6 at noon on the legacy ×π
  scale) that clips to a pale cyan from any height, whatever the island; the island's tint (`LIGHT_PAL.sea`, 89) could only shade it.
  Darkening only the shore left the water as pale as before (first try, not kept).

**What changed** (`js/80-world-terrain.js`; the old look unchanged on both counts):
- `shoreColor(x,z,h)`: the shore's colour by the island of the point's own cell (`shoreIsleAt`, `nationKeyOf`, as `snowLineAt` reads
  its cell): the Gatelands `0x7f7764`, a grey-brown sand with shingle (`0x5a5650`) laid in patches by two noises (6 m and 1.8 m); the Mark
  dark slate `0x4f5052` with the same shingle; Aurenne the old pale sand unchanged. The strand within about a metre of the waterline is
  wet and up to 22% darker (half that on Aurenne's). The first cut blended by `regionWeights`, and a chunk laid after a far jump took
  the last place's island (the loaded-regions gotcha): keyed by the cell it cannot. The islands are apart, so no shore has a seam.
- The new look's sea: base `0x103c50`, emissive `0x03141c`. The island's tint now has a dark water to colour: the Gatelands a deep
  green-teal, Aurenne's harbour a deep blue-turquoise under the golden hour, the Mark darker still. The sheen, the sky's reflection
  at a low angle and the glint (`landWater`, 89) are unchanged and now carry the brightness.

**A correction to the shots** (`scripts/look_shots.mjs`). A view taken after another one jumps the camera and loads the cells with
`g.cells()`, which ticks the world between rounds; a chunk laid then took its ground colour from the regions loaded at that moment, the
last view's (the far-jump gotcha). On the coast after the harbour, 26 beach vertices near the camera were Aurenne's pale (probed in
`tests/tmp`, not kept); a coast shot alone had none. The script now recolours every loaded chunk (`recolourChunk`) once a view's jobs
have run, which is what an arrival through `WORLD.enter` gives. No view's camera, hour or anchor changed; both rows of a sheet get it,
so earlier sheets' coast after the harbour (and any view after a far jump) may differ slightly in the ground near the camera.
