## v80 — Session 1323 — a gate's fights stand in pockets, and a foe drawn off walks home (card F5, the sheet's session 3)
F5's build sheet (`docs/design/gate-interiors-build.md`, "Pockets and the leash", from Michael's B on #448: "the fights in pockets of 15
to 30 m") hands its session 3 to the systems builder: pockets, waking and the leash, and `gatepockets`. Sessions 1 and 2 (deep work,
1283–1302) laid the gates out as spaces but kept the old foe placement: any floor cell more than 5 cells from the door, 12 m apart, so a
nave's foes stood alone along its length and each woke on its own. Session 1298 also left a massive gate holding its 30 foes on each
floor (60), where the sheet's table gives the gate 30.

**Where the pockets are** (`gatePocketSites`, `js/52-dungeon-gen.js`). Each space on a floor holds pockets spread evenly along it, with
the stair hall left out: as many as keep them 25 to 42 m apart (33 m is the aim), or one alone in a short space. So two pockets never
wake as one, and both ends of every space are within 25 m of a pocket. A pocket's radius is 0.4 of its spacing, 7.5 to 15 m, and no
more than half the space's width. A nave's pocket is a bay on its middle line, a chasm's is on the bridge, and the ring chamber's two
stand either side of the dais. Each centre is snapped to the nearest floor cell of its own space. Nothing is rolled: the layout decides
it, so the same seed gives the same pockets.

**Who stands in them** (`spawnFloorEnemies`, `js/56-dungeon-build.js`). A gate keeps its size's count from today's table (5, 9, 14, 20,
30; enemies by place keeps its tables), counted across the gate. So a two-floor gate is split by floor, with floor one taking the larger
half. The massive gate goes from 60 to 30, as the sheet's table says. The two quest gates of #613 go from 28 to 14 and from 40 to 20.
Q5's eight kills on the Vault of the Tide's floor two still have its 10. Seven in ten foes, rounded, stand in pockets of 2 to 4,
at least 4.5 m apart. The rest stand outside every pocket, 12 m apart as before. A small gate has fewer foes than its pockets can take
at 2 each: a tiny gate's 5 fill 2 of its 4 or 5 pockets. The pockets that are filled are then spread evenly along the way (see Needs
eyes). A mimic that moves to its chest's corner leaves its pocket. Each foe carries its pocket (`e.pocket`, id `<floor>:<n>`), scaled
to metres with it by `dunScaleWhole`. Forts, lairs and the tutorial crypt keep the old placement.

**Waking and the leash** (the dungeon foe tick, `js/90-main.js`). A pocket's foe that turns alert, by sight or by a blow, wakes every
foe of its pocket that is not a statue, a mimic or the cloak's sleeper. That is the sheet's "a bay fights together". A ranged foe in a
pocket sees at 20 m (`GATE_ARCHER_SIGHT`), not 8. It also forgets you past 20 m: under the old 14 m rule it saw you at 15 m and forgot
you in the same tick. The sheet's archers stand on balconies, and the gates have none yet, so this applies to the ranged foes of every
pocket. A pocket's foe more than its radius + 10 m from the pocket's centre (`GATE_LEASH`) gives up. So does one that forgets you at
14 m. It turns, plans a path home by the cells (`bfs`), and walks there at 0.6 of its chase speed. It keeps its wounds and its posture,
and sees nothing until it is home, so running back and forth never resets a fight. A blow on the way wakes it again, but past the leash
it turns home again on the next tick. The chase still goes through `targetOf(e)` (the co-op rules). Foes outside the pockets behave as
before.

### Needs eyes

  It should feel fair, not slow.
