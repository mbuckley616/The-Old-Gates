## v80 — Session 1439 — The crown braid and the plait over the shoulder (Michael's A on #272; OG-025, OG-001)
Michael's A on #272 kept groomed strands and modelled locks, and asked that the builder keep refining the hairstyles that "aren't quite
landing". The decision named the next styles: from OG-025 and OG-001, crown braids and a plait over the shoulder. The builder had six
styles (short, loose, one braid, two braids, a tail, a bun), and every braid or tail hung down the back. The Markwomen of OG-025 wear
their hair plaited forward over one shoulder or wound round the head. This session adds those two styles to `people.js`.

**The plait** (`plait`). The hair is gathered low behind one ear, on a side the seed picks. The plait is the braid's three locks, tie and brush,
laid on a line that `drape` draws forward round the side of the neck toward the front of that shoulder (a new `pull` option, which
steers the line until it passes the shoulder's front). Then it falls under a lighter gravity down the breast and is pushed clear of the clothes
as the braids are. It lies over a fur mantle and comes out through a cloak's opening at the throat.

**The crown braid** (`crown`). This is one braid laid round the head as a closed ring. It runs over the top just behind the hairline and
low round the back over the occiput, and its height comes from the bearing (1.05 rad up at the front, −0.12 at the back). Each ring
point is the head's own surface in that direction, smoothed round and set out by the braid's half-thickness, then laid again 1 cm apart
and closed. Its three locks cross a whole number of times, so they meet where they began. It has no tie, because the ends are tucked.
Every strand is combed to the ring's foot (its line brought down onto the skin), so the strands lie flat under the braid and do not
stand up into it. Each strand goes to the foot at its own bearing round the head's axis, out from a whorl at the crown. Two simpler
rules failed on the way:
- Combed to the nearest ring point, the top of the head parted down its middle to the ring's two sides. That left 2–3 of 88 midline points bare
  (2.3–3.4%, against the check's 2%).
- Combed to the ring's middle, not its foot, the strands at the top stood up into the braid as a spray. Aimed at the foot, about 300
  strands could not get within 1 cm of it at their own layer's height and wandered for a metre, tripling the triangles. A crown strand now
  ends within its layer's height plus 8 mm of the foot and walks at most 30 cm.

The crown takes 3,000 strands, 15 mm wide, because each one is short (it only reaches the ring), so 1,700 left the scalp between the
hairline and the band thin. Under a hood, a helm or a wrap both styles are tucked in as every other style is.

**Who wears them.** The Markwomen's roll gains plait 3 and crown 2 (in 13), and the Gatelands women's plait 1.5 (in 11.5). This changes
which style some seeds draw: a townswoman who rolled braids may now wear a plait. Your own saved look is not rolled again, so it does
not change. `vendor/people.builder.js` is rebuilt from `people.js` (`--pack /nonexistent`, so the committed pack and its SHA stay as they are).
