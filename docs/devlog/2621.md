## v80 — Session 2621 — the Gatelands' windmills come into the world (#1983 A, the first of the works)
The look builder's run of 24 Nov, third session, on `auto/look-2619`. Michael's A on #1983: *"by what each serves, windmill and lighthouse
first"*, one kind a session. The question put the windmill on a rise 150–400 m out of one village in two, among its fields. Session 949
drew it (`places.js` `gatelandsMill`, OG-046's "farms and windmills"); no mill stood in the world.

The gate's rings (#1991 A) were looked at first and left for the next run. The prototype's hill is a dome 78 by 68 m and 27 m high. Its
passage runs 15 m into it, under reveals the land's heightfield cannot overhang. That makes it a stamp, a roof patch and the door spot
moved, which is more than this run had left. The lean-to workshop (#1966 A) wants its prototype first.

**The kit piece.** `PKIT.places.mill` (`js/38-kit.js`) is `gatelandsMill` as drawn:
- the limewashed tower, battered from 6 m at the foot to 4.6 m at the curb 9.6 m up, on its tarred plinth;
- two round-headed doors, front and back, under dressed stone, and four floors of windows turning up the tower;
- the thatched ogee cap with its finial, and the windshaft at 6° up;
- four common sails, 8.6 m stocks with their lattice and cloth;
- the tail pole down to its cart wheel, the worn millstone and the sacks of meal by the door, and a path of flags.

What differs:
- The ground is the game's, so the prototype's grid of turf is left out.
- The path is the kit's flags, since the kit has no cobbles.
- The sails are their own group about the shaft (`spin`), so they can turn: the four sails and their cloth are two draws.

The whole mill is 3,668 triangles in 13 draws. The lowest a sail's tip comes over the ground, at any turn, is 2.36 m.

**Where** (`millsAt`, `82-world-structures.js`):
- **Which villages.** One village in two in the Gatelands, by its own stream (`mill:<site>`), on the new look.
- **Where to look.** Up to 48 spots on the ring 150–400 m out, inside the village's own cell, read by place (`withPlace`).
- **What a spot must clear.** 40 m off every pad, 60 m off every door, 30 m off every road's line and 30 m off every lake's rim. The ground
  must be 3 m or more over the sea, and level within 1.2 m over its 7 m round.
- **Among the fields.** The spot must be open country: the land's field weight .6 or more and its wood .3 or less, read as `landMask`
  reads them. The first try had no such test, and the nearest mill stood in a dense autumn wood.
- **Which spot.** The highest of those that pass. Its front door turns toward its village, and its sails into the west wind, give or take
  14°.

On seed 1, 22 of 107 villages keep one. The other half of the roll finds no open rise on its ring, mostly in wooded country.

**Its ground** (`millStamps`). There are three stamps:
- a knoll level 7 m round the tower, easing into the land over 8 m;
- a clearing of 16 m, so no tree stands where the sails sweep;
- a pasture 45 m round (S2088's `graze`: one tree in twelve kept), so the mill stands in its open field and reads from the road.

With the clearing alone, the plains' own scatter crowded the tower to its sails. The mill is built in the cell's statics' step (`placeMills`).
Its tower is solid as two boxes, square and turned 45°, an octagon round its batter. `tickMills` (called from `tickStars`, one line in
`50-travel.js`) turns each mill in the scene about a turn every eight seconds.
