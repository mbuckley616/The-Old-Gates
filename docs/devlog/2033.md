## v80 — Session 2033 — the perched stone on its pavement by its road (card F8, OG-124; Michael's A on #1292)
The look builder's second session of the run of 9 Nov, on `auto/look-2032`. S2030 named the perched stone as the next place by a road. It is
the fifth of the Gatelands' kinds, and the last of them that does not need ground the world cannot give: the merchants' halt wants a stream,
the expedition a trench, the unfinished camp snow. Until this session a cell that drew it stood empty.

**The piece** (`PKIT.places.erratic`, `js/38-kit.js`). It is places.js's `gatelandsErratic` (Session 1031; OG-124's panel 6, "small mysteries
with no marker: … an odd rock"), brought in as S2025, S2029 and S2030 brought theirs. Left out: the page's hill, its green road and its five ash
trees; the world brings the turf, the road and the trees. The lace wall is 28 m long, not 64.

The pavement could not come in as drawn. In the prototype each clint's top is the old surface and the gryke between two is 0.9 m deep, so you
walk on the clints. In the world you walk on the terrain, and outdoors nothing lets you stand on a solid's top (only a laid spell does). The
world's ground is therefore the grykes' floor:
- the world's stamp levels the pavement's ground (`waysideStamps`, a pad 11.5 m round its middle);
- a dark gryke floor lies 1.5 cm over it, inside the clints' ragged edge;
- each clint stands 0.08 m proud of it, its edges 0.04 lower, cut by the prototype's two sets of joints at 43° with grykes 0.12 to 0.24 m wide.
The joints, the ragged edge into the turf and the hart's-tongue rising out of the slots read. The depth is the dark.

Kept as the prototype has them:
- the waisted pedestal, 0.45 to 0.6 m over the pavement, and the boulder of dark stone on its flat sole, overhanging it all round so the daylight shows under it;
- the smaller stranger and three cobbles;
- the lace wall of single slabs, with daylight through it, across the slope above the stone;
- seven hazels up out of the grykes to the east;
- the hawthorn bent by the west wind toward the road.
The path worn from the road to the pavement's edge is a strip a hand over the drawn ground, as the cellar's is. It faces the road at −z, as the
others do. 10 draws, from the prototype's 11 (the turf is the world's).

**In the world** (`js/82-world-structures.js`).

- The land check reads the pavement's middle and eight points on its rim (`WAYSIDE_PADS.erratic`) and allows the usual 1.2 m of rise.
