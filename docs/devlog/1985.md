**Why this item.** Session 1983 left it owed. A count of the Mark's first six places found 4 of 18 homes still on the old builder's
house, on lots of 5 to 7 by 4 to 5 m, and "fitting them wants a smaller Markish house … a prototype and a question of its own". Those six

stand as the old builder's house among them. In the port `c1_1_s1` that is 13 of 26.

**What is in the way.** A throwaway patch of the lot rule in `js/83-world-generator.js`, tried in the game and then restored (`W0`, `D0`
and the house's surround in `ext` read from a page global), showed that depth binds, not width. The hall needs 6.5 m of depth plus 1.95 m
for its footing and steps. With the 1.8 m stoop cut to 0.6 m alone, the count does not move (124). With a smaller minimum it does. The
patch kept the hall's 0.15 m surround at the back and sides as 0.10 and 0.12, so the counts below are close, not exact:
- the hall cut down to 5.0 m deep on its full steps: 149 of 198 (75%);
- the hall down to 5.0 m deep on a low footing, 0.6 m of steps: 168 (85%);
- a house down to 4.2 by 3.4 m on 0.6 m of steps (the town house's range): 176 (89%).

### Decision
DECISION #1300, *the Mark's tight lots: the hall cut down, the hall on a low footing, or a two-storey town house?*
- A: the hall cut down to 5.0 m deep on its full footing and steps, 75% of the ports' and city's homes.
- B: the hall on a low footing (two steps, 0.6 m), down to 5.0 m deep, 85%.
- **C (recommended)**: the Markish town house where the hall will not fit, 89%, and every lot in the picture.
- D: leave the old builder's houses there until F14.
Whichever is chosen, its rooms come after it. A and B can open on the longhouse room where they are deep enough. C would want a narrow
two-floor room, built from the kit in a later session.
