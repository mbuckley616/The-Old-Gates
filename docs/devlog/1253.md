## v80 — Session 1253 — F10, part three: every animation on the builder's skeleton, checked; the swimmer's depth; the held pieces on the builder's forearm (card F10, FREEZE #547)
Card F10's fifth goal is that every animation moves onto the builder's skeleton: swings, the roll, the bind, the parry, the bow, sneak,
swimming, sitting and sleeping, the fall on death. Part one hung the builder's body on the 17-bone driver with F4's `hbAttach`, and
`hbPose` copies the driver's sixteen joints onto it each frame, so in principle every pose the driver takes the builder takes too. Part
one tested a walk, a swing and a roll by counting the bone matrices that moved. This session checks each state the other way: does
each limb of the builder's body lie where the driver's does?

**How.** `tests/f10motion` (new) drives `tpUpdate` at fixed 1/60 s ticks into each state (rest, walk, sprint, sneak, the block with a
shield, a forehand at its strike, a cast, a jump, the bow at full draw, the fall on death, and swimming) and measures the angle
between each limb's line on the two bodies: shoulder to elbow, elbow to wrist, hip to knee, knee to ankle, hips to neck, both sides.
The two bodies do not rest alike (the builder's forearms hang 18° from the driver's, its upper arms 9°, its legs 6° to 7°, its spine
8°), but if the copy is exact those offsets turn with the limbs and stay the same in every pose. They do, to a tenth of a degree, in
all ten states: the copy carries every pose tpPose makes. The bind and the parry are the block's pose and the swing's (combat's
resolver decides them; the body has no pose of its own for either), so they are covered by the block and the swing. The death fall
lays the builder's body down with the driver (its head 4 cm over the root).

**The swimmer's depth (fixed).** `tpUpdate` sinks a swimmer so that "the head's top [is] a quarter metre over the sea" (F2b): it lowers
the body by `SCALE.PERSON_MODEL ×` the root's scale. Part one scales the player's root so the builder's body stands at the genome's
stature (`hb.st` metres), which makes `PERSON_MODEL ×` the scale `hb.st × PW.BODY`, 0.89 of the body: the builder's crown rode
0.44 m over the sea and its eyes 0.32 m, a swimmer standing in the water to the collarbones. On the new look the body is now lowered
by its own height, `hb.st`: the crown is 0.232 m over the sea and the eyes 0.119 m. The old look's line is unchanged.

**What the hands hold (fixed).** Part one carries a copy of each held piece in the builder's group, following the piece on the driver's
bone, with a weapon's grip moved into the builder's fingers. The pieces kept the driver's forearm's angle, though, and the two forearms
rest 18° apart: the blade was tilted 18° from where it sat in the old body's hand (part one's note, "the sword lies flatter"), and the
shield hung where the driver's forearm is, up to 9.6 cm from the builder's. `tpHBHold` now carries each piece from the driver's forearm
to the builder's: the turn that takes the driver's elbow-to-wrist line onto the builder's, about the elbow, and the move from one
elbow to the other; then the grip goes into the fingers as before. Each axis of every held piece now makes the same angle with the
builder's forearm as the piece does with the driver's, and the shield keeps its distance from the forearm, in every state.

**What the card asks that the body never had.** There is no swimming stroke, no seated or sleeping pose for the player in third person,
and no ragdoll for the player (the fall on death is a pose): the old body had none of them either, so there is nothing to move over.
A stroke, and sitting in a chair or lying in a bed in third person, are new animation; they are listed as owed, not built here.
