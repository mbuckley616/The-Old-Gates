## v80 — Session 1588 — The ship's rail, and over the side at the net
The scale sheet Michael approved (`scale-numbers-build.md`, the Places table) puts a rail 1.0 m above every deck, "so you cannot walk off
a rolling deck". It was never built: on every hull you could walk off the side into the sea, and from Portclare's quay (1.1 m) you could
step down onto her deck (1.0 m) without touching the net. A plain jump rises 0.84 m, so a rail that holds you needs a deliberate way over
the side. Michael's answer on #882 (29 Oct): **E at the boarding net goes over the side.**

**The rail** (`shipRailHolds`, beside the nets in `86-world-crime.js`). The player's step in the loop (`90-main.js`, the overworld's two
axis moves) is refused when it crosses a deck's outline (the platform's own `inside`, the hull's breadth at that point) while your feet
are under the rail's top: 1.0 m over the level you stand on (the castle's when you are up on it). It holds both ways, off a deck and onto
one from a quay or a deck alongside, and for every ship afloat: yours, a black sail's, a merchantman's. It holds a swimmer at her side
too: the first run of the check found that a walk from the quay dropped into the gap and the deck's platform then lifted the swimmer
straight aboard (the ground under you is the highest platform there, whatever your height), which is how one could always swim onto a
deck. A net is the way up from the water, as it was meant to be; the climb moves you itself, not the step, so it is untouched. Inside the hull's outline nothing changes: the
castle's ladders, its edge and the bulkhead are what they were.

**Over the side** (`overSide`, `overSidePrompt`). On a deck, with either net under the crosshair (the same box the climb uses, 5 m from
the eye) and within 2.5 m of the rail above it, the prompt reads *Press 'E' to go over the side*. E sets you a metre outboard of the net,
level with the rail, and you fall to what is there: the sea beside her, or the quay she lies alongside. It answers on a boarded ship too,
and not at the helm. The prompt sits after *climb aboard* another ship in the ship's dispatch (`85-world-sea.js`, two calls), so a black
sail's net under the crosshair from your deck still offers her net first.
