## v80 — Session 1962 — the long cloak's columns start evenly across the back: no groove down the spine, no step at the hem
Session 1958 owed this: "a small step is left at the very middle of the man's hem". Looked at closely (`tests/tmp`, the hem column by
column, thrown away), the step was the bottom of a bigger fault. A groove ran down the middle of the man's back from the collar to the hem.
Both are in Session 1958's own pictures and in Session 1953's.

**Why.** Since Session 1944 the cloak's 56 columns start at even steps along the shoulder line. That line is made of the furthest body points
found in 168 directions round the neck. Over the spine those points jump up and down the 19 cm band they are sought in (105.9, 102.8,
104.8, 102.4 cm) and in and out round the neck (19, 22, 15, 9, 12 cm). So the line zigzags there, and its measured length counted every zag.
The steps fell 1.5 to 3 cm apart across the back, where they are 6 cm over the shoulders. Six columns crowded together over the spine with
6 cm gaps either side of them, and kept that crowding all the way down. Bunched cloth reads as a pleat (the groove), and the crowded columns'
hems sat 4 cm above their neighbours (the step).

**What changed** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/people.js`; `vendor/people.builder.js` rebuilt with `--pack /nonexistent`). Before the line is
measured, `buildCloak` smooths it with six passes of a 1-2-1 average over neighbouring points; the ends stay fixed. The starts are then laid
along the smoothed line and cleared of the body as before. A shoulder cape is left alone: it keeps its evenly turned starts. No new part,
no draw, and the triangle count is unchanged.

Two other ways were tried and dropped:
- **Measuring along the smoothed line but placing the starts on the true one.** The starts landed on the zags again: 1.4 to 15 cm apart.
- **Smoothing the line but keeping each start's own height.** This gave a jagged top edge at the back.
