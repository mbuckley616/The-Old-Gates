## v80 — Session 1168 — A failing gate's skeleton: extra arms, spurs, lit cracks (L4, OG-073, prototype only)
Backlog L4's *failing gates change their foes* had no plan in the kit. OG-073 draws one foe per school at three conditions; Michael kept the sheet, and the catalogue calls the failing column the useful one: "the same model with extra limbs and broken anatomy, which a build can make from one rig". The monster rule keeps the glow to the seams. Solas's row comes first because its steady foe, the skeleton (Session 934), is already a plan, and the sheet's failing skeleton "grows extra arms and spines". The catastrophic column is a boss tier below the dragon (Michael's 8 B); it is not built here. This asks nothing: Michael kept the sheet and the backlog item says what to build.

The headgear by tier (L7, OG-052) was the other candidate, but the concept artist already has it out as a decision, so it was left alone. This run's branch starts from main while the old `auto/bodies` PR (#458, Sessions 1163 to 1165) is still open. That PR changes `people.js` and `builder.check.mjs`, so this session kept to `creatures.js` and its check to stay clear of it.

- **The plan** `failingskeleton` (`creatures.js`, listed in `FAILING`) is built from the steady skeleton with `re()`. It carries `school: 'Solas'`, `condition: 'failing'` and `from: 'skeleton'`, and keeps the skeleton's sword, shield and rags in the first pair's hands. What the gate has added:
  - **a second pair of arms** from the middle of the cage, 56 cm long, held out to the sides and bent 40°;
  - **a third pair**, small and crooked, 40 cm long, from under the cage. Each pair swings on its own beat (phases 1.7 and 3.4);
  - **nine bone spurs** down the back, raked up and out;
  - **three more ribs** each side, so the cage runs down to the pelvis;
  - **the head** pitched a little further down;
  - **the bones cracked**, with only the cracks lit Solas's white-gold (HDR 2.6, 2.15, 1.25). The sockets are lit pale gold.
- **Two small additions to the kit:**
  - `bare.seg` cuts a bare body's bone shafts into rings 2 cm apart. The glow is read at the vertices, and one-segment shafts had none along the bone, so the cracks could not show. The steady skeleton keeps its one-segment shafts.
  - `spines` takes a `dir`. On a bare body a spur now stands on the vertebrae (scaled by `bare.vr`); before, it floated 10 cm behind them at the grown spine's radius.
  - A spur is named `spur` and recorded in `B.spurs`.
