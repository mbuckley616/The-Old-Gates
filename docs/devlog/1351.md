**The shell.** The room is built inside the coaching inn's own front, as every room in the kit is built inside the street's shell (#303 A).
`pieces.js` now exports that front's plan (`gatelandsCoachInnPlan`), and `coachInn` builds its inn from it. That is a refactor only: the
vertices of all three ways of the coaching inn (three, two, house) hash identical to main's. The front is 12.6 by 9 m with walls of 0.45 m,
three storeys of 3.0, 2.9 and 2.6 m, and four flues in each gable's stack. The door is under its fanlight in the middle bay, and the
back door opens to the yard in the second bay from the west.

**The ground floor, `gatelandsCoachRoom` (`rooms.html?room=coach`).** One flagged taproom, 11.7 by 8.1 m inside. The front door opens
straight into it, as the village inn's does.
- The open hearth is under the west stack, 1.9 m wide and 1.35 m high, with its crane, pot and turf fire. A settle faces it, with a stool.
- The bar runs across the back wall, from the yard door's jamb to the stair: a counter of 1.05 m, the rack of kegs behind it, and two
  shelves of jugs either side of the back window.
- There are three tables with benches: by the west front windows, at the hearth's back corner, and east of the door.
- Under the east stack is the coach passengers' corner. It has a grate with a surround and mantel, two armchairs on a rug, and a long
  table under the east window with benches either side.
- The stair is the townhouse's dog-leg (16 risers a storey, the half-landing against the back wall, stacked twice). It is set in its own
  well in the fourth bay, the one bay whose back wall has no window, so no flight crosses a window seen from the yard. The well is
  closed by partitions and open to the taproom at its foot. Under the flights is a cupboard.

**Upstairs.** A corridor 1.2 m wide runs across the house from the landing, lit by two lamps on each floor. Off it are the rooms to let:
- On both floors, three across the front: west (4.6 m wide), middle (2.3 m) and east (4.6 m).
- On the first floor, two more behind: west and east. The back middle is the landing, lit by the back window.
- On the second floor, the back has no window, so it is the garret: trunks, and a linen press against the well.

Each room has a bed of 2 m, a chest with a candle at its foot, and a washstand against a partition. No bed or washstand stands under a
window, because the first floor's sills are 0.4 m off the boards. Three of the first-floor rooms have a grate on a gable: the west front,
the west back and the east front. The east back has none because its gable has a window there.

**The question (o.first).** Coaching inns kept their best first-floor front as the room where coach passengers dined.
- `first: 'rooms'` makes that floor's front three rooms: eight rooms to let in all.
- `first: 'long'` makes it one long room, the coffee room: a table of 5.8 m for twelve between the two front fires, a sideboard and a
  rug. That leaves five rooms to let.

Both are drawn. The answer decides how many rooms the innkeeper lets (`innRooms`) when the room comes into the game.

**The counts.** The room is 12,680 to 12,920 tris (rooms) and 13,568 (coffee room) in 20 draws, on the front's 7,083 in 11.

### Decision
DECISION #660: what the coaching inn's first floor holds at the front.
- **A. Three rooms to let** *(recommended)*: eight rooms in all, every one with a door off the corridor.
- **B. The coffee room**: one long room for the coach passengers, a table for twelve between two fires. Five rooms to let.

Recommendation: A. Eight doors on two corridors give the inn's rented room and its other guests' doors (the crime system's locks and
witnesses, `innTaken`) the most to work with. The passengers already have their corner by the east grate downstairs.
