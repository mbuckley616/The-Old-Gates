The quarry had not been, so this session builds it: `gatelandsQuarry` in `docs/prototypes/levelup/places.js`, at `places.html?place=quarry&view=quarry|face|kiln|top|far` (`&dusk`). Cited image: OG-046, whose *mines & quarries* panel shows benched faces cut into a hill with a crane over them, and whose row in the catalogue says they tie to the survival skills. The look follows the small field quarries and draw kilns of the Irish midlands, which is the Gatelands' country. It is stone for walls and lime for the townland, not a mine. Nothing in it touches the canon.
- **The pit:** a floor 18 m wide and 19 m deep, cut level into a hill that rises toward the back and opens to a lane at the front.
  - The back face is worked in two lifts: a lower face of 3 m to a bench 2.5 m deep, then an upper face of about 3.5 m to the turf.
  - The side faces run down to nothing at the mouth.
- **The faces are built from the rock's beds,** not drawn on:
  - The hill has one set of strata (0.35 to 0.7 m thick; the bed under the bench reaches 0.8 m so the bench lies on a bedding plane). The same beds appear on all three faces.
  - Each bed is broken into blocks of 0.8 to 2.2 m along its joints. Each block stands proud of the face line by 0 to 13 cm, so the courses read by their shadows.
  - The rock stops 0.15 m under the turf. Each bed is cut off where the soil comes down over it, so a band of soil shows along the top of every face, as it does in a real quarry.
- **The rim:** a dry-stone wall of 1.0 m runs along the rim, 1.6 m back from the faces, wherever the drop below is over 1.5 m.
- **The work on the floor:**
  - shear legs of two poles with their head 6.5 m up, guyed back to a stake, and a crab winch at one foot. The tackle holds a block of 1.2 by 0.8 by 0.7 m in a chain sling, 0.55 m off the floor;
  - a block being split, with seven plugs and feathers standing in its row of holes;
  - rough blocks lying where they fell, and a spoil heap of rubble along the west face's foot;
  - a stack of squared stone by the mouth, with a two-wheeled stone cart;
  - a mason's lean-to under thatch with a banker, a block, the mallet and three chisels.
- **The kiln:** a draw kiln of rubble built into the bank east of the mouth, 5 by 6 m and 4.4 m high, battered.
  - Its draw arch, 1.6 m wide and 2.3 m high under dressed voussoirs, opens onto a yard levelled to the floor. The arch's back is black with soot, and burnt lime is spilt before it.
  - The pot is open on the top, ringed with dressed stone. The bank behind is made up to a terrace 0.3 m under the top, so the stone is barrowed in from the hill. Broken stone lies there ready to charge her.
- **Fixed on the way:**
  - **The land's step in front of the faces:** the land first stepped up from the floor to the hill one grid cell inside the pit, in front of the faces, so a wall of turf stood where the upper face should show. The boundary's own vertices are now counted as inside the pit, so the step falls behind the rock.
  - **Turf through the faces:** the blocks had 2 cm joints, which showed the turf behind them. They are now butted and overlapped 12 mm, so no hairline shows the sky through the turf's underside.
  - **Gaps on the slopes:** the side faces first ended in steps on the slope, each block's top cut flat at the lowest turf over it. Then a bed cut by the turf left a wedge of gap under the bed above it. Every bed is now cut by the same line under the turf, top and bottom alike.
  - **The arch:** the draw arch was first a hole that crossed the front wall's outline, which the triangulation drops. It is now a notch in the outline itself, with the arch's floor levelled to the yard.

### Needs eyes
- **The faces' texture:** the faces carry the kit's one rock texture at one scale, so up close the beds read by their setbacks more than by colour. A limestone with darker partings between the beds would read better, and that is a texture, not a shape.
- **The kiln's masonry:** the kiln uses the kit's coursed stone, which reads as blocks rather than the rubble of a country kiln.
- **No smoke or glow:** the kiln stands cold. Whether it burns, and who works the quarry, are the systems builder's and the quest writer's.
