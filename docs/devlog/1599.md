## v80 — Session 1599 — The shallows' fish schools wear the kit's herring (card F4, new look)
L3's peaceful land animals still wait on a spawn (the systems builder's), so this run took the next animal the game already has as a
placeholder. That is the fish schools in the shallows (`85-world-sea.js`, `spawnFishSchool` and `tickFish`). A coastal chunk with water
1.2 to 7 m deep has about an even chance of a school: 10 to 17 fish circling, each a four-sided 0.42 m cone in one InstancedMesh. No
concept sheet draws them. OG-016 puts "fish in schools" in the coastal waters, and a herring is not an open design, so this needs no question.

**The plan** (`creatures.js`, `herring`, in `SHORE` beside the gull): the Riverjaw salmon's body at 0.4 of its size, slimmer and deeper
than wide (spine 0.42 by 1.2). It has no hooked jaw, a big dark eye, fins at 0.8 of the salmon's and a small forked tail. Its flanks are
silver under a blue-green back. The kit's counter-shading only darkened what faced 0.15 to 0.85 up. A deep, narrow fish's flanks face
sideways, so on the herring that left the back as a line along the ridge. A plan's new `saddleUp` sets that range, here −0.05 to 0.4.
Without it the default holds, so every other plan is as it was. It is 18 bones and 16,244 triangles at the kit's own cell.

**In the game** (`34-creatures.js`, behind the new look): seventeen skinned bodies a school would be seventeen draws where there was
one. So `hcSchoolKit` builds the herring once, at a cell of 0.036, and bakes it at rest with the people's `hbMerge` into one plain
geometry of 1,472 triangles. It is scaled to the old cone's 0.42 m and its head is turned to −x. `hcSchools` gives that geometry and one
material to every school within 35 m of the eye, so a school is still one instanced draw. Past 35 m (with a metre's hysteresis) a school
keeps its own cone, 8 triangles a fish, so a loaded coast's shallows stay cheap. The tail swings in the vertex shader: a wave down the
body from about a third of the way back, 3.5 cm at the tail, phased by `gl_InstanceID` so no two fish beat together.

The head goes to −x, not +z, because `tickFish` turns each instance to face out from its circle (−a+π/2 on a cone pointing +z). The old
cones swam sideways. With the head at −x the same turn points each herring along its circle, and `tickFish` is unchanged.

`dropChunk` (`80-world-terrain.js`, one line) disposes every InstancedMesh's geometry. That would dispose the herring every school
shares, so it now calls `hcSchoolDrop` first, which hands the school its own cone back. A fish decides nothing, so it has no key.
