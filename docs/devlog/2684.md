## v80 — Session 2684 — the hands in first person are skin, not white gloves: they take the frame's tone curve

Michael, in chat on 25 Nov, after a look at the critic's street at 10h (`docs/critic/2026-11-24-street-10h.jpg`): the first-person
hands read as two white mitts in every daylit frame. They are the builder's own hands (F10, part two), with the genome's skin in their
vertex colours: a mean of 0.565, 0.483, 0.406, taken down by `FP_HB_DIM` (0.64) to a mid tan. The colour was never the fault.

The fault was the pass they are drawn in. The world's frame is tone-mapped: `landPost` sets ACES at `LAND.exposure` (with P10b's
grade in the same chunk) in the scene's `onBeforeRender`, and at Middle and High `landRender` composites through ACES too. A room's
frame goes through `landRoomPost` (Neutral). The view model is drawn afterwards in its own scene, `VM_SCENE`, which had no hooks, so it
was drawn linear. `vmLight` (S1306) gives it the place's own lights (at 10h, a sun of 0xfff0d1 at 1.15, an ambient and a sky of 0.55
each), and skin under the world's whole light with no curve to roll it off clipped to near white.

The fix, in `js/16-viewmodel.js`: `vmLight` records the scene it read (`VM_POST.src`), and `VM_SCENE.onBeforeRender` and
`onAfterRender` run that scene's own hooks when it has a post (`userData.landPost`). The hands, the weapons, the shield and the torch
get exactly the curve, exposure and grade of the frame they are drawn over: ACES and the grade outdoors, Neutral in a room, and nothing
on the old look, where neither post exists. The renderer's tone mapping is put back after the draw by the scene's own after-hook.
Nothing about the lights, the colours or the bake changed.
