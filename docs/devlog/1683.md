## v80 — Session 1683 — a gate's pool kerb shows the gate's condition
Session 1678 owed this one: the pool's kerb was a smooth lathe in every gate, and "in a ruined gate it might want chips and a fallen stone, as in the condition
kit". OG-120 (backlog L10, *A gate's condition, seen*, Michael's note) sets out the three conditions. A steady gate is whole. A failing gate is the same hall with
blocks out of place, and a catastrophic one is broken. `gateCondition` already rolls one condition per gate (half steady, 35% failing, 15%
catastrophic). The floating stones and the gaps in the floor read it. The pool now reads it too.

`gatePoolProp(id,R,cond)` (`56-dungeon-build.js`) takes the gate's `gen.cond`, and `gtKerbWear` picks the stones from their own stream (`gatepoolwear`
and the pool's id). The steady kerb's shades and heights therefore never move, and the same gate is worn the same on every visit.
- **Failing:** two stones, roughly opposite each other, have settled. Each has dropped 3 to 6 cm and leans 6 to 10 degrees out about its foot's outer edge, a shade darker.
- **Catastrophic:** four stones lean 10 to 16 degrees. One of the four is split in two, with a 6 cm crack between the halves. One half keeps the lean and the other stands
  nearly upright. A chip of it, 0.16 by 0.14 by 0.12 m, lies on the floor beside the kerb.

The solid is unchanged, a column of the pool's radius. Every leaning top stays within 10 cm of the rim (7 cm at 1.8 m), and a body stops 0.3 m
out, so nothing new stands in the way. The chip is 0.14 m high, under a step, and is not a solid. A failing kerb is still 388 triangles at 1.8 m and 724 at 2.6 m. A catastrophic one
is 414 or 736 (the split's halves and the chip). Both are still 2 draws, and the ice still covers the water as before.
