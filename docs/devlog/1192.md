## v80 — Session 1192 — the Gatelands farmhouse as a room (L9, OG-115; card F9; prototype only)
FREEZE #470 (card F3) is still open, so this run stayed in the prototypes. OG-115 draws three homes for the Gatelands: the cottage,
the townhouse and the farmstead. The kit had built the cottage (Session 852) and the townhouse (Session 1189), and the backlog
carried the farmstead as owed. The street builds a farmhouse on every country lot of two or three bays, and until now its windows
looked into a dark box. The only room built inside that shell so far was the village inn (Session 893), which is a public house.
This session builds the farm family's home inside the street's own shell, as Michael's A on #303 sets: `rooms.js`
`gatelandsFarmhouse`, inside `GL_PLANS.farmhouse`. The shell is 10.5 by 6.2 m, with whitewashed walls of 0.5 m and storeys of 2.9
and 2.7 m. Its slate roof sits behind parapets, with a stack of two flues on each gable. The door is in the middle bay and the back
door opens to the yard east of the middle. It asks nothing: the plan is the Irish country's two-storey farmhouse, which the shell's
front already draws (a central door with a fanlight and a window either side, three windows above). I left the shell untouched.

**The plan.**
- **The hall** runs 1.7 m wide from the front door to the back wall, flagged. Lime-plastered stud partitions at x ±0.9 close it.
  The front door's leaf hangs open against the east partition.
- **The stair** rises up the hall's west side from front to back: 16 risers of 0.179 m on goings of 0.21 m (2R+G 0.569). Its string
  and handrail are on the hall's side, and it arrives on the landing at the back.
- **The kitchen**, east of the hall, is the house's working room, flagged, under bare joists with three oak beams across it.
  - The open hearth sits under the east stack: a breast 0.5 m proud, a 1.6 by 1.3 m sooted opening, an oak lintel and a hearthstone.
    A turf fire burns on the flags, with the crane, a pot on its hook and a kettle at the edge.
  - The dresser of delft stands on the back wall, three shelves of plates over a cupboard.
  - The settle bed stands against the hall partition: a bench by day, its box a bed at night.
  - The deal table is under the front window, with its form, a candle, a crock and a soda loaf.
  - There is a stool and a súgán chair at the hearth, the churn by the back door, a spinning wheel (a Saxony wheel, wool on the
    flyer), two flitches of bacon hung from the beam, and a tin lantern.
- **The parlour**, west of the hall, is the good room: boarded floor, plastered ceiling. It has a grate of coals in a painted surround
  with a mantel shelf, two chairs at it, a round table with a candle, the glazed press of good delft, a long-case clock, a rug, and a
  side table with a jug under the front window.
- **The landing** runs over the hall from the stair's head to the middle front window, railed round the well, with a blanket chest
  under the window. A door off its back leads into each room.
- **The west room** is the parents'. It holds their bed with its head to the back wall, a rocking cradle by the fire (OG-115's
  childcare scene), a chest at the bed's foot and a washstand with basin, jug and candle under the window.
- **The east room** is the children's: two beds with their heads to the partition, a painted chest under the back window, a
  washstand and a súgán chair by the fire.
- **Every fire** is on a gable's flue, one a storey each side: the kitchen hearth and the parents' grate on the flues the shell
  already draws. The ceiling upstairs is boarded under the slate.
