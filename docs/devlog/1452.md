## v80 — Session 1452 — the Mark's longhouse room by size (prototype, card F9)
(Numbered 1450 when written; renumbered because QA's run took 1450 and 1451 at the same hour, on `auto/qa-1450`.)
Section I held nothing for the look builder, and #744 (where the farmsteads stand) is unanswered, so the farmhouse's room waits. The
backlog's L9 line on OG-115's homes has every Gatelands and Aurennais home in the game with its room, but not the Mark's: a Markish
home on `?look=new` is the kit's hall outside (Session 1286, #277 C, #285 A) and still opens on a generated room. The room is drawn
and chosen: `markLonghouse` in `rooms.js` (Session 870, Michael's A on #311, "as drawn"). It is drawn for one hall, 7.6 by 14 m under
shingle with a smoke louver. The game's halls are fitted to their lots instead.

**The halls the game builds.** A survey of every Markish settlement on the new look (327 sites, each planned with `genSettlement` and
let go) counted 1,392 kit halls that are homes: 930 shingle, 462 turf, 5.6 to 8.4 m wide and 6.5 to 14.5 m deep (median 7.2 by 9.5;
409 are under 8 m deep, many held to 7.5 by the row behind). 741 have a chimney, because `markLot` gives one to 65% of shingle halls
and 30% of turf. The drawn room does not fit most of them. Its long hearth sits under the louver at 15% of the depth behind the
middle, and its partition stands 2.6 m from the back wall: under about 12 m deep the two overlap. A chimney hall has no louver at all,
and its stack (0.9 m square, a quarter of the width left of the axis and a quarter of the depth back, from the ground through the
roof) stands inside the room, across the left aisle and the left row of posts.

**`markLonghouse({ w, d, roof, turf, porch, chimney, carve, wall, shell })`.** The room is built inside the shell the street stands,
with that shell's own options. With no options it is the drawn hall. Its geometry matches main's to the millimetre, except that two
furs are now cut 1 to 1.3 cm short so that none runs past its bench's end. The rules:
- **The drawn plan** (partition and family quarters) where a louver hall has room for a long hearth of 1.7 m or more, centred under
  the louver, with 0.5 m clear before the partition. That is 80 of the 1,392.
- **One room** in any other louver hall (571). The partition goes. The bed is a bench bed of furs, 2.1 m by the bench's depth, at the
  back of the left wall's bench, its pillow against the back wall. The chest stands at its foot along the wall with the oil lamp on it,
  and the cradle is in the right-hand back corner. The long hearth is the longest of 2.6 down to 1.1 m that fits between the door's turn
  and the back (2.6 in every hall surveyed). The Mark's own houses slept on their wall benches, so this is the longhouse with its
  quarters let go, not a new room.
- **A chimney hall** (741) is one room round a peis, the raised hearth with cheeks and a stone hood built against the stack's face to
  the axis. It is the hunting cabin's (Session 1199), with a pot on a hook. The bed and chest go on the right, and the walk runs straight
  down the axis past the fire. A post that would stand in the stack is left out, the left bench breaks round it, the lamp hangs over
  the right aisle, and the cradle goes in the left back corner where it clears the stack (not in the narrowest, shortest halls).
- **A narrow hall** (under 5.8 m inside, 280 of them) has benches 0.9 m deep and posts 1.0 m in, where they were 1.1 and 1.25. That
  keeps the aisle each side of the long hearth at 0.69 m. The chopping block moves to the wall, and the workshop's pegs are as many as
  its bench holds (3 to 5).
- **Turf halls** take their own walls (2.4 m) and pitch (32°). That leaves 2.61 m under the tie beams.

`rooms.html?room=longhouse&lw=&ld=&chimney=1&roof=turf` shows any of them, and `view=back` in one room looks at the bench bed. The
size parameters are `lw`/`ld` because `w` is already the still page's width. The first pictures read it as a hall 1,920 m wide.
