## v80 — Session 1490 — the day in through a room's openings: the longhouse and the barn (section I; #765 C)
The second of section I's items for this lane from the critic's walk of 27 Oct: a Markish longhouse near black at noon (`c2_1_s7`,
`g_c2_1_s7_16`, Dáire's House, the chimney hall: the frame's median 17, and *the two side windows light nothing past themselves*). The
barn of Session 1488 had the same fault (27 at noon against the byre's 60).
Numbered 1490: QA's Session 1489 landed on main first. The code's comments, the suites' checks and the backlog's line say S1490.

The rooms' daylight was a plane of the sky's colour outside each opening, lit by nothing, and the room's air (`kitRoomAir`'s ambient and
hemisphere) by the hour; nothing came in through the opening. Michael's C on #765 (*the lights, with the eye half open*) lights a room
from its own openings, fires and lamps, and the concept artist's `light.js` (`lightRoom`) lays the day in as a soft sky spot just inside
each opening, aimed in and down, by the opening's area and the hour's sky. This session carries that one part of the rule into the game:

- **`kitDaylight(sc_, ops, K)`** (`83-world-generator.js`, beside `kitRoomAir`): one `SpotLight` of the sky's colour 0.3 m inside each
  opening, aimed 3 m in and 1.6 m down, of `KD_SKY` (2) × the opening's square metres × the hour (1 from 7 to 18h, 0.4 at dawn and dusk,
  none after dark), at most 6; `K` is the room's own share. The lights carry `userData.daylight`.
- **The longhouse** (`buildKitLonghouse`): a spot inside each side window (0.6 by 0.5 m, one pair every 4 m) and in the open front door,
  so 5 or 7 spots.
- **The barn** (`buildKitBarn`): the openings of half a square metre or more, which are both doorways on the yard and the pitching door,
  at a share of 0.4. At the full share the stable door's spot, a metre from the partition's boards, read 96 from the arrival. The slits
  (0.16 by 0.95 m) light only themselves: eight more lights would buy nothing you could see.

The strength was chosen on Dáire's hall at noon. Without the spots it read 17, at 2 it reads 37 (the arrival frame's median, the
critic's measure), at 3.5 it read 44 and at 6 it read 51. The prototype's longhouse reads 21 at noon under C (with its louver, door
shut) and its cottage 65, so 37 is a hall plainly lit by day through its door that is still darker than a cottage. Nothing changes after dark: how far the eye opens at night (C's
other half, `landPost`'s exposure stepped toward the frame's median) is owed, and is the larger part of #765.
