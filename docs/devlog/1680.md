### Decision
**DECISION #976 — Magic B: what Sioc's touch does to a lock.**
**The question.** Magic B's build sheet (`docs/design/magic-build.md`, the Touched column) gives Sioc's touch "freezes a pool, a lock's works". The pool is built (Session 1680, on the look builder's S1678 pools). The lock has no rule: nothing says what a frozen lock does, and a player feels it on every locked chest and after-hours door. The lock today is Oblivion's (S142): a pin per tumbler, set at the shear while it dwells 150–300 ms by Finesse, and a mistimed press snaps a pick.

- **A (recommended). The works stiffen.** For 30 s by the play clock a lock you touched with Sioc holds each pin at the top twice as long (the dwell ×2, so 300–600 ms), and a mistimed press drops the pin without snapping the pick. You still need a pick and still play the lock; in a town it is still a lock picked, seen or unseen by the same rule. One use of Uisce.
- **B. Frozen and struck.** The works shatter: the lock opens with no pick and no game, but it is broken for good (the door stays unlocked, the chest's lock is gone), and in a town the crack is heard at twice a witness's range.
- **C. A pin a tier.** Sioc sets one pin at Impression, two at Comprehension and every pin at Mastery; the rest are picked as now.
- **D. Leave it out.** Sioc's touch freezes pools only.

**Recommendation: A.** The brief asks for "a challenging, rewarding progression" and for magic that changes how you move through the world, not one that replaces another skill. A keeps the lock a lock and makes a mage-thief a real build: the spell buys time, the hand still opens it. B makes picks and Finesse pointless for any Adept mage; C is A's idea with a hard skip at Mastery, which turns the pick game off at the end.
