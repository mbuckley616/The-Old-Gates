## v80 — Session 1643 — The Orrery moves to a true headland (L5, OG-106; the critic's 31 Oct report)
The critic's third finding on S1634's Broken Orrery: it did not stand on a headland. On seed 1 the search had picked a brow 41.9 m up,
but along its axis the ground behind it rose to 48–50 m from 40 to 120 m inland. The platform sat 6–8 m below the land behind it,
cut into the seaward slope; from 70 m inland only the rings showed over a flat terrace, and from Pharenna's edge, 250 m out, the hill hid
it completely. The search's score counted height and sea headings, but nothing about higher ground behind the site.

`wonderOrrerySite` (`js/82-world-structures.js`) now measures, for every point that passes, the highest ground on sixteen headings at
45, 70 and 100 m, and its score loses `WONDER_ABOVE_K` (1.5) for each metre of that ground over the point. One metre behind costs as much
as one heading of sea. Nothing else in the search changed: the same grid, the same clearances from sites, doors and roads, and the same
limits on fall and on distance to the sea.

On seed 1 the brow scores 26.6 less 1.5 × 8 and loses to a low, true headland: **(26375, 7875), 12.8 m up, the sea on 12 of 16 headings
and 65 m off, its ground falling 3.8 m round it, and nothing within 100 m standing more than 0.8 m over it** (score 20.5). It is 519 m past
the nearest pad. The probe of S1634 found that Aurenne's high shores are steep and its headlands low, 8–12 m; this is one of those.
At 12.8 m the platform is lower, but the outer ring's top is 57 m over the sea (44 m over the floor), and the land does not hide it: from
220 m inland the rings stand over the trees by day and by night. The steps are 12.84 → 13.44 → 14.24 → 15.04 m, and the stamp's work is
smaller (a fall of 3.8 m, not 9.9).

The search costs about 1.4 s more on software GL with the extra 48 samples a passing point (2.3 to 3.5 s in the suite; 2.75 s in a probe).
Only points near the sea reach that step, and the search runs once a seed.
