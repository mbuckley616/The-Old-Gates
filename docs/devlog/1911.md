## v80 — Session 1911 — OG-040's last four in the country (backlog L3)
The run's third item. Sessions 1906 and 1907 gave the last four of OG-040's strange beasts bodies on the crawlers' path: the Mossback, the
Sailshell, the Shatterhorn and the Mineprowler. Both entries left the tables to the systems builder. No question was needed. This is the same
rule Sessions 1870 and 1895 applied to the other ten: the catalogue's OG-040 row (`docs/art/review.html`) gives each beast's habitat and
rarity, and Session 1769's weights turn rarity into a number (common 3, unmarked 2, uncommon 1, rare 0.5). A beast the catalogue calls hostile
answers back by #1050 A's rule.

**Where they stand** (`WILD_ISLE`, `88-world-ticks.js`). One at a time, each:
- **The Mossback** (woodlands, uncommon): 1 in the Gatelands' forest and autumn woods.
- **The Shatterhorn** (alpine peaks, rare): 0.5 on the Mark's tundra, its high ground. It is not on the moor, which is not peaks.
- **The Mineprowler** (deserts, uncommon): 1 in Aurenne's dunes and wastes.
- **The Sailshell** (deserts and dry valleys, unmarked): 2 in the dunes and the wastes, and in the scrub (`wasteland`) for the dry valleys.

The backlog's note (S1907) put the Mineprowler "by Aurenne's mines and ruins". The catalogue says deserts, and the catalogue is the rule.
A chunk still rolls a group one time in four, so the country holds as many animals as before.

**Their paces** (`WILD_ISLE_KINDS`, `42-zone-enemies.js`), in metres a second, walking and running:
- The tortoises: 0.2 and 0.4. The kit's own walk is the slowest it has (speedK 0.22 and 0.25), and a tortoise does not bolt.
- The Shatterhorn ("a peaceful giant"): 0.8 and 6, a browser's walk, a little under the Frostmane's 7 for its size.
- The Mineprowler ("lithe six-legged spiny sand predator: hostile"): 0.6 and 7, a lynx's walk and a fox's run. It is in
  `WILD_ANSWER_KINDS`, so it grazes, never bolts, and turns on you when you strike it or come within 4 m. Like the Stormplume and the
  Redcrest it then fights with the Boar's 16 health and its blow of 5.

**Meat** (`MEAT_BODIES`, `MEAT_N`, `50-travel.js`). The Shatterhorn is a grazer, so by S1769's rule it gives a deer's 2 to 4. The tortoises
and the Mineprowler give none. No rule covers a tortoise, and none was invented.

### Verified (headless Chromium)
`wildlife` 1/1, no retry, with one new check. Each of the four builds as a wild animal on its own body. The Mineprowler answers at 16
health and 5 damage; the other three are at 1 health and do not answer. The Shatterhorn gives meat and the other three give none. The rows
read exactly `gatelands/forest:1,gatelands/autumn:1`, `mark/tundra:0.5`, `aurenne/dunes:1,aurenne/wastes:1` and
`aurenne/dunes:2,aurenne/wasteland:2,aurenne/wastes:2`. A Mineprowler grazes with you at 10 m and turns on you at 3 m: 53 health lost
over 4 s. Every earlier check passes. `parsecheck` is clean.

### Needs eyes
- A tortoise making off at 0.4 m/s is easy prey to anyone who wants one. It gives no meat and no loot, so there is nothing to farm. Whether
  striking one should feel worse than it does is a design call, if it comes up in play.
- A 6.3 m Shatterhorn on the tundra's slopes: `wildGround` places it by its centre only (slope, solids, roads), so its hind legs may stand
  over a drop on broken ground.
- The Sailshell is the commonest of the four (2 in three of Aurenne's biomes), because the catalogue gives it no rarity. The Sailshell
  "migrates between oases", which nothing models.
