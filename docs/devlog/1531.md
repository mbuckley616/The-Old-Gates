## v80 — Session 1531 — Card F11 in the game: your sloop is the kit's Gatelands sloop on the new look

Card F11 (ships and the sea) had every hull drawn in the prototype (`docs/prototypes/levelup/ships.js`, Sessions 858–1046, all five
classes in all three dresses; OG-004) but none in the game: your ship on the new look was still the old vertex-coloured loft with a
gaff and a jib. F11's parents (F2a and F3) have merged, so this is the first kind brought in, the one every player sails first.

`PKIT.ships.sloop(o)` in `js/38-kit.js` is `gatelandsSloop` ported as plain script, as the kit's other pieces are: the same lines
(13 by 4.4 m, draft 1.05, the deck 1.0 over the sea), ten clinker strakes a side lapped 2.5 cm, tarred below the water and oak above
with the green sheer strake under the cream wale, the raked stem and sternpost, the fore-and-aft deck planks cut to the hull's
half-breadth, stanchions and a rail 1.0 m over the deck, one mast, the square green sail with the harp on a yard 9 m up (its foot
2.2 m over the deck), shrouds and stays, the rudder on the post and a tiller (Michael's A on #306). It is baked as one geometry with a
group a material (the hull in 9 draws), the yard and sail as a rig of their own in the mast's frame, and the tiller as its own group.
Three changes from the prototype: the hatch is forward at L/2 − 3.2 (`o.hatchZ`), where the game's cabin door is (`cabinUpdate`);
the braces and sheets are left off, since they would hang loose once the yard braces round; and the harp's texture carries no
colour space, as the kit's do.

`shipBake('sloop','player')` on the new look now returns `shipBakeKit()` (`85-world-sea.js`), which keeps every contract the rest of
the sea code reads: the deck's outline (`deck.at`, the kit's half-breadth less 6 cm, inside the stanchions; from −6.15 to 6.15), the
props (the mast, the bucket, and the hatch's coaming as a box, so the stowed cargo keeps off it), the rig typed `square` so
`shipTrim` braces it to the wind, and `at(z)` for the nets, which now also gives the hull's own section (`sect`), so
`shipNetsGeo` lays the boarding net 5 cm outside the moulded line, clear of the laps. `buildShipMesh` takes the bake's materials and
hangs the tiller in place of the wheel (`userData.tiller`); at the helm it is put over against the turn. Other people's sloops (the
black sails) and the other four classes are unchanged, as is the old look.

The sloop is 24,748 triangles in 12 draws with its nets (the old one 5–6k in 4). The clinker strakes are most of it; it is one ship
near you, against a town street of 1.1–1.85 M, so it was not cut down.

Four suites described the old sloop and were changed to describe the new one where the bake is the kit's: `sailtrim` (one square
rig, under 30k triangles), `hullbakes` (the rig), `ships` (under 30k, and its mast as tall as the cog's rather than shorter) and
`netlook` (the net measured against the kit's own section; the crosshair's box 30 cm inside the beam rather than 25, since the
clinker hull draws in to 1.92 m at the waterline). `shipaim` looked astern from the helm to prove E does nothing there; a tiller
lies astern of the helm, so with a tiller it looks forward instead.

### Decision
Which island's dress your own ship wears. Today every sloop of yours is the Gatelands', and the cutter (the Mark's yards only) and
the caravel (Aurenne's only) are not in question. For the sloop, the cog and the galleon:
- **A.** The yard's island: the dress of the island where she was bought or last refitted, kept on the ship (recommended).
- **B.** Your own people's: the dress of the island your character comes from, wherever she was bought.
- **C.** The Gatelands' always, as built today.
