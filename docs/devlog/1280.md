## v80 — Session 1280 — the Mark's halls in the game (card F6)

The Mark's house in the new kit was settled in October: Michael's C on #277 (two halls in three dark shingle, one in three turf, by lot)
and his A on #285 (the turf redone as dry sod). `markHall` was drawn in `pieces.js` (Sessions 819, 825, 859) and its lot rule `markLot`
in Session 831 (`mark.html?street=`); Session 1159 copied `markHall` into the game's kit for the alehall. But on the new look every
Markish town still stood in the old builder's houses: only the Gatelands' lots had come into the kit (Sessions 1065–1143). This brings
the Mark's homes and shops in, as Session 1072 did for the Gatelands. Nothing new is drawn, so no question.

**The kit.** `js/38-kit.js` copies `markLot` as it stands (`PKIT.mk.lot`).

**The lots** (`genSettlement`, `83-world-generator.js`). The kit-towns pass that widened a Gatelander settlement's lots now runs for a
Markish one too (`MK`), sharing its helpers (`corners`, `apart`, `clear`, `near`); the Gatelands' own passes sit in an `else` and are
unchanged. A Markish home or shop of the kit's trades (not a church, keep, guild hall, inn, the shipwright or the foreign quarter by the
quay, which keep the old builder) takes `markLot(site.id, lot.n)`: its roof, porch, chimney or louver, carving and wall planks, all keyed
by the town and the lot's id. The hall stands gable to the street, and the lot becomes its footing and its stone steps: 0.15 m of footing
behind, the hall, and 1.8 m of steps before the door (3.35 m with a porch). The lot's front stays on the street line, so the door's
place, the id and the resident stay where they were, and you knock at the steps' foot: the world has no footholds outside, so the steps
are part of the house's solid, as a porch's are.

The prototype's halls are 8.5 to 14.5 m deep (turf 8.5–11, shingle 11–14.5), but a town's lots are 4–6 m deep in rows 10.7 m apart, so
a hall is fitted: the widest that fits at the least depth (down to 5.6 m wide), then the deepest at that width (down to 6.5 m); a porch
that will not fit gives way to plain steps. Each is halved to within 5 cm. My first try walked every width and depth in steps (up to
480 tries a lot) and cost the town 640 ms; halving takes about a dozen. One more rule came from the suite's first run: a porch hall's
door looked onto a neighbour's side wall 1.4 m off (the lots keep 1.2 m apart). So an apron of 1.8 m before each hall's steps is kept
clear of every other lot, and each lot clear of every hall's apron. That cost 9 of 48 halls in the test town; their lots keep the old
house.

**The halls** (`kitMarkHall`). `PKIT.mk.hall` with its lit windows keyed by the town and the lot as a Gatelands house's are, set back on
its lot by its steps, into the town's one kit group (merged per material at the end, so the halls share the Gatelands' path: one mesh
per material, the far copy, `houseLod`). The far copy is a box for the footing and the walls in the plank's colour and a prism for the
roof with its ridge along the hall, dark for shingle and tawny for turf. Smoke rises from the chimney or the louver.
