## v80 — Session 1308 — The cliff terraces' bridge stands on the gorge's floor (card F8, prototype)
The second of the critic's three findings on the prototypes (`docs/critic.md`, s827) that falls in this lane: in the Mark's cliff
terraces (Session 1242, OG-034), "the stone bridge's near pier stops in mid-air about two-thirds of the way down the cliff, with the
gorge's shadow behind it, so from the valley the arch hangs off the terrace like a doorway rather than standing on anything."

**What was wrong.** On paper the piers were sound: each ran down to a metre under the gorge's bed (−26.5 m), inside the rock. But
the gorge's wall falls from the terrace's level to −16.6 m in the first 1.4 m inside the lip, and to the bed 1 m further in. So
the pier's inner corner stood proud of that wall from the springing (−5 m) down to where it met the rock at −16.6. That joint
lay in the gorge's shadow, so seen from the valley the pier's lit face ended in a dark void about two-thirds of the way down.

**What changed.** Each pier now goes down plumb from the springing to a knee at −14 m, then batters out down the gorge's wall to
a footing on the bed at 3.2 m from the middle, clear of the stream (1.3 m). The footing's toe stands 0.5 m proud of the highest
ground under it. The pier's foot goes 1 m below the lowest ground under the whole pier, read from the land through the bridge's
width at every 0.2 m (it was the bed's height at the middle). The arch, its ring, the deck and the parapets are unchanged, and
the clear span at the springing is still 9.2 m. It costs 16 triangles: 59,392 on the page, against 59,376.
