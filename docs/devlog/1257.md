## v80 — Session 1257 — the rooms' post: a lamp close by shades instead of burning out (backlog I, s782)

The critic found that in Odhrán's Stores at 13h, standing behind the keeper, his dark purple tunic read as bare skin and his
hands burnt yellow-white, and that the Wayfarer at 22h had a white-yellow patch of wall by a candle. The cause is the frame,
not the bodies. Session 1180 (F3) put ACES and P10b's grade on the open world's frame only and left rooms drawn as before, linear
and clipped at 1. A kit shop has the lantern over the room (2.2, warm, falling off over 17 m) and a warm ambient of 0.45. Together
they put about 2.2 on a keeper 3.4 m off, more by the smithy's forge (1.6 at 0.9 m up), so every pale surface clipped flat. Since
the light is orange, the red channel clips first, and a purple tunic's red runs ahead of its blue and reads as skin.

A generated room's scene (`buildInteriorFor` and `buildKitTaproom`, `83-world-generator.js`) now calls `landRoomPost`
(`89-world-land.js`), the rooms' counterpart of `landPost`. On the new look it turns tone mapping on in the scene's
`onBeforeRender` and back off in `onAfterRender`, so the hands' scene drawn after keeps theirs. The world's grade chunk is split
out of `landPost` as `landGrade()` so a room loaded straight from a save gets it too. The curve is Khronos' neutral one
(`THREE.NeutralToneMapping`, exposure `LAND.roomExposure` = 1), not the world's ACES. I tried four side by side by the smithy's
forge. ACES stopped the burn but greyed the firelight: the orange walls went beige and the room lost its warmth. AgX greyed it
more. The neutral curve is the identity up to about 0.76 and rolls off above it while keeping each colour's hue, so a room is as
bright as before and only what was clipping changes. The grade rides along, as it does outdoors: shadows a touch cool, highlights
warm, saturation 1.14. Rooms of the legacy zones (`58-interiors-legacy.js`), dungeons and gates are untouched. On the old look
`landRoomPost` returns at once.

`f3land`'s last room check said "indoors the frame is not tone-mapped", which was F3's scope (rooms "drawn exactly as before"),
not a decision of Michael's. It now says the room is tone-mapped with the neutral curve and the renderer goes back to none after it.
