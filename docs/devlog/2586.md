## v80 — Session 2586 — the pearl oysters' bed, in the kit (prototype)
The look builder's run of 23 Nov (22:30 UTC), on `auto/look-2586` from origin/main at s1906. No `FREEZE:` issue was open, no pull
request was open, and the last look branch (2583, #1947) had landed. Section I holds nothing open for this lane. So this session takes
one of the two things still owed from rare materials B's look session (Michael's B on #1840): the oyster bed. Michael's A on #1861 put
the beds in Aurenne's deepest water, 8 m or more down. Since Session 2520 the game has laid them as five grey squashed spheres, which its
own comment calls a stand-in. The streak is still owed and waits on the sky's file (`89-world-land.js`, card F3's).

**What was built** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/props.js`, `oysterBed`; the page `rare.html?view=oyster`). The piece follows the game's own
numbers: five oysters within 2.4 m of the bed's middle (`PEARL.ring`), each opened where it lies, with the bed's record a bit per oyster.
- **The bank.** A low mound of shell grit and dead valves, 0.22 m high and 4.8 m across where it stands 2 cm proud. Its pale grit fades
  into the sand by 7 m. A swimmer on the surface 8 m above sees this pale patch on the darker floor before the oysters themselves, so it
  is what you dive for (`&above`).
- **The rubble.** Four flat stones half in the grit, one by each of the first four oysters, which grow on them.
- **The oysters.** Pearl oysters of 0.24 to 0.30 m. Each has two rounded valves on a straight hinge with small ears; the lower valve is
  the flatter. The outside is olive-brown with pale rays out from the hinge, growth bands and a paler last band at the frilled edge. This
  is a 128 × 64 texture of its own, laid round the valve (`shellTex`). The oysters lie hinge-down, 20 to 40° off flat. One in two closed
  ones carries a few blades of weed.
- **Opened** (`o.taken`, the game's bits). The upper valve is thrown back about 100 to 120° on its hinge, so both insides show silver
  nacre in a dark lip, empty. The game hides a taken shell today. With the piece, an opened shell stays where it was, open, until the bed
  comes back.
- **Round the bank.** Seven tufts of ribbon weed and two or three small sea fans, each a stem forking three times in one plane, so the
  bed reads as a living place and not a heap. There are 26 dead valves on the bank, about a third of them nacre up.
- **The numbers.** 7,600 triangles in 8 draws with every oyster shut, 9,100 in 9 with two open (the lip is the ninth draw). The dead
  valves are the coarse version of the same valve, 60 triangles each; the first try drew them at 480 each, and they were half the piece.

Pictures, the first try to the last: the first had the oysters half-buried in stones 0.5 m wide, the rock texture's marble veins on the
grit, and a flat orange fan that read as a leaf. The oysters stood 30 to 60° off upright, so an opened one showed its outside. Now the
stones are 0.3 to 0.5 m wide and flat, the grit and the rubble use the plaster's grain, and the oysters lie nearer flat.
