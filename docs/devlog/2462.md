  halfway between the neck and the shoulder joint, across the chest, and over the belt to the left hip. From there it goes round the flank
  and back up the back.
- **How it is laid.** The strap is where one plane cuts the body. The plane holds the body's depth axis and the line from the right
  shoulder's top to the left flank 4 cm under the belt's middle.
  - The surface is taken from the body and what it wears, not the arms, the neck or the head. Nothing is taken under 8 cm below the belt's
    middle, where a tunic's skirt is not the body.
  - In each of 128 turns round the section's middle, the strap takes the outermost of those points, widens it over the two turns either
    side, smooths it and pads it 3 mm.
  - Two first tries failed. A plane through the scabbard's own axis was too steep to leave the body at the flank, so its turn cut through
    the chest. A plane aimed at the flank over the belt came out under the right arm, not over the shoulder.
- **It is skinned to the body.** Each point takes the weights of the six body or garment points nearest it at the bind. The cloak is
  left out because it rides the pelvis. So the strap bends with the chest and the shoulder in the walk. It is detached with an identity
  bind, as `hbAttach` leaves every skinned piece. The first try used three's default `attached` mode, which re-derives the bind from the
  mesh's world matrix, and it drew nothing where it should.
- **The scabbard lies on the strap.** For a claymore or a longsword on the back, `tpSheath` now sets the blade's axis in the strap's plane
  rather than at S2451's fixed 30°. On the test body that is 29.7°, so the look barely moves.
  - The leather lies on the strap's hindmost point (or on a cloak or a pack frame, if that stands further back), not 1.2 cm off the back.
  - On the back the strap is the scabbard's bed and hardly shows.
  - A third try aimed the strap at the flank 7 cm over the belt: at 40°, the claymore's point stood half a metre out past the left hip.
- **Cleanup.** The strap's geometry is its own. `tpSheathDrop` disposes it with the sheathed group, both when the body is carried over and
  in `tpDispose`. Spears, staves and the great axe on the back have no strap and are placed as before.
