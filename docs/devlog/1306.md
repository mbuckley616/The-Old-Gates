## v80 — Session 1306 — the hands are lit by the place they are in
The critic's s827 line in backlog I: the first-person hands had their own fixed lights in `VM_SCENE` (`js/10-player.js`: an ambient
0.8 and a warm sun 1.2), so they measured 179–188 of 255 whether the frame round them averaged 117 (Clairancy at noon) or 4 (the
medium gate's ring with no lamp). In F5's dark gates they glowed like a lamp.

`vmLight(scene, dt)` (`js/16-viewmodel.js`) runs just before the view model is drawn (`js/90-main.js`). It reads the lights of the
scene drawn under it, which are all direct children of it (every point light is mapped onto the scene's pool, `sweepLights` and
`_sweepScenePool`), and gives the view model the same lights as they fall at the eye:
- the ambient lights summed, into `VM_SCENE`'s ambient;
- the hemisphere lights summed, into a new `VM_HEMI`, with the world's up turned into the camera's frame (the view model is drawn
  in that frame);
- the directional lights as one sun (`VM_SUN`), its colour their sum, from their direction weighted by intensity;
- the pool's point lights by the legacy falloff `05-compat.js` puts on both looks, (1 − d/distance)^decay, as one more directional
  light (`VM_PT`) from their weighted direction. So a torch on the wall lights the hand on its side.

The values ease over about a tenth of a second, so a pool light handed to another source does not blink, and a new scene is taken
at once. The view model's own glows (the torch's, an enchantment's) are its own children and stay as they were. Holding a torch
still lights the hands, since its world light is in the pool at the eye. Two lights more in `VM_SCENE` are added at load, before
any hand's material is made, so nothing compiles twice.
