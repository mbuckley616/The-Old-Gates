## v80 — Session 1097 — One piece in three conditions: the gate kit (backlog L10, prototype only)
Every F6 town building now waits on a decision (#408, #410, #411, #416), and the Makers' trials' props are done (Sessions 837 to 849). The next item on the look builder's list is L10's one-piece-in-three-conditions kit (OG-126, KEEP). It has two halves, the pieces and the six layouts. Its rule: the same pieces in each school's dress, and each piece in three conditions, whole, then cracked and leaning, then broken and floating with violet light in the seams. The row's note keeps the rule and the archetypes but not its small flags and corridor rooms. A gate's floors are slabs of 2 to 3 m and its spaces are §2's.

This session draws the pieces and the rule in a module of their own, `docs/prototypes/levelup/gatekit.js`, with its page (`gatekit.html`, `gatekit-page.js`) and its check.
- **A piece is a list of its stones.** Each stone is a block carrying its own moulding and script. A condition is one rule that moves the stones, never a second model, so a generator can lay any piece in any condition.
- **Seeded by place:** everything that varies comes from a stream keyed by the piece's key and its condition. The same piece breaks the same way on every visit, and two places break two ways (the co-op rule on outcomes keyed by place).
- **The eight pieces, at the nave's measures:**
  - **slab:** a floor of 2.5 m slabs, 0.6 m deep.
  - **wall:** an aisle wall 14 m by 12 m, 2 m thick, in courses of 1.25 m with staggered joints, a coping and two script strips.
  - **arch:** a two-centred pointed arch of 10 m on jambs of 2.2 m, 18 m to its point. Its voussoirs are cut two segments at a time, with a roll moulding on the inner edge and script at the key.
  - **pier:** 4 m square and 20 m high in courses of 1.6 m, with script up all four faces, a ring sigil at the seventh course, and a base and cap.
  - **stair:** a flight 8 m wide, 12 rises of 0.35 m between stepped cheeks.
  - **bridge:** a span of 16 m on one pointed ring, spandrels coursed to a deck of eight slabs, and a parapet.
  - **balcony:** a gallery of 9 by 2.2 m at 6 m on six tapered corbels, with a balustered rail, against its wall.
  - **panel:** a script panel of 3 by 6 m in a frame, three strips of glyphs and a ring sigil.
- **The conditions:**
  - **whole:** nothing moves, and the script burns in the school's light.
  - **cracked:** a standing piece leans 1.5 to 3° on its foot, toward one side or the other. Floors and spans do not lean. A stone in four slips up to 8 cm and turns up to 1°. Dark zigzag cracks run up three faces in ten, the script dims, and a strip in three goes dark.
  - **broken:** past a ragged line (42 to 54% of a standing piece's height, or half along a floor or a span), stones come loose:
    - **Floating:** seven in ten float, lifted 0.5 to 3.7 m (higher the higher they sat), turned and drifted.
    - **Fallen:** the rest lie fallen at the foot, on their sides, 2 to 5 m out. A floor's loose stone drops away into the dark below it.
    - **The seams:** a violet seam burns on every seat a stone has left and under every stone that floats, and the script on what remains turns violet. Fallen script is dead stone.
- **Dressed by school:** `GK_SCHOOLS` takes the stone and the script's light from `gates.js`'s six schools.
- **The page:**
  - `?view=kit`: the eight pieces in rows, the three conditions left to right.
  - `?view=piece&piece=…`: one piece three ways from the eye.
  - `?view=hall`: a nave 26 m wide of six bays. Its piers, arcades and floor are whole in the first two bays, cracked in the next two and broken in the last two, with a broken stair and panel at the end.
  - The scale figures are plain 1.8 m shapes: the people's pack is not in the checkout.

### Needs eyes
- **The question:** what a broken gate's floating stones do in the game is asked as DECISION #417. The recommendation is A, hanging still and solid: they cost nothing at run time, and a broken hall becomes a place to climb.
- **The seams** are a thin plate at each seat. Where a whole course has lifted, they read as bright bars between the courses (the wall's broken top). A seam drawn only along the break's edge might read better.
- **Floors:** the floor's slabs are boxes on a plane, so the nave's first bay shows a 0.6 m step where the slab pieces begin.
