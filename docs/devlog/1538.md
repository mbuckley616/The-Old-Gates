## v80 — Session 1538 — Card F11: your sloop wears the dress of the yard that sold or refitted her (#839 A)

Michael answered DECISION #839 with A: *the yard's island, kept on the ship*. Session 1531 brought the kit's Gatelands sloop into the
game, so until now every sloop of yours was the Gatelands' wherever she was bought. The prototype (`docs/prototypes/levelup/ships.js`,
Sessions 900 and 902; OG-004, OG-028) already had the sloop in all three dresses, and this session brings the other two into the kit.

`PKIT.ships.sloop(o)` (`js/38-kit.js`) takes `o.dress`. It is still one hull with the same lines, clinker strakes, deck, rail, hatch and
tiller; only the colours and the rig change:
- **The Mark's** (`markSloop`; Michael's A on #312 and #323, #306's one rig to an island). The hull is tarred black above a red-brown
  bottom with a gilt wale, a gilt beast's head on the stem head, and twelve painted round shields hung outboard on the rail, clear of the
  shrouds' feet. The rig is the cutter's gaff main with a topsail on a topmast, and a staysail and a jib on a bowsprit, all in tanned
  sails. The boom is 2.15 m over the deck, so you can walk under it.
- **Aurenne's** (`aurenneSloop`; #316 A, the sun-star). The hull is tallowed white below the water and oiled above, with the sheer
  strake blue under a gilt wale. The mast is raked 4° forward and carries one lateen, its yard slung to starboard, white with the
  sun-star in gold.

The game trims every rig to the wind about its own frame (`shipTrim`), so each dress gives its rigs as a list (`rigs`, each with its
type). The Gatelands' yard and sail are type `square`, braced about the mast as before. The Mark's boom, gaff, main and topsail are
type `gaff` and swing to leeward about the mast. Its staysail and jib are a rig of their own in the hull's frame, type `head`: `shipTrim`
does not turn a type it doesn't know, and it mirrors a sail that isn't square, so they belly to leeward and stay on their stays.
Aurenne's yard and lateen are type `lateen`, swung about the sling (where the raked mast is at the yard's height), not the mast's foot.
As in Session 1531, the running rigging is left off: braces, sheets and the lateen's tack line would hang loose once the rig turns.
The bake now merges geometry by material, not by key, so where two keys share a material (the Mark's sheer strake is its tar, its wale
its gilt) they share a draw.

In the game (`85-world-sea.js`), the dress is kept on the ship as `worldState.ship.dress` (`'gatelands'`, `'mark'` or `'aurenne'`).
`buyShip` writes it from the yard's island (`nationAt` of the site), `grantShip` from the Compact's port, and a yard's refit
(`yardOffers`, `86-world-crime.js`) writes that yard's island before `applyShipClass` rebuilds her. The Compact's free refit, where
she lies, leaves her dress alone: no yard is involved. A ship from a save made before this has no dress and is the Gatelands', as she
was built. `shipBake` keys the kit's bake by dress (`sloop|player|mark`), and the mesh's `bakeKey` carries it, so the stowed cargo's
slots (`shipStowSlots`, which reads only the key's first part) find their bake. `worldState.ship` is already saved whole, so the dress
needs no new key in the save. Only the sloop is the kit's so far: a cog or galleon of yours keeps her dress in the save, but is drawn
as the old cog or galleon until those hulls come in.
