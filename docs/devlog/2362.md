**Why the mail was black.** Every room's `mail` was a plain grey (0x5a5c60) at metalness 0.7. The rooms have no environment map, so
a metal surface has nothing to reflect, and at 0.7 seven tenths of its light is gone: away from the window it drew near black at any hour.

**What changed** (`js/38-kit.js` and the prototype's `docs/prototypes/levelup/rooms.js`, the two copies kept the same):
- **A ringed surface.** A new painter, `PAINTERS.mail` (rooms.js: `mailMat()` over common.js's `texPair`): rows of rings, each row offset
  half a ring, painted as a height field with the normal map from it, bright on the ring and dark in the gaps. 8 rings to a 128-pixel tile,
  repeated 14 times, so a ring is about 1.5 cm at the rooms' 0.6 a metre (a first try at 2.6 cm read as chicken wire). `mailMat()` is
  that surface at 0x9ea2a9, roughness 0.42, metalness 0.35. Every room that draws mail takes it: the Gatelands merchant's house
  (`townhouseRoom`), the Mark's trader's hall (`markTrader`), the Mark's town-house shops, and the guild forge's armour stands
  (whose mail was already at 0.35). One material swapped for another, so no room gains a draw.
- **The window's helms.** In the merchant's house the two helms on the show board stood on a 4 cm post. Each now sits on a turned
  head-block (a 0.37 m oak turning, 0.17 m across at its foot), under a mail aventail that hangs 0.2 m from the brow band and flares
  to the block's shoulder (`helmStand`). The helm's height is the same, and its face still turns to the street. About 110 triangles more
  in the armourer's room, in its existing oak and mail draws.
