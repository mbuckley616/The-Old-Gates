the garrison's soldiers (`k` 1 and 2) walk a beat from the plaza to the town's first gate (`S._gates[0]`) and back, a leg a game-hour. They

**What changed** (`js/87-world-quests.js`, one hook in `js/42-zone-enemies.js`):
- `tickSieges` marks soldiers 1 and 2 of an occupied town's garrison `_beat`. Each tick, `patrolTick` reads `hourNow()`. It walks out on the
  even hours from 6 and back on the odd ones (`patrolLeg`). From 20h to 6h the target is the plaza. When the leg changes, it moves the
  soldier's home and lays its way.
- **The way is the watch's street grid** (`townRoute`, S166/S378), not the straight line. Dunmore's gate is 86 m from the plaza across the
  houses, and a straight wander would stop at the first wall. `patrolStep` walks that way while the soldier is not alert. It is called
  first in `tickZoneEnemies`' wander branch. A soldier who turns on you drops its way, and finds it again from where it stands once it
  gives up. A cell gone solid under its feet does the same.
- **The pace.** At its own 1.2 m/s, a soldier covered 72 of Dunmore's ~100 m of street in the game-hour's 60 real seconds. The first run
  left both 14 m short of the gate when the leg turned. A leg is now paced to end in 50 s: the way's length ÷ 50, never under the soldier's
  own pace and never over 2.5 m/s (`PATROL.legS`, `PATROL.pace`). Dunmore's is about 2 m/s, a brisk walk. A town larger than 125 m by
  street would turn its patrol back short of the gate.
- **A town with no wall** has no gate. There the beat's end is where its first road leaves the pad, at pad − 5 (the wall ring's radius).
  That reading is mine, not the draft's.
