## v80 — Session 1196 — the middle level as one mesh, and a faster build (card F4, part two)
Session 1195 left Dunmore at 982 of the 1,000 draw-call budget with six builder bodies near the square, and every body in the
middle range (16 to 42 m) still drew all its garments: 14 to 27 draw calls each, less only its hair cards and eyes. This session
makes that level one draw call.

**The bake** (`hbMerge`, `32-people.js`). When a body is attached, its solid parts are copied into one skinned mesh on the same
163-bone skeleton: the skin (with its own vertex colours), every garment in its material's colour, and what hangs on a bone (the
basket in the hand, a guard's helm and spear), put into the bind pose through that bone and weighted wholly to it; a part on the
body's root (the cloak, the brooch) goes to the root bone. The cards (hair, brows, beard, moustache) and the eyes are left out; the
scalp shell and the beard's base stand in for them. One shared vertex-coloured material, so no new shader programs. The bake is
41,600 triangles for the largest body against 273,000 whole (its loose hair is 220,000 of those), and takes a few tens of
milliseconds at attach. At level 1 the whole body is hidden and the bake shown; at level 0 the other way round.

The garments' hems and openings are cut in their shaders (`people.js`'s `CUT` and `hole`, over the rest position `aBase`), which a
bake cannot carry. So the builder's cloth, shell and mail materials now keep their cut and hole on `userData` (three lines in
`people.js`; nothing they draw changes), and the bake drops each triangle whose middle the cut or hole would discard: the cut as
`people.js` writes it, the hole translated from GLSL to a JS test (`hbCutFn`, its noise term at its middle; a hole that will not
translate, or throws, is skipped and its triangles kept). The cloak is the one that still shows wrong: its open front is not one
of those cuts, so at the middle level a cloaked person carries a panel of the cloak's colour down the front (`f4-baked.jpg`).

**The build, a little faster.** `surfGrid` and the hair's seam check keyed their grids by strings (`i + ',' + j + ',' + k`); they
are numbers now, `(i·4096 + j)·4096 + k`, the same cells in the same order. The same six genomes built by the old and new copies
of the builder hash the same to the vertex (6 of 6), and a body takes 973 ms against 1,093 on this CPU: 11%, not the third the
profile suggested. The rest of the time is spread through the hair's and cloak's fitting; the next real saving is building a
town's bodies while its cells load.
