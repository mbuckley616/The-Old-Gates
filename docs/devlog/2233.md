**What was wrong.** The yard's search (`townGardens` and `townRacks` in `js/82-world-structures.js`) keeps a garden or rack off the town's
solids, off every door, out of the sea and 3.5 m from a road. The only way it knows a road is `roadInfo`, and that covers the world's
roads only. A town's own walks are kept in `S.paths` and drawn as flat ribbons on the town's ground: its streets (2.4 m wide), the ring lane (2.2 m) and every door's footpath (1.3 m). The search never looked at
them. In Dunmore the critic's case was only the one that got seen. A check of my own fitted a garden to every home in three towns, one at
a time, as the page does. Before this change, 20 of Dunmore's 44 gardens had a bed on a walk, as did 17 of Portclare's 24 and 2 of
Hearthwick's 7. No rack was on a walk, but Portclare's drying rack at `g_portclare_1` stood 0.1 m from a walk's edge.

**What changed.**
- `js/82-world-structures.js`: **`yardWalks(site, walks)`** turns the town's walks into segments, each with a bounding box, and its
  `near(x, z, m)` says whether a point is within m of a walk's edge. Both searches take an optional fifth argument, `walks`. Every sample
  point of the garden or rack must be 0.3 m clear of every walk. The sample grids are about 0.55 m apart, so even the narrowest walk (1.3 m)
  cannot slip between two samples. Nothing else in the search changed: the same spots, tried in the same order.
- `js/83-world-generator.js`: while a town builds, it passes `S.paths` itself, because `SETTLE` does not hold the town yet. A garden or rack
  fitted later from the page (`homeYardRacks`, 84) reads the walks from `SETTLE`.
