**What was wrong.** Every range in a damage roll was half-open: `lo + floor(r × (hi − lo))` gives `lo` to `hi − 1`. A Bronze Sword whose
sheet says *ATK 8–9* dealt 8 on every swing; a bow's 6–10 never rolled 10; the item card's deltas compared tops no blow could reach. Fists
said 2–4 and rolled 2 or 3; Stone hand's [4,8] rolled 4 to 7, which is what its perk text and `perkarms` already said. Separately, the
sheet's attack readout (`#sa`) still showed `lo + level × 1.5` to `hi + level × 1.5`, the flat level term S941 took out of the damage:
a new character's fists read *3–5* and punched for 1.

**What changed.**
- `rollRange(lo, hi, r)` (`js/14-items.js`): `lo + floor(r × (hi − lo + 1))`, both ends. The swing (`meleeDamageChain`, `74`), the bow's
  release roll and an arrow's at a keyed foe (`fireArrow`, `arrowRawFor`, `62`) use it. Each draws from the same stream as before.
- **Fists and Stone hand keep the fight they were tuned to.** At both ends Fists' 2–4 would have been ×1.2 on the mean, and the critic's
  brawl in `fistfight` went from 6–11 punches to 5–6. A rule of play like that is not a bug fix's to change. So their sheets are written as
  they rolled: `FISTS.atk` [2,3] and `STONE_HAND_ATK` [4,7]. A punch is the same as before.
- **A weapon gains its top value.** That is half a point on the mean of every weapon roll: +6% on an 8–9, +2% on a 20–30. It is what the
  sheets have always shown, so I took it as the sheet's rule, not a new one. If the fights feel easier, cut the tables' tops by one.
- `atkShown()` (`62`): the sheet's attack is the range × its skill × Might, floored as the swing floors it. Before the buffs, the blow's
  row and armour. A new character's fists read *1–1* (2–3 × 0.64 at Hand-to-hand 5).
