## v80 — Session 1327 — a gate is steady, failing or broken: gone spans, floating stones and a pit that kills (card F5, the build sheet's session 4, part 1)
F5's build sheet (`docs/design/gate-interiors-build.md`, "Condition") hands its session 4 to the systems builder: condition, the failing
and broken ways, the floating stones as footholds, the minimap's outlines, and `gatesave`. The sheet's one open number, how often a gate
fails, has Michael's answer: **A, 50 / 35 / 15** (20 Oct 2026, `docs/decisions.md`). This session builds the condition and the ways.
The minimap's outlines and `gatesave` come next. The budget half (`gatedraws`) was already done in Session 1302.

**The roll** (`gateCondRoll`, `js/52-dungeon-gen.js`). A gate's condition comes from its seed through its own hash and is never saved.
Half of gates are steady, 35% failing and 15% catastrophic. An authored gate may name its own: a `cond` on its `WORLD_DUNGEONS` entry,
which `buildDungeon` passes to `makeGate`. None names one yet. The main quest's gates roll as they fall: the Dungeon of Shadows (42) is
failing, the Crypt of Embers (137) steady, and the Vault of the Tide (845) catastrophic. `makeGate(size, seed, floors, cond)` takes
a forced condition for tests. The condition pass (`gateCondition`) runs after the layout on its own stream from the seed, so no draw
of the layout moves: a steady gate is the same gate it was, cell for cell.

**What it does to the way.**
- **A failing gate** loses one span in three (rounded, at least one) of each chasm's bridge. A span is the run of 2-cell bridge
  between two piers' landings. Its naves' floors have gaps: pits of 1 or 2 cells by 1 or 2, one for every 60 m of nave (2 to 4).
  A gap is kept off the piers, 3 cells from a threshold and 4 from another gap. It stays only if everything you could reach before
  is still reachable, so the way goes round it (the sheet's "another way round always exists"). A gate of two floors keeps floor
  one's naves whole: a pit there would fall into floor two's rock, and the stair hall reads which floor you are on by your height.
- **A catastrophic gate** loses every span and has twice the gaps. Until the bodies builder makes the schools' catastrophic forms, its
  ring chamber is guarded one tier up (the sheet). In a small broken gate, a ring Golem has 71 health against the 41 outside the ring.
- **A gone span** is crossed by the floating stones (#417 A: they hang still, and you can stand on them). The stones lie along the
  bridge's line, each 2 m along it and 2.2 m across, with equal gaps between them and at either end. The stones are as many as keep
  each gap no wider than 1.6 m; a 15 m span takes four at 1.4 m. They hang at the deck's height, give or take 0.15–0.25 m, and
  zigzag a hand's breadth off the line. Each is a foothold keyed by place, `<seed>:<floor>:stone<n>` (the co-op rule), and a stone
  box in the spaces' one merged mesh, so they add no draw call.

**The chasm is now open air to you** (`dSlideP`, `dPitBase`, `js/56-dungeon-build.js`). Since Session 1293 a chasm's open cells (map 8)
were solid to everyone, so its edge acted as a wall. Now your own step (the loop's `dSlideP`) lets you over them. Over the air with no
stone under you, the ground is the pit's floor, 25 m down. You fall, and the fall's blow (`FALL_PER`, Session 429) kills even with
Cat's fall: 1.26 of your health, or 1.02 at 8 m free. Once you are below the floor over the air, the pit's sides are walls, so you
cannot step out through the rock onto the ledge. This holds in a steady gate's chasm too: the sheet's "a fall past 21 m kills, so the
floor is never reached alive" means a pit you can fall into. A foe's `dSlide`, its `bfs`, its sight and a shove from a body
(`pushFromBodies`) still treat the air as a wall. So a foe never walks off a ledge and is never pushed off one, and it cannot follow
you over the stones. Its leash (Session 1323) walks it home.
