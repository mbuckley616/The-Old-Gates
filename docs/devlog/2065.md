**What changed.**
- `index.html`: a canvas (`#eq-fig`) in the Inventory's `#eq-grid`. On the new look the wrap takes the class `fig`, and the grid is laid by
  areas, 52 + 200 + 52 px. Head, back, chest, hands, legs and feet stand on the left. Amulet, weapon, off-hand, ring and ammo stand on the
  right. The window is 200 × 332 between them, with the prototype's arch, gradient and bronze rings. Every slot keeps its id and its click.
  On the old look the canvas is `display:none` and the 3 × 6 grid is as it was (A, as the brief says).
- `js/66-hub.js`, `sheetFig*`, called from `renderHubInv`:
  - **No second body.** The body is the third-person rig's own (`tpEnsure`: your look's genome, dressed from `EQ` by `tpHBDress`). It is
    lent to the portrait's scene for the draw and given back to its parent, place, turn, visibility and level of detail in the same call
    (`sheetFigLend`/`sheetFigGiveBack`). It is posed by `pwIdle` at rest through `tpHBPose`. A held weapon's wrist is turned 1.5 rad, so the
    blade or the club hangs by the thigh. At 0 it pointed at you.
  - **No second WebGL context.** The game's renderer draws into a half-float target (4× MSAA), the lights unclamped. One quad then
    tone-maps it with three's ACES fit into a byte target, which is read back onto the canvas with straight alpha, so the floor's shadow
    lies on the window's gradient. The body's geometry and textures are shared, and the world's frame and its tone mapping are untouched.
  - **The lights** are the prototype's, divided by π for the game's legacy light scale (`05-compat.js`): key 1.05, hemisphere .2, rim .8,
    back .26.
  - **When it draws:** when the panel opens, when what you wear changes (`tpSig`), and on a drag (0.012 rad a pixel). It draws once a frame
    at most, and only while the hub is open.
  - The first draw of a material it has not drawn before compiles first (`compileAsync`, with the target set), which in a browser with
    `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` is off the main thread.

### Needs eyes
- **On software GL the first draw holds the page about 17–48 s.** That is SwiftShader compiling the body's programs for this light, and it
  has no parallel compile. Every later draw waits about 3 s on the readback, because the world keeps rendering behind the hub every frame

  not measured. A suite that opens the Inventory and keeps it open past a frame pays the first cost once a boot. `backslot` does not.
