## v80 — Session 1397 — the journal's *Great gates* page
*Numbered 1397: the look builder's Sessions 1394–1396 took the numbers on main while this ran.*

The great gates' build sheet (`docs/design/great-gates-build.md`, "What it touches", the UI) lists the journal's *Great gates* page,
six rows and the lock line, and backlog A carried it as owed after Sessions 1346–1367 built the rules. Every line on it is already
written and approved in the drafts: piece I's rows (*${gate} — ${School}. Torn.* and *Sealed.*) and the lock's line under the six
rows (*Bound, all six, and held.*), and piece II's journal lines (the note's *A note says something lies under a flagstone at the
Ashfeld.*, *Aldwyn asks to see you at Ironhaven.*, the knife's and the needle's). The game wrote the log's lines but had no page.

`greatJournal()` (`87-world-quests.js`) builds the page from what is already saved, and stores nothing: the tools' lines from
`worldState.greatGates.ev` (`noteRead`, `runner`, `knifeLifted`, `needleGiven`, each a time), in the order they happened; then one row
for each gate torn or sealed, in the schools' fixed order; then the lock's line once the fate is *sealed*. The Journal's Quests view
shows it as a block headed *Great gates*, under *Things not yet yours* (`66-hub.js`), the tools' lines dated like the quests' lines.
A world where nothing has happened has no page.

A great gate's name is known only at its knot (the gates are stand-ins by seed until the look places them), so `cutKnot` and
`bindKnot` now take the gate's name as a second argument and keep it in `greatGates.names` (`greatGateName`); the knot's three
callers pass it. Nothing else changes in them, and a call with no name (the console, the suites) keeps none. A gate cut or bound
before this session has no name kept: its row reads its school alone (*Uisce. Torn.*). A name that starts in lower case (*the great
gate*, the knot's fallback) is capitalised at the head of the row. The draft's straight apostrophes are the game's ’, as in every
line built from these drafts.
