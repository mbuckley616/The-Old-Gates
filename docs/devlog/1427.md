## v80 — Session 1427 — the Gatelands townhouse's room on 3, 4 and 5 bays (prototype, card F9)
Section I held nothing for the look builder. The backlog's L9 line on OG-115's homes still owes "the townhouse's and the farmhouse's
rooms in the game". The townhouse is the Gatelands home the game builds most: a survey of every Gatelands town, port and city on the new
look counted 2,426 kit townhouses. Its room was drawn in Session 1189 (`gatelandsTownhouse`, `rooms.js`; Michael's A on #303) for the
house as first planned: 6.4 by 9 m, its door at the east end. The towns build it by bays (#396 C) and grow it back to 8 m where the row
behind allows (#408 B). So the street holds townhouses of 3, 4 and 5 bays (6.45, 8.4 and 10.35 m wide), 4 to 8 m deep, with the door at
either end. Of the 2,426, 1,828 are 8 m deep and 2,328 (96%) are 3 to 5 bays and 6.75 m or deeper. Seven have 2 bays, and 91 are
shallower than 6.75 m.

As Session 1380 did for the cottage, this session makes the room fit those houses in the prototype before the game takes it. Nothing new
is drawn, so there is no question.

**A fault in the street's piece, fixed first.** `glBayPlan`'s townhouse put the back door at the back of the door's bay. That is the
hall's bay, where the stair's half-landing stands 1.5 m up against the back wall. The drawn plan has it in the kitchen. The bay plan now
puts it 0.35 m in from the far bay's middle, which is the drawn 1.6 m on three bays. The ground floor's back window moves to the middle
of the kitchen, between that door and the partition. Two bays keep the old back, because there the door and the window would overlap; those seven houses keep the old room anyway. `pieces.js` only; the game's copy in `js/38-kit.js` follows when the room comes in.

**`gatelandsTownhouse({ bays, d })`.** The plan is `glBayPlan('townhouse', bays, { doorX: 1, d })`, door east; a west-door house is this
one mirrored. Every piece is placed from the west gable, the partition (1.15 m west of the door's middle), the front and back walls and
the stair:
- **The stair** keeps the drawn well. The half-landing is 1.15 m deep on the back wall, with 7 treads of 0.27 m to the well's front at
  3.04 m from the back.
- **The cross wall** between the front and back rooms stands 4.4 of 8.1 m from the back, as drawn.
- **Upstairs, the landing in front of the well needs 1.36 m.** In a house shallower than the drawn 9 m, the hall's bay keeps its cross
  wall there. The partition stops at it, and the front room turns round the partition's end into the bay over the front door.
- **The back room's door off the hall** stands in front of the well where it fits. Otherwise it stands under flight B's top, set back
  until there is 2.0 m of headroom over it.
- **Upstairs, a door off the landing would open over the well** in every house shallower than 9 m. There the back rooms are reached
  through the front ones: the drawn double doors on the first floor, and a new door beside the bed on the second.
- **The range** stays 2.05 m off the back wall, but stops short of the dresser in a shallow kitchen.
- **The dining table** shortens to fit the room (1.13 m at 6.75 m deep).
- **The bunks** stand clear of the stack's breast in a house under 7.6 m deep.
- **The settee** stays only where the front room is 3.1 m or deeper (7.8 m houses and up). In a shallower house it would close the way
  to the double doors.

**Four and five bays widen the west rooms** by 1.95 or 3.9 m:
- **The parlour:** a bookcase on the cross wall, and on five bays a china press as well.
- **The kitchen:** a meal chest on the back wall, and on five bays a settle as well.
- **The drawing room:** the settee moves over to the partition, with a bookcase beside it.
- **The bedroom:** a clothes press.

Without `bays` the house is the drawn one. The drawn room changes in four small ways:
- The hall table and its glass sit between the two doors (0.4 m nearer the front).
- The drawing room's settee stands west of the partition instead of across its end, 1.15 m long instead of 1.55.
- Its round table moves from the window to the fire, between the chairs.
- The seven walks are laid from the same anchors.

The room also returns `part.h`, `mid`, `upOK`, `kdoor`, `settee` and `bays`, which the game's copy will read. `rooms.html?room=townhouse&bays=3|4|5&d=…`
places its views from what the room returns.
