## v80 — Session 1377 — Cloch's fallen blocks in the Mark's lesser gates

The last of the toll pieces that falls to the look builder (great-gates-build.md §4, the approved draft's piece III: *in the Mark's
lesser gates, a fallen block closes one passage on each floor (the dungeon generator's blocked door, the way round longer)*). The
systems builder built Cloch's tremor in Session 1370 and left this here, because it lives in the gate's generator and builder.

**What a passage is.** S1370 noted that no blocked door existed in the generator to reuse. A probe over the 100 gates `gatespaces`
walks (20 seeds of each size) found that closing any one corridor cell keeps the floor connected on only 2 of 120 floors. A gate is
a tree: its side rooms hang off one corridor each, and its spaces join by single thresholds. So no way round was there to be found,
and one is cut with the block.

**The rule** (`gateBlock(gen, seed)`, `52-dungeon-gen.js`, pure). On each floor it takes one passage: a straight corridor cell outside
every space, with corridor either side, 2 or more cells from a threshold or a door, 4 from the way in and 3 from any room. Candidates
are shuffled on the gate's own stream (`seed + 0xb10c`), so a gate falls the same way every visit while the toll holds. Beside the
block, a U of five cells is cut through the rock on one side, only where it and every cell beside it are rock, so it opens into
nothing but the passage. The block's cell turns to rock (0) and the U to floor (1); `gen.blocks` keeps `{floor, c, r, ew, side, way}`.
The walk from one side of the block to the other goes from 2 steps to 6 (6 m to 18 m). 98 of the 120 floors find one; the rest
have no corridor that long and stay as they are.

**When** (`clochBlockDue(portal)`, `56-dungeon-build.js`). For a lesser gate (not a great one, not the tutorial) whose door stands on
the Mark (`nationAt` of the door), while Cloch's toll holds (`gateToll('cloch')`: Cloch torn, three torn in all). It is read on
entering, so a gate you are in keeps its passages until you next come in. `buildDungeon` calls `gateBlock` right after the gate is
laid.

**Saves.** A foe's id is its index in the floor's shuffled list of floor cells, and a chest's comes from the rooms. The foes' list is
built as if nothing had fallen: the way round is left out, and the block's cell stays in, its foe set in the passage beside it. With
the toll on, every foe and every chest of a gate stands where it stood without it, so a save made before the toll still finds them by
their `<seed>:<floor>:<index>` ids.

**The look** (`gateFallBuild(gen)`, after `dunScaleWhole`, in metres). The shell walls the block's cell off from both sides. Against
each face leans a block fallen from the vault: 2.6 by 1.8 by 0.9 m, tipped back 17° onto the face, filling most of the 3 m passage.
A broken course stone (1.3 m) lies beyond it, and eleven rubble stones of 0.15 to 0.45 m spill 0.8 to 2.2 m out. That is one mesh
per block, both faces, 1,824 tris in one draw. The first cut went into the floor's props bake and vanished: the scaling pass moves a
bake's touching pieces by their cluster's middle, and a piece this large joined the wall-foot rubble and was carried off. So it is
drawn after the scaling instead, as the knot and the Shuttle are laid.

**Found.** `makeGate('small', 57)` lays a different map on its second call in a page than on its first. The generator's 100 gates
otherwise come out the same twice. This predates the session; `gatespaces` compares `gateBlock` on two copies of one map instead.
