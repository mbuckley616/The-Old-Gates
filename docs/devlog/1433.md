## v80 — Session 1433 — the blizzard and the heavy rain (Michael's A on #723)
Section I held nothing for the systems builder, and quest review's run 28 found nothing. Michael answered #723 on 25 Oct with
**A, a fifth**: a fifth of a cold climate's winter snow becomes a blizzard, a fifth of autumn's rain becomes heavy rain, both out of
their own kind's share, so bad weather overall is as common as before. Session 1420 had built the sight figures for both (70 m and
280 m, Michael's C on #679), but the game never rolled either.

**The roll.** `heavyWx` (`87-world-quests.js`, beside `seasonWx`) is the last step of `weatherWeights`, after the biome's weights and
the great gates': in a cold climate's winter it moves a fifth of `w.snow` to `w.blizzard`, and in any autumn a fifth of `w.rain` to
`w.heavyrain`. Last, so a tundra's extra snow and a fen's extra rain are split too: the Mark's winter rolls a blizzard 7.5% of the
time (snow 30.1%), a tundra's 9.8%; the Gatelands' autumn rolls heavy rain 4.3% (rain 17.1%), a fen's 5.9%. Never a blizzard in the
Gatelands, in Aurenne or out of winter. The answer's figures name the Gatelands' autumn for heavy rain; its rule is "a fifth of
autumn's rain", so the Mark's autumn gets it too (1.2% of its rolls), and Aurenne's (2%). If Michael meant the Gatelands alone, it is
one condition on the second line.

**What they are.** A blizzard is snow and heavy rain is rain, marked heavy: `WX.type` and `WX.next` stay among the six kinds, and
`WX.heavy` (0 to 1) eases in and out over the same 25 s a change of weather takes. So everything that reads `'snow'` or `'rain'`
(the sky's cloud in both looks, the fur cloak's warmth, the lamp, the oilskin's sea, the townsfolk's weather line, the Noon Ring)
reads them as the kind they are, with no edit in the look's files. The roll's names (`blizzard`, `heavyrain`) are turned into a kind
and a mark by `wxSetNext`; `devWeather('blizzard')` works, and `devWeather()` names it. Heavy, the fog closes to the heavy row of
`WX_SIGHT` (blended in density, as every change is), the rain and snow particles are 1.6 times as many, a blizzard's snow falls
faster and is driven sideways (6 m/s more drift), the weather's sound is 1.6 times louder, and the sky dims a fifth more. The
oilskin's forecast says *a blizzard ahead* or *heavy rain ahead*.

A weather set by hand must not keep an old roll's mark, or a test's `g.spin('rain')` after a heavy rain was rolled at the start
(4% of autumn rolls) would read 280 m. So `WX.type` and `WX.next` are accessors on the `WX` object (`85-world-sea.js`, one line,
the look builder's file): setting `next` clears its heavy mark, and setting `type` snaps `WX.heavy` to the mark. The cell's reroll
(S450) also rolls again a heavy weather carried where it can't come.

Neither adds cold or wear (the climate rule is its own item), and the look of each is card F3's: on both looks today a blizzard is
denser, faster, sideways snow in a 70 m fog, and heavy rain denser rain in a 280 m fog.
