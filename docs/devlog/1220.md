OG-110's Aurenne woman is "in red and gold with a circlet". Session 1206 made the circlet. Session 1206's devlog listed the gown as owed:
red and gold with an embroidered border, the formal dress OG-109 gives Aurenne ("embroidered formal dress in saffron, red and teal"). The
kit could dress a woman in red, but its only border was the Mark's woven band (Session 1156). That band is a strip of another cloth with
lozenges, which is right for the Mark and wrong for a silk-and-linen gown. Michael kept OG-110 and asked for nothing different, so this
session asks nothing.

What changed, in `people.js`:
- **The embroidery (`EMB`, `embAt`).** It is a shader, worked on the gown's own cloth. Across the border (t, 0 to 1) it has a couched gold
  cord along each edge. Between the cords runs a vine, one wave every two border widths. Each wave has a ring with a knot in the bay
  under each crest and over each trough, and a leaf where the vine crosses the middle. The threads have a satin sheen across each stroke.
  The cloth darkens in a hair's width round each thread, so the threads read as lying proud. The threads also catch the light as metal:
  roughness goes to .42 and metalness to .45 under the thread only, through `embK` after the material's own roughness and metalness.
- **Where it lies (`EMB_NECK`, `EMB_SLEEVE`, `embHem`).** At the neck it starts at rest .578, is 2.8 cm deep and makes six waves round
  the neck's axis. At the sleeves' ends it is 3.6 cm, ending 4 mm short of the tunic's cut, round the wrist. That band has six waves,
  not the four the wrist's girth gives, because the sleeve hangs open wider than the wrist, and at four the rings came out three times
  the hem's size. At the hem it starts 2 cm up from the cut and is 5.5 cm deep. On a long gown the hem border is read off Session 1163's
  table of the cut's own heights, so it follows the ragged edge, with thirteen waves round. On a knee-length or short skirt it runs over
  their straight cut lines, with eleven and ten waves. Every band closes on a whole number of waves, so there is no seam.
- **`embOf`.** The flag is `D.gownBorder`, the thread's colour. It applies to the tunic (neck and sleeves) and the skirt. It is not
  applied under a winter coat or a robe, or where the Mark's woven bands are worn: the bands win.
- **The roll (`PEOPLES.aurennais.gown` .2).** One Aurennais woman in five who wears a long skirt and no apron wears the gown. The
  bodice and skirt are in one cloth: madder on half of them, saffron or teal on the rest. The thread is gold. On a saffron gown, two in
  five take madder thread instead, since gold on saffron barely shows. Cuffs of another cloth come off. The roll comes from its own
  stream (`seed|gown`), so every other roll of every seed is as it was.
- **The builder:** one colour row, *gown embroidery*.

Two problems showed in the pictures and were fixed:
- **A hair of gold traced the triangles round the border.** At a triangle's edge the derivatives `fwidth` reads can be far too large.
  When that happened, each stroke's smoothing widened until a thread a border-width away coloured the edge. A stroke's cover is now scaled
  down where a pixel spans many times its width (`min(1, 2.5 w / f)`), and the lines are gone (`builder-gown-hemclose.jpg` against
  `builder-gown-hemplain.jpg`).
- **The neck band stopped short at the sides.** The first cut copied the woven band's front-only limit (|x| < .1). The gown's band now
  goes all the way round on whole waves.

Cost: 0 triangles and 0 draws. The two garments the gown already had carry it in their shaders.
