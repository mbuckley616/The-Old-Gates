**The change** (`js/86-world-crime.js`, after the boarding nets; one hook each in 85's `otherPrompt`/`otherInteract` and 88's tick):
- **The throw** (`throwHooks`, the named action). You must be on your deck or at the helm, with her hull under the crosshair (her sides
  to a hand over the rail), and within 10 m of her deck from the eye. Her net under the crosshair still climbs it, as before, so the
  net comes first. You and she must be moving at under 3 m/s to each other: the size of the difference of the two ships' velocities,
  so sailing alongside her at her speed counts. Faster, and the prompt says *"Close with her more slowly to throw the hooks"*. At the
  helm, E throws instead of letting go of the wheel while she is aimed at. In a storm (sea state 3) the prompt says *"No hook will
  hold in this sea"* and E does nothing. In a rough sea (2), a throw misses one time in two, on `seededRng('hooks', <her id>:<throw>)`
  (co-op rule).
- **The haul.** 1.0 s after the throw the hooks bite or fall short. Then, over 4 s, she is drawn alongside: parallel to your hull,
  whichever way she faced (bow to bow or bow to stern), 0.6 m off your side, as far along as she was. Your way falls off to 0 over the
  same 4 s. The times are read from the clock (the dropped-frame rule), as the ladder's are.
- **Lashed.** She is held there and both ships are at 0 m/s. Your helm makes no way, and her own steering is overridden. Two lines run
  from two cleats on your rail to hers, drawn as plain lines that arc out on the throw. The look builder's session on the page gives
  them a rope.
- **Across** (`crossTarget`). Standing on your deck, with her hull under the crosshair within 3 m, E puts you at her near rail on her
  deck, which boards her (`boardOther`: her crew turn, her chest is rolled). From her deck, E with your hull under the crosshair puts
  you back. The rail (S1588) still holds a step across; the crossing is the way over.
- **Cutting** (`cutLine`, the named action). E within 1.3 m of a cleat sets your knife to its line. 1.5 s later it is cut if you have
  stayed by it; walk off and it stays (*"You leave the line uncut."*). With one cut, the other holds her. With both cut, she is free
  and sails on. A cleat stands 0.35 m in from the deck's edge at a quarter of her length fore and aft. The sloop's deck narrows there,
  so the cleat finds the edge first: the first try, at a fixed width, put both cleats off the planking.
- The lashing drops if she founders (*"The lines go with her."*), if your ship goes down, or if you leave the open world. Other ships
  are not saved, and neither is a lashing.

**Not in this slice:** her crew's own hooks and her hand cutting your lines in 6 s are the page's third session (repel boarders). That
is also when a merchantman's crew might cut themselves free. Today a lashed merchantman simply waits.
