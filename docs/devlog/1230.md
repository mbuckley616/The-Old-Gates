## v80 — Session 1230 — The neck's border stops at the neck's base (bodies builder; prototype only)
Session 1220 left a second note under *Needs eyes*, carried by Session 1228: the Aurennais gown's embroidered border at the neck had its
lower cord running down into the crease where the collar meets the shoulder, so from the side the cord was cut off by the shoulder's line.
This session fixes it in the kit. It is a fix to today's bodies, so it can be done under FREEZE #528 (all of `js/`, `index.html`, `tests/`
and `.github/`, while the new look lands). Nothing outside `docs/prototypes/levelup/` and the docs changed.

**The cause.** The border was laid on a plain band of rest height, .578 to .606, round the neck's axis (x 0, z .025). Over the front the
neck stands straight there, about 5 cm out all the way up, and the band lies on it. At the sides it does not: casting rays out from the
neck's axis onto the tunic's rest triangles, the surface at 90° is 5.3 cm out at .596 but 16 cm out at .576, the top of the shoulder, so
the band's foot (and the lower cord 2 mm over it) sat on the crease itself. Behind the shoulders the neck flares gradually into the
trapezius: at 110° the surface goes from 5.6 cm out at .604 to 9.1 cm at .580, so the band's lower half lay out on that slope and the
rings and the vine were drawn out wide (`builder-neck-back-before.jpg`). Measured the same way (the check below), the surface under the
old band stands out up to 4.05 cm past its top, at 112° behind the shoulder, and 3.7 mm for each millimetre down at its steepest.

**The fix.** A band round the neck now reads its foot off a table of 48 heights round the neck's axis (`neckBandTable`, `NECK_BAND`'s
`neckY` in GLSL, `neckBandY` in JS for the checks), as a long gown's hem reads its cut off `lozHemTable` (S1163). Each height is found by
walking the band's depth (2.8 cm for the gown's border) down the tunic's own rest surface from the top at .606, a millimetre at a time,
along the profile the rays find, instead of down y; the heights are smoothed round, never lower than the walk's own. Over the front,
where the neck is straight, the walk ends where the band always ended: .5784 at 0°, within half a millimetre of .578 out to 45°. At the
sides it ends at .5812, on the neck short of the crease; behind the shoulders at .5874, before the slope. The band's top stays at .606,
and `t` runs from the foot to the top, so the cords, the vine and the rings keep their places across it and the border is the same depth
down the cloth all the way round. The Mark's woven band at the neck (S1156, 2.6 cm, `LOZ_NECK`) had the same plain foot (.58) and takes
the same table for its own depth; it is drawn only within 10 cm of the middle at the front and back, so it moves only behind the
shoulders. The table is computed once per depth from the pack and passed as a uniform; one band round the neck per material (a gown's
border and the woven bands never meet on one tunic). 0 triangles and 0 draws added.
