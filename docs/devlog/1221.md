## v80 — Session 1221 — a foe's distant copy in its genome's colours (card F4, part six)
*Renumbered from Session 1206: the bodies builder's Sessions 1205 and 1206 landed on main first, and the files of one name
held this PR in conflict; 1217 to 1219 were taken on other open branches too (the renumber, one run later, by the deep-work session).*
Owed since Session 1201: on the new look a human foe's builder body was rolled from its genome only at its first attach, when it
first came within 46 m. The driver, its old 17-bone body, kept the colours of the old genome (`personGenome`'s skin and hair). So past
42 m, where the driver shows again, a bandit's skin and hair changed at the line each time it crossed it. The townsfolk never had
this: `hbPrep` rolls their genome before the driver is baked and tints it from the genome (`hbTint`). A foe is baked before it has
an id, because `keyFoe` names it after `buildZoneEnemy` or the dungeon's builder has made it, and the co-op rule keys its body by
that id (`foe:<id>`).

**What changed:**
- **`keyFoe`** (`js/42-zone-enemies.js`) calls `hbFoeKeyed(e.limbs.person, e)` when the foe it names is a person. It is the one
  place every foe is named: the zones', the sites', the dungeons' and the quests'.
- **`hbFoeKeyed(rig, e)`** (`js/32-people.js`) rolls the genome then (`hbFoeGenome`, as before, keyed `foe:<id>`). It tints a copy of
  the driver's genome from it (`hbTint`: the skin, the hair, the tunic, the legs, the boots) and bakes the driver's two copies again
  from that copy, near (`geoHi`) and far (`geoLo`). Only the colours differ, so the vertices are the same and only the colour attribute
  is copied into the driver's geometry; the new bakes are thrown away. It does nothing on the old look, for a foe already rolled, or
  for one that is not a person (the dead, the stone, the folk of the hills keep their bodies, `hbFoeOk`). The genome is the one the
  first attach would have rolled, so the near body is the same as before.

**The cost:** two bakes a foe when it is named, 22 to 81 ms on this CPU (eight measured: 81, 48, 41, 22, 36, 25, 26, 25; the first
is the slowest). Foes are named as they are built, while a zone, a site or a dungeon floor loads, so this adds to the loading,
not to play: a floor of fifteen foes takes about half a second longer.
