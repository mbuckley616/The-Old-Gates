## v80 — Session 1731 — `fpweapons`' ten minutes: seventy copies of the game's loop (QA)
QA's order 2, owed since Session 1714. In the weekly run of 1 Nov (`check` 41305372499, main c97ed1c, shard 7), `fpweapons` hit
`run.mjs`'s 900 s cap on its first try, in the bows' evaluate (`fpweapons.test.mjs:52`, *Target page, context or browser has been
closed*). It passed the retry in 834 s. The bows' evaluate alone took 10.5 minutes, so the suite cost shard 7 about 29 minutes against
the 75 s in the time table. Here on main (s1138) it was red 1 of 1, again at the 900 s cap, at the same line.

The suite's fault, not the game's. The bows' work is small: a probe timed `tpWeapon`, `buildViewmodel` and the two bounding boxes at
2–6 ms for each of the three bows. The time goes before the evaluate starts. The thrust check just before it (S1465) steps the game by
calling `loop(t)` by hand about 70 times, and the first thing `loop` does (`90-main.js`) is `requestAnimationFrame(loop)`. So each call
leaves one more copy of the game's loop running after the evaluate returns. With about 70 of them, every frame renders the world about
70 times, and the next `page.evaluate` waits behind all of that on software GL. Every other suite that steps `loop` already stubs
`requestAnimationFrame` while it does (74 places). `fpweapons` and `tpweapons` were the only two that did not; `tpweapons`' thrust
(S1466) is its last evaluate, so only its close paid.

Both now stub `requestAnimationFrame` for the stepping and restore it in a `finally`. No check changed, and none of the numbers it
reads changed. A sweep of every `page.evaluate` that calls `loop(` finds no other evaluate without the stub.
