## v80 — Session 1172 — The six gods' row at the town church (L9, OG-113, prototype only; asked as a decision)
(Numbered 1171 while it was being built. QA's auto/qa-1171 took that number first, so this one is 1172.)

Michael's C on #439 puts the row of seven (the six gods and the Guest's struck niche) once in each town, and the one-god niche on the
roads. With it he asked: "Would it make sense to have the rows in or near churches?" The canon says it would. The Church is the
Church of the Weaver (§1.3), and the Weaver is one of the six (§4.1). The concept artist's Aurenne row already carries the Church's
notice, *kept by order of the Prior of the parish*. So the row belongs at the church. Where at the church is a question of room. Since
#433 B (Session 1138, on #393) a town lays out its church's plot before its lots. The plot is the church end-on to the square, with a
yard of 2 m on the side away from the street and 3 m behind the chancel. The Gatelands row is 9.4 m long and needs about 2.5 m of depth
with its basins and a person kneeling at one. That does not fit beside the nave.

This is a prototype only, and it touches no game code. `shrines-church.html` (`shrines-church-view.js`) builds the Gatelands' town
church with its west tower (#410 B, `gatelandsChurch` from `pieces.js`, three bays) on that plot. Around it are the square, the main
street along its flank, a limewashed drystone yard wall with a gated way in beside the tower, a high cross, headstones, a yew, and
townhouses across the street and behind the yard. The row stands at the church in one of three places:
- **`?place=back` (A): behind the chancel,** in the 3 m yard, facing the east window, on the plot as it is laid now. It fits only
  just: 1.34 m from the basins to the chancel's eaves. One person can kneel there, but nobody can pass behind them. From the square it
  cannot be seen at all.
- **`?place=side` (B): along the yard's long side,** facing the nave's flank, its back to the yard wall. The yard is widened from 2 to
  4.5 m, so the plot goes from 10.4 to 12.9 m wide. The gravel path from the gate runs between the row and the nave, with 2.82 m from
  the basins to the flank. From the square you see it through the gate. The high cross and some graves move to the east yard.
- **`?place=square` (C): on the square,** free-standing beside the tower door, in the concept artist's wayside dress (its own wall,
  holy well, rag thorn, high cross and ring-stone), its east end 1.4 m short of the tower's face. The plot stays as it is. The row is
  the first thing you see on the square, but it takes about 13 by 4.5 m of it.

`row()` in `shrines.js` gains `o.yard`. For the Gatelands, inside a churchyard, it builds the niches, their rags and the primroses
only, without the row's own wall, well, thorn, cross and ring-stone, because the yard has its own wall and cross. That brings the row
from 32,583 to 21,931 triangles in 27 draws. Its default is unchanged, and the concept artist's page renders as before.

The page imports `gatelandsChurch` from `pieces.js`, which main does not have yet: the church is on #393 (auto/backlog, Session
1087), which was in CI when this was built. Until #393 merges the page stops at that import. The pictures and the check below were
made with #393's `pieces.js` copied into the working tree and not committed. This branch touches no file of #393's, so the two land
in either order.

### Decision
**Where does a town’s row of seven stand at its church? (look builder, Session 1172) — DECISION #464**
Your C on #439 puts the six gods' row (with the Guest's struck niche) once in each town, and you asked whether it belongs in or near the
church. It does: the Church is the Weaver's, and the Weaver is one of the six. The town now lays out its church's plot first (#433 B):
the church end-on to the square with its west tower, a yard of 2 m beside it and 3 m behind. The row is 9.4 m long and needs 2.5 m of
depth with its basins. Where does it stand?
- **A. Behind the chancel, in the plot as it is.** It fits only just: 1.34 m from the basins to the chancel's eaves, room for one
  person to kneel. It cannot be seen from the square, and the empty seventh niche is found only by walking round the church.
- **B. Along the churchyard's long side, the yard widened by 2.5 m** *(recommended)*. The row stands among the graves, facing the
  nave, with the path from the gate running past it. You see it through the gate from the square. The plot grows from 10.4 to 12.9 m
  wide, one number in the town's layout; no other building changes size.
- **C. On the square beside the tower door, free-standing**, in the wayside dress with its own wall, well, thorn and cross. It is the
  first thing on the square, and the plot is unchanged. It takes about 13 by 4.5 m of the square where the stalls and the town's people
  walk, and it stands apart from the church rather than in its keeping.

Recommendation: **B.** The row is in the Church's keeping, in the yard where the town buries its dead, which fits a Church that
blesses the dead into the gates (§3.1). It can be seen from the square and found by its gate, and you pass the Guest's empty niche
on the path to it. It costs 2.5 m of one plot. A hides the row, and C turns it into a monument on the square. The same answer would
carry to the Mark's and Aurenne's churches when they are built.
