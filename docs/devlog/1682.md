## v80 — Session 1682 — a foe goes round a frozen pool instead of wading through the ice
Session 1678's entry left a clip owed. Its pool is solid while it is water (a column in `DUNGEON_COLUMNS`). When `freezePool` freezes it, the column
goes and the ice becomes a foothold 0.32 m up. You walk up onto it. A foe in a gate reads no footholds: its height is its floor's `baseY`, set
once. So a foe chasing you across the ice walked through it at floor height, sunk to the shins in a sheet you were standing on.

There were two ways to fix it. One was to give every foe in a gate a foothold reading. That would touch every branch of the dungeon's foe tick in
`90-main.js` (twelve `position.set(e.x,e.baseY,e.z)` calls), and it would change foes on a ring's dais, a flooded hall's islands and the floating
stones too. That is a rule of the foes' movement, and too wide a change for one clip. The other follows what the chasm already does (Session 1327): the
open air is no wall to you (`dSolidP`) and is a wall to a foe (`dSolid`). This session takes the second. A frozen pool stays a wall to a foe's step only.

- `dIceHit(x,z)` (`56-dungeon-build.js`) is true within a frozen pool's radius plus a body's 0.3 m on the current floor, read from
  `GATE_THINGS`. `dBlkF` is `dBlk` or `dIceHit`, and `dSlide`, the step every gate foe takes, now tests `dBlkF`.
- Nothing else changes. `dBlk` and `dSolid` are untouched, so your own step (`dSlideP`), the push between bodies, arrows, line of sight and bolts
  meet the ice as open floor. They do the same as before the change, with the column gone.

So a foe stops at the kerb of a frozen pool as it stopped at the water's, and goes round it the same way. Standing on the ice puts you out of
reach of a foe at the kerb if the pool is wider than its blow. A 1.8 m pool's middle is 2.1 m from where a foe stops, and a foe swings within 0.9 m.
Whether that is a fair use of Sioc's touch is a question about the rules. The systems builder may want a foe that crosses the ice. That needs the foothold reading above, as its own change.
