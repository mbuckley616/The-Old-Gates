## v80 — Session 1662 — the names cut on the ring post's boards (arenas session 2's owed item; OG-089)

Session 1658 built the ladder's ring with its post: squared oak 3.1 m high with six blank boards down its face, "where the names are
cut". The names lived only in E's message (`ringPostLines`); the boards stayed bare whatever happened in the ring. The arenas sheet's
"the post with its names" (Michael's B on #914) wants them on the wood. The ring's reference is OG-089; OG-079 and OG-080 give no
shape for a post.

**The faces (`markRing`, `js/38-kit.js`).** Each board gets a face 4 mm proud of it, 0.25 by 0.12 m. All six are in one mesh with the
ring's own canvas, 256 by 768 px, one row of 256 by 128 to a board (the board's own 2:1). `ringNames()` makes the canvas and returns
`paint(list)`, which cuts `list[k]` into board k in capitals, Georgia bold. Each name is fitted to the board's width: one line from
62 px down to 28, then two lines split at the best space. A letter is fresh wood in the groove (.60, .48, .32) with its upper-left
edge in shadow, read against the board's dark carve. Everything else on the canvas is transparent and cut away by `alphaTest`, so a
board with no name shows its own wood. `paint` keeps the last list's key and does nothing when it has not changed. `PKIT.mk.ring`
returns it as `paint`. The material is the ring's own, so the ring is 7 draws, not 6, and 4,696 tris, not 4,684.

**What is cut (`83-world-generator.js`).** `ringPostNames(site)` gives the top board the champion. While you hold the title that is
your own name (`playerName`); otherwise it is `rings.champion`, the challenger's name once the title passes. The next five boards are
the last five killed after they yielded (`rings.by[site].dead`), newest first. A post is painted when its town builds. `houseLod`,
which already decides each ring's near copy, calls `paint` while that copy is shown, so a title won or a fighter killed is on the wood
the next time you look. Off the near copy nothing is repainted. E at the post still reads every name; the boards show the last five.
