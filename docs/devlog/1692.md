## v80 — Session 1692 — a foe crosses a frozen pool to reach you
The systems builder's run of 1 Nov, its second item: backlog I's *s1111 — a melee foe cannot reach you on a frozen pool* (the critic's report of 1 Nov,
filed for this lane).

**What was wrong.** Sioc touched on a gate's pool freezes it (S1678, S1680), and the ice is a foothold 0.32 m up that you walk across. A foe in a gate
reads no footholds, so before Session 1682 it walked through the ice at floor height, sunk to the shins. S1682 made the frozen pool a wall to a foe's
step (`dIceHit`, `dBlkF` in `dSlide`), and said the systems builder might want a foe that crosses the ice instead, which needs a foe to stand on it.
In play the wall made the ice a refuge. In seed 909's pool (R 1.8), an alerted Skeleton came to 4.16 m from the critic in the middle, 2.36 m off the
rim, and never swung. Any side room with a pool was a safe spot once you had Sioc.

**The change** (`56-dungeon-build.js`, beside S1682's code, and one call in `90-main.js`'s dungeon foe loop).
- `dSlide`, the step every gate foe takes, tests `dBlk` again, so the ice is open floor to a foe as it is to you. The water is still a wall: its
  column is in `dBlk` until `freezePool` takes it away, as before.
- `dIceLift(x,z)` is the ice's height, 0.32 m (`GT_POOL.ICE`), within a frozen pool's radius on the current floor, and 0 elsewhere.
  `dFoeIce(e)` keeps the foe's floor height as `_floorY` the first time and sets `e.baseY` to it plus the lift on each tick. Every place
  the loop sets a foe's height already reads `baseY`, so the foe stands on the ice where you stand, and steps down off it at the rim.
- `dBlkF` and `dIceHit` stay: the slime's split (S1686) still keeps the two children off a frozen pool.

### Verified (headless Chromium)

  rim plus a body). Once the pool is frozen it reaches the middle (0 m), where `dIceLift` reads 0.32, and 0 past the rim. (This replaces S1682's
  check that the ice was a wall to a foe.) Through the game's loop, with you in the ice's middle and an alerted Skeleton set 3 m off the rim (the critic's case), the Skeleton came
  onto the ice to 0.64 m from you, stood 0.32 m up on it and struck you within the 10 s. On S1682's wall the critic measured it stopping at 4.16 m and
  never swinging. I did not re-run the old code against the new check. `gatethings` 1/1, 18 checks (+1).

  inside a wall (its bearing was checked open at R + 1.4 m, not at R + 3). The test now takes a bearing open all the way out.
- `python3 scripts/parsecheck.py`: OK.
