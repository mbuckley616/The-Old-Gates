## v80 — Session 2485 — both hands on a two-hander's grip
The second session of the bodies builder's run of 21 Nov, on `auto/bodies-2484`. It takes what S2484's measurements turned up. In third
person on the new look, a two-hander (a claymore, a great axe, a warhammer) was held in the right hand alone. The builder's left fist hung
0.31 m under the grip, open and holding nothing. This was true standing, walking and in every pose that keeps the two-handed grip.

**Why.** `tpGripL` (S246) puts the *driver's* left wrist on the driver's blade, and that lands exactly (0 m). Two things lie between that
and what is seen:
- The blade that is seen rides in the builder's right fingers (`tpHBHold`), 0.30 m from the driver's.
- The builder's arm copies the driver's joint for joint but has its own length, so its wrist lands 0.24 m from the driver's.

S2484 tried aiming the driver short by the builder's miss, as the mantle's hands are set (`tpHBMantle`). That did not converge: the miss
went from 0.33 to 0.55 m. In a test of five passes, the builder's fingers landed 0.24–0.31 m off whatever the driver's aim.

**What changed** (`js/54-thirdperson.js`, the new look only, a look only):
- The left arm is now reached on the builder's own skeleton, after `hbPose`, by the new `tpHBGripL`. The bow hand's fingers are set on the
  builder's bones in the same way (`tpHBBowHand`). Twice over:
  - `lowerarm01` is turned about the elbow's own hinge (the plane of the upper arm and the forearm) until shoulder to fingers equals
    shoulder to the seen blade's left grip mark.
  - Then `upperarm01` is swung to put the fingers' middle on that mark.
- **Weight.** It is laid over the copied arm by the grip's weight: 1 while held, and `tpDrawLeft`'s weight during the draw and the sheathe.
  Its target is the seen blade, so S2484's left hand now also lands on the blade as it comes out.
- **When it applies.** `tpPose` and `tpDrawLeft` leave an aim (`R.gAim`), which `tpHBPose` uses once and clears.
  - It is skipped if anything has since moved the driver's left arm: the climb, the mantle, the rod, the net and the glass all set it after
    the grip.
  - A pose that sets no aim is never pulled to a grip: the sheet's portrait, whose two-hander stands on its point, and the creator.
- No triangles. Per frame while a two-hander is held: two passes of two bone turns, with no further `hbPose`.
