## v80 — Session 1246 — F10, part one: the player's body from the builder's genome (card F10, FREEZE #547)
Numbered 1246: QA's run took 1240 while this one ran, and the other lanes 1241 to 1245 (the card was set running as Session 1240).
Card F10 (v2-scope.md §2) moves the player's own body onto the builder, as F4 moved the townsfolk and the human foes. The card is
ten to twenty hours; this session is its first part and stands on its own: the genome, the creator, third person and what is worn
and held. First-person hands and the animations F4's driver does not already carry (the bind, the parry's body, swimming, the bow's
draw on the builder's fingers, the ragdoll) come next, on `auto/fable-f10-body-2`. Everything here is the new look's only
(`hbOn()`); `?look=old` builds the old body as before.

**The genome.** The player had no genome of the builder's, and no sex, height or build of their own: `tpBuild` made a 17-bone body
from `personGenome` with the look's colours over it. Now `worldState.look.hb` holds a saved builder genome (`HB.toSaved`: the
sliders, the face, the palette, the hair and the beard, 681 bytes for the test's character), and `look` is already in `SS_CHAR_WS`,
so the genome is in the character row. `lookHBGenome(L, people, name, female)` (`54-thirdperson.js`) reads it, or, for a look with
none, rolls one from its seed (`player:<name>`, or the seed the creator's shuffle drew) inside the people's ranges with
`HB.rollGenome`, which is what F4's townsfolk use. The look's own choices are laid over it: the skin, the hair's colour and cut (the
creator's thirteen styles mapped onto the builder's six with a length each, `LOOK_HB_STYLE`), the beard (its fifteen kinds as the
builder's one length, `LOOK_HB_BEARD`), and three new choices, the body (man or woman), the height and the build. Height is the
people's stature range in metres (short, middling or tall: its bottom, middle or top) turned into the builder's height slider by
`HB.heightFor`, which measures the morphed body; build is the muscle and weight ranges. A save from before F10 has no `hb`; its body
is rolled from its name and its old sex (the driver's), the same on every load, until F13 maps old looks to genomes.

**The creator and the barber.** Both preview through `tpBuild`, so both now show the builder's body; after each rebuild the creator
copies the genome it was built with back into the look it is editing (`CCL.rig.hbSaved`), and `ccBegin` saves it with the rest of
the look. The creator gains three rows on the new look, *Body*, *Height* and *Build*; the *Beard* row is not offered for a woman.
The shuffle draws a new face as well (a new seed; it is a choice at creation, then saved, so `Math.random` there decides nothing
that is ever rolled again). The barber edits the hair and beard over the saved face; it no longer counts the genome's own fields
when it asks whether anything changed (so a look that gained its `hb` on opening the chair does not charge the fee for nothing).
Changing the people or the body rolls a new face from the seed, since a face is rolled inside a people's ranges.

**Third person.** `tpBuild` still builds and poses the 17-bone driver exactly as before (every swing, the guard, the bow, the
climb, the roll and the fall are its), and on the new look it then hangs the builder's body on it with F4's own `hbAttach`, so the
skeleton copy, the left/right check, the hips' travel and the float64 skinning at world coordinates are F4's, not a second copy.
The driver's mesh is hidden (`hbLevel(rig, 0)`; the player is always at the near level). One thing differs from the townsfolk: F4
scales a builder body to its driver's stature, and the player's driver has no stature of its own choosing, so the player's root is
scaled to put the builder's body at the genome's own height in metres (`rig.hb.st`). Each frame after `tpPose`, `tpHBPose` copies
the pose (`hbPose`) and moves what is held.

**What is worn and held.** `tpHBDress` dresses a copy of the genome from `EQ` as `tpBuild` dresses the old body: an empty chest is
the undyed linen shirt and an empty leg the linen braies (Michael's B on #83), bare feet without boots; your tunic, breeches and
boots in the look's dyes; a robe as the builder's robe; a worn metal chest piece as the builder's mail in the material's metal, the
light line's jerkin as a leather tunic, the robe line as a robe in its tier's dye; a metal helm as the builder's helm; and the cloak
in the back slot as the builder's cloak in its kind's colour (or the dyer's), a short cape for the Aurennais cape, fur-lined, hemmed
and hooded as `TP_CLOAK` says. What the hands hold (the weapon, the bow, the shield, the torch, the tome) stays on the driver's
bones where `tpPose` turns it, as every test and the shot origin expect, and a copy of each (its geometry shared) rides in the
builder's group, following it each frame (`tpHBHold`), a weapon's grip at the middle of the builder's curled fingers. Both fists
close (`hbCloseFist`: Michael's D on #99, the folded fist on an empty hand, and a hand closed on what it holds); a tome is held
open-handed. The armour kit's own shapes (lamellar, the muscle cuirass, plate and the marks by tier) are the old body's; fitting
them onto the builder's body is part two's, with the gauntlets, greaves and the hood without a cloak, which the builder's dress has
no piece for yet.

**Fixed on the way.** The creator's preview turned itself during its first four seconds: the builder's body takes most of a
second to build, and the people's button and the class each rebuild it, so the four still seconds ran out before it was shown. The
preview now holds still for four seconds from each rebuild.

### Needs eyes
- How the player's body reads: the face is rolled, not chosen (the creator offers the people, body, height, build, colours, hair
  and beard over a rolled face, and the shuffle rolls another). Sliders for the face are the builder's page's; whether the creator
  should carry them is a question for later, not asked here.
- Each change of kit builds the body again: 0.55 s on this container's CPU, about the same as one townsperson in F4. Equipping in
  the inventory therefore hitches for half a second on the new look. Caching the body and re-dressing only the garment that
  changed is owed (part two or three).
- The enchantment's motes on worn pieces hang on the driver's bones, inside its hidden mesh, so they do not show on the new body
  (the motes on a held weapon do: they ride in its copy). Owed with the armour kit's fit.
- The builder's cloak is rigid on the body's root, as the townsfolk's are: it hangs straight from the shoulders and does not follow
  the torso's lean, a swing or the stride. On a townsperson that reads as a heavy cloak; on the player, whose back is always in view
  in third person, it may read as a board. Skinning it to the spine and swinging its hem is owed (part two, with the armour's fit).
- The head turns with the view (`tpPose`'s look, as before): a high pitch tips the builder's longer head back further than the old
  head seemed to. The pictures are taken level.
- What the hands hold follows the driver's hand, not the builder's: the grip sits in the builder's fist, but the blade's angle is
  the old pose's, and at rest the sword lies flatter across the body than it did on the old body (`f10-third-rest.png`). Posing
  the builder's wrist and fingers for each grip is part two's, with the bow's draw.
- The camera, the shadow and the LOD rules are F4's: the player's body casts no shadow of its own, as F4's builder bodies do not
  (the driver casts it for the townsfolk; the player's driver never cast one in third person).
