## v80 — Session 2464 — the Frostborn Vault's headwall: a cliff in ledged beds, not pleated curtains (prototype; OG-104)

**The item.** Section I holds nothing for the look builder, and this lane's six open questions (#1754, #1761, #1791, #1808, #1815,
#1823) all wait on Michael. Session 2461 left this note under *Needs eyes*, and the producer named it as the lane's next: the Vault's
headwall came from the land's 8 m grid, so from the far view it read as pleated curtains, not as a cliff with ledges. OG-104
(`docs/art/thumbs/OG-104.jpg`, the Mark panel) paints the vault under sheer snow-streaked rock.

**What was wrong.** The headwall was one step in the land's heightfield: 130 to 170 m of height over 22 m of depth, which at 8 m a row is
two or three rows. Each long triangle ran from snow at the foot to snow at the top. The land's colour comes from its slope, and those
triangles were coloured by blending their corners, so the wall came out a pale grey. The wobble that was meant to break up the edge
(`fbm(x / 12, …)`) pulled those triangles into vertical folds.

**What changed** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/ruins.js` `frostbornVault`; the prototype only):
- **The cliff is now its own mesh.** It runs the plane's whole width (1,600 m), in columns 3 m apart within 260 m of the middle and 6 m
  apart beyond. The rows run up the beds. It keeps S2461's front line and height (`zh(x)`, `cl(x)`), and its foot is set 12 m into
  the ground.
- **Beds.** There are 13 beds, each 10 to 26 m high, keyed by `ruins:frostborn:beds`.
  - Each bed leans back 0.1 m in every metre of height.
  - At its top, over 1.2 m of height, each bed steps back 1 to 9 m in a ledge. The ledge pinches and widens along the wall with a noise at
    30 m.
  - Each bed's face is roughened ±1.6 m at a scale of 14 m.
- **Buttresses and gullies.** Along the wall, 22 to 60 m stretches are drawn as in Stormwatch (S2457), keyed by `:buttress`.
  - 16 buttresses stand 6 to 16 m out, from 8 to 68 m under the top down to the foot. None stands out within 104 m of the middle, so
    none crowds the face's back.
  - 9 gullies are cut back 5 to 12 m the whole height.
- **Snow and the cap.** The triangles are flat-shaded, and each is snow where it faces up (`ny` over about 0.5) and dark rock where it
  does not. The ledges, the buttresses' shoulders and the cap therefore carry the snow, and the faces stay bare. Each bed has its own tint.
  A cap of snow runs from the top 14 m or more back onto the land.
- **The land behind.** It is drawn as the cliff read backwards: at every height the land stands at least 10 m behind the cliff, and behind
  the columns 4 and 8 m either side too. The grid's interpolation within one 8 m cell can never bring it through the face. Each
  column's face is kept as a table of 420 heights, so the land reads it by lookup. A first version solved by halving on every land
  point and took minutes; the whole site now builds in about 1.1 s.
- **`ground()`** now reads the cliff's own face, then the cap, then the land, so a test or a placement asking for the ground there gets the
  rock.
- **Two faults fixed along the way.**
  - The triangles were first wound inward, so every ledge faced down and none took snow.
  - The first beds stepped back 1 to 3.5 m, which at 700 m (the far view) did not read at all; they were doubled.
- **Cost.** The cliff is 43,000 triangles in one new draw (`rock`, the kit's rock texture under the vertex colours). The site is 173,365
  triangles in 12 draws, against 130,558 in 11.
