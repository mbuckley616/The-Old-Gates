## v80 — Session 1217 — the townhouse's door at 2.2 m, and the scale suite reads the kit's doors (Michael's A on #514)
Session 1211 found three reasons why `tests/scaleratio` read Dunmore's doors at 0.49, 0.50 and 2.9 m on the new look, and raised the one
that was a design call as DECISION #514. Michael answered A (20 Oct): "bring the townhouse door to 2.2 m under the same fanlight." This
session builds that answer and fixes the suite's two faults.

**The door.** The kit's townhouse front door is a `fan` opening 1.1 m wide. Its leaf rises to the arch's spring, and the fanlight fills
the half-circle over it (`38-kit.js`). The opening was 2.95 m, so the leaf was 2.4 m. It is now 2.75 m, which puts the leaf at 2.2 m under
the same 0.55 m fanlight. That is the foundation's house door (1.22 of a 1.8 m person, with the eye at 0.75 of it). Both places that
draw the door changed: `PLANS.townhouse`, the default plan, and `bayPlan`'s townhouse, the one the towns use. The prototype it was
copied from (`docs/prototypes/levelup/pieces.js`, the same two lines, and the "Town door" swatch in `pieces-gatelands.js`) changed with
it, so the next copy does not undo it. The windows beside the door still head at 2.6 m; the door's dressed arch now reaches 3.07 m
against 3.27 before. Nothing else in the plan moved. The triangle counts are unchanged: `kit` still builds the townhouse to 5,033, and
its four bay plans to 4,443 to 6,221.

**The suite.** `scaleratio`'s rule for the old builder's doors is kept as it was, line for line: a flat leaf stands proud of its
surround, so the leaf is the run of hits within 6 mm of the hit at 1.3 m. Three things are new.
- **The house's own footprint.** The suite took the first solid with a house id within 8 m of the door. For 7 of Dunmore's kit houses
  that was a neighbour's, so the rays ran along a wall (2.9 m). It now takes the solid with the house's own id (`s.hid === h.id`), and
  falls back to the old search for anything without one. The ray is aimed along that solid's facing (`c`, `s`, signed toward the door).
  Before, it aimed from the solid's centre to the door, which is off square for a kit house whose door stands in a bay, not mid-front.
- **The kit's doors, by what the ray meets.** The kit builds one mesh per material, so a hit names its material (`PKIT.gl.mats()`).
  On a kit house the leaf is its paint, with the raised panels (3 cm proud, which the old rule read as a 0.5 m leaf) and the iron.
  A glazed door's glass and its thin bars belong to the door. A rail of 6.5 cm or more ends it at the rail's foot: the fanlight's spring
  bar (7 cm) or the shop door's transom (11 cm). The sash's bottom rail (5.5 cm) and the glazing bars (3 cm) do not end it. Anything else
  ends it: the dressing, the wall, a miss. On the way down the step before the door is passed through, to the leaf's foot. One limit is
  written into the suite: a farmhouse's transom door would read to its transom's head, since its rail is as thin as a glazed door's.
  No town has a farmhouse; the villages do, and the suite does not measure them.
- **Speed.** A kit door's rays meet only its own building (the town's baked meshes made every ray cross the whole town). They step 2 cm
  while they stay on the leaf or its glass, and go back to 5 mm through anything else, so the edges are found at 5 mm as before. An old
  builder's door still meets every mesh within 4 m of it, at 5 mm throughout.
