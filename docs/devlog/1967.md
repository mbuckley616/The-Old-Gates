## v80 — Session 1967 — the north road's night frame, profiled: no night cost beyond the box's own drift
The systems builder's run of 7 Nov, started at 16:47 UTC. There was no `FREEZE:` issue, and this lane's last branch
(`auto/systems-1965`, #1268) had landed. The highest session on any branch was 1966, so this is 1967.

**Why this item.** Section I holds nothing for this lane. `docs/quest_review.md` has no open finding in its files. Every answer
Michael has given this lane is built. The rest waits:
- water waits on DECISION #1254;
- the climate's first-time lines wait on *Dressed for It*'s approval;
- the Register questline waits on its draft;
- F5's session 5, the great gate's row, waits on backlog A placing a great gate.

So this run took the one open note on a system of this lane's (the tick and the frame budget, as in Sessions 1278 and 1467). The
critic's playtest of 7 Nov (s1330) saw three frames on Dunmore's north road take 32–34 s at 02h, against 9–18 s at 10h. With the
draw stubbed, 02h still took 9.6 s. They called it too noisy to file and did not file it.

**How it was measured.** Throwaway scripts in `tests/tmp/`, on the new look at Middle, at s1340 (main 27afb80):
- A new character, Dunmore settled, then 230 m up the north road with the cells loaded.
- `requestAnimationFrame` stopped, and the game's own `loop` driven at fixed 1/60 ticks.
- Each real frame ended with a 1-pixel `readPixels`, so a frame's time includes software GL finishing it.

**What it showed.**
- **The tick with the draw stubbed is not dearer at night.** Medians over 300 ticks were:

  | Hour | Median | p90 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10h | 4.7 ms | 8.8 |
  | 02h | 5.1 ms | 7.1 |

  There were 27 lights at both hours. The first 10h run after arriving read 15.3 ms, which is the arrival settling, not the
  hour. Every global function timed (3,585 of them) gives the same top at both hours: `tickPeople` 2.1–2.4 ms (its `pw*` poses),
  `tickCreatures` 0.7, `hbTick` 0.3. No function of this lane's is over 0.2 ms. The critic's 9.6 s "with the draw stubbed" cannot
  have been the tick. The likeliest reason is that one of the frame's other `REN.render` calls (bloom's inner render, or the view
  model's) was still drawing.
- **A real frame is the draw, and night costs it little more than repeating the same hour does.** Frames on software GL:

  | Hour | Real frame |
  |---|---|
  | 10h | 6.7–10.4 s |
  | 02h, 21h, 23h | 8.6–12.3 s |

  In one boot, 10h measured three times read 6.9, 8.8 and 9.6 s, as the run went on. A given hour drifts upward across a boot by
  as much as night adds. A day frame measured early against a night frame measured late, as the critic's were, shows most of the
  2–3 times by itself.
- **It is not the shadow.** At night the sun stands 20.9° up, against 56.8° at noon. Turning off the shadows of the 100 instanced
  tree meshes (281k of the shadow pass's 333k triangles) changed nothing. Turning off the sun's shadow altogether gave 8.4–8.8 s,
  inside the spread with it on.

  A correction to my own first reading: three calls the shadow step on every pass, the bloom's quad passes included. So a timer
  round `REN.shadowMap.render` measured nearly the whole render (7–12 s), not the shadow pass.
- **Nothing builds up.** Over 1,800 ticks alternating 10h and 02h the scene held 8,621 objects, 3,467 meshes and 27 lights. The
  renderer held 826 geometries and 160 textures.

No code changed. The backlog's *Stutter on the new look* carries the numbers, with nothing owed.
