## v80 — Session 1194 — the Gatelands farmstead: the byre, the barn and the yard, three ways (OG-115, OG-114; card F6; prototype only)
FREEZE #492 (card F4) is open, so this run stayed in the prototypes. Session 1192 built the farmhouse's rooms and left OG-115's
"farmstead with outbuildings" owed: the byre, the barn and the yard between them. OG-114 also lists the farmstead first among its
eight workplaces, and the kit had no farm building except the house. This session builds them in `pieces.js`, as places beside the
house rather than rooms inside it.

**The two new plans** (`GL_PLANS.byre`, `GL_PLANS.barn`):
- **The byre** is one low storey, 12 by 5.4 m. It is limewashed rubble 0.6 m thick on a tarred plinth, like the cottages, under a
  slate roof at 36°, eaves at 2.6 m and the ridge at 4.73 m. On the yard it has two red half-doors (1.05 by 2.0 m) and two small
  lights. Two vents are at the back, and a hay hatch in the west gable opens into the loft over the cattle.
- **The barn** is two storeys (2.7 and 2.4 m), 12 by 6.4 m. It is bare rubble 0.7 m thick with dressed quoins, under slate at 40°,
  with the ridge at 7.97 m. On the yard it has a round-headed cart arch (2.8 by 2.6 m) and a stable door. Ventilation slits are in
  both storeys. A pitching door in the east gable has the kit's hoist beam over it.

**The farmstead** (`gatelandsFarmstead({ kind })`) uses the kit's farmhouse with these two buildings round a cobbled yard. The way in
is a pair of round, limewashed gate piers with conical caps, 3.4 m apart, with an iron field gate swung back into the yard. Field walls
of limewashed rubble (1.4 m high, with a rounded coping) close the rest. In the yard:
- the dunghill before the byre's doors;
- the cast-iron pump and its stone trough by the house's yard door;
- a two-wheeled cart, shafts down;
- a rick of hay, roped;
- a clamp of turf.

The layout follows the Irish country's three farmyards, built as three options because the choice is open:
- **`yard`** (the courtyard farm): the house faces the road and the yard lies behind it. The byre runs down the west side and the barn
  down the east, with a wall across the foot. The way in is a lane past the house's east gable. The first cut left 1.25 m between the
  house's back corner and the barn's end, too narrow for a cart, so both outbuildings now start 3.6 m behind the house. 23.8 by 22.0 m.
- **`line`** (the linear farm): the byre stands against the house's west gable and the barn against its east, all fronts in one line.
  The yard lies between the row and the road wall, with the gate in front of the house's door. 34.5 by 15.6 m.
- **`range`** (the parallel farm): the byre and the barn stand end to end in one range across the yard from the house's back door.
  24 by 22.8 m.

Each layout is about 8.5k triangles in 20 draws once merged by material. `pieces-gatelands.html?view=farmstead&farm=yard|line|range|all`
shows them on a country road, with `&cam=road|yard|corner|high`.

### Decision
DECISION issue: which farmyard the Gatelands' farms take.
- **A. Round a yard** *(recommended)*: the house to the road, the byre and the barn down either side of a yard behind it. The farm
  reads from the road as a house, and the yard is a place of its own, closed on four sides. 23.8 by 22 m.
- **B. In a line**: the byre, the house and the barn in one row behind a yard on the road. It is the commonest and the cheapest to
  place, but it needs a lot 34.5 m wide.
- **C. House and range**: the outbuildings in one range across the yard from the house's back door. 24 by 22.8 m.
- **D. By the farm**: the line for a small farm, round a yard for a large one.
