**The bywash.** The pound's surplus water goes round the lock on the towpath's (west) side, the side with room, since the cottage stands on
the east lockside 2.4 m off the paving.
- **The weir:** a notch 1.2 m wide is cut through the upper pound's west bank 6 m above the wing walls. It is lined in ashlar under a dressed
  cope 8 cm over the land, with a stone apron on the pound's side. A dressed weir crosses it with its crest 5 cm under the pound's water, so a
  thin sheet spills over into the channel 0.2 m below.
- **The towpath's slab:** the towpath crosses the notch on a stone slab, flat over the copes at 0.78 m and eased down to the path over 0.95 m
  at each end. The first slab stood 0.12 m proud of the path, which made the towpath 1 in 4.1 there; eased, it is 1 in 6.7.
- **The channel:** 1.4 m between ashlar faces, 0.4 m behind the towpath's lip, from the notch to 1.3 m below the lower gates' line, in three
  reaches (water at -0.25, -0.55, -0.85) with a pitched bed. Each fall is a dressed sill and a spill of 0.3 m with foam at its foot.
  The first channel was 1 m wide in four reaches down to -1.6, which left a slot 2.2 m deep at its foot that read as a trench. The rest of the
  fall (0.9 m) now goes into the culvert.
- **The culvert:** the channel ends at a headwall with a lintel over the culvert's mouth and an iron grate of nine bars. The culvert runs
  unseen under the towpath and the lockside to an outlet 0.8 by 0.65 m in the tail's west wall, 3 m below the lower gates. There a spout
  falls 0.85 m into the tail's water, with foam where it lands. The wall run is split round the opening, solid below its sill and over its head.
- **The land:** the land's grid takes lines at the channel's and the notch's faces, and the ground inside is cut out under the bed. The land's
  ripple is eased to nothing within 3 m of the cut so the copes sit level. The first pictures showed a dark trench along both walls. That was
  shading, not a cut: each wall's outer vertices shared a smoothed normal with the 2.6 m drop inside the wall. By the bywash the land's
  vertices now take the uncut ground's own normal. The pound's water sheets are cut back from 12 m to 6 m each side of the middle (the banks
  hide them past 5.5 m either way), so the upper sheet no longer lies over the channel's lower water.

**The walkway.** Each lower leaf carries a board 0.4 m wide on its downstream face, its top flush with the coping's. Shut, it runs from the
coping's edge to the mitre and meets its fellow on the middle line. It has a handrail on its outer edge, posts every metre or so and rails at
0.5 and 1 m, painted white like the beams' ends. Open, the board lies along the recess at the coping's height, clear of the chamber's 4.4 m
below. In the first picture the board and rail were black against the black leaf and could not be seen. The board is now weathered oak
