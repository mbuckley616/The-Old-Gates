## v80 — Session 1485 — Aurenne's village church (card F6)
No red of the look builder's, and nothing in section I names this lane. Next in card F6 was what Session 1305 left owed and the last run
named: an Aurennais village's limewashed church. Michael chose the domes by rank on #294 (C: blue glaze on a town's church and the
cathedral, limewash on a village's), and the kit's `aurenneChurch` already draws the village's rank (`rank:'village'`: the dome and the
tower's little dome in limewash), so this needed no new question. Images: OG-061, OG-043.

**The piece.** A village's church is `PKIT.au.church` at `AU_VCH` (`{rank:'village', w:12, tower:1}`, the prototype's village church in
`aurenne.js`): 12 m a side, the limewashed dome, one bell tower on the right. With its porch, steps and tower it stands on 16.85 by
18.98 m; it is 11,556 triangles, merged into the village's kit meshes in 10 draws (the town's 11: the glaze folds into the limewash).
`kitAurChurch` builds both ranks from `lot.kit.o`; the far copy's dome and the tower's cap are limewash for the village.

**The plot, and why not the lot.** I first fitted the church on the village's church lot as the courtyard house fits a home's lot (homes
under it let go). Not one of 39 fitted: a village is laid on a 17 m grid of streets, and any 17 by 19 m footprint crosses one (106 tries
failed on a street, 65 on the ring). And the street ribbons are drawn before the lots, so a church set down afterwards would have had a
path painted under it. So a village lays a plot, as a town does (Session 1305): `townPlots(true)` lays the church astride a street through
the heart, its door on that street's line, a yard of 1.5 m round it and no row of the gods. The village's church is a roll made after its
lots (`plan.optional`), so the plot is laid only if a first pass of `planLots` says the village has one; the lots are then laid round the
plot, and a village with a church plot keeps its church even if the second pass's rolls fall otherwise (the plot changes how many ring lots
are tried). To ask `planLots` that early, the town's state (`TST`) is now read just before the streets: it depends on the site alone.

**Level ground.** The podium stands on the ground with no footing, and a village's ground is not a town's: of the first plots laid, the
fall across the church's ground ran from 0 to 22.6 m. A village's plot is now taken only where its 3×3 of ground points lie within 0.5 m,
its steps 14, 10, 12, 16 or 8 m out (18, a town's second choice, will not fit inside a village's ring at 40 m). Of the 39 villages that
rolled a church among the first 60 surveyed, 24 take the new church; 15 keep the old church on their lot, as before.

**What it changes.** Only Aurennais villages with a church, on the new look. Their layout changes (the lots are laid round the plot), so a
house's id in those villages moves, as Session 1305's did in the towns. Every one surveyed has one home more than before (the church's old
lot is a home now): 7 or 8 buildings against 6 or 7.
