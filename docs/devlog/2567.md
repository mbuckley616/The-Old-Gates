## v80 — Session 2567 — Briarweed is a bramble, not a spider

The critic's first backlog I item of 23 Nov (s1889, `docs/critic/2026-11-23-briarweed-spider.jpg`): Briarweed on open turf read as
a 1.2 m yellow spider. The plant kit's `bramble` (`js/30-plants.js`) made it so: six bumpy balls packed at the centre (a body),
nine bare canes radiating from that one point, each arching in a plane and touching down 0.5–0.8 out (eight-odd legs), and its leaves
lay flat along the canes' tops, mixed toward the herb's brown `col` (0x7a5522), so from eye height they showed only as a yellow fringe.

The bramble is now a low thicket with no hub. Its eleven canes rise from four crowns spread up to 0.19 off-centre. Each arches at its
own height (0.12–0.28) and length (0.40–0.78), and its heading turns by up to ±0.7 rad as it goes, so the canes cross one another
and none runs straight. Each tip bows back into the ground. On every segment but the last, a leaf hangs off each side. The leaves are
larger (0.12–0.17 against 0.08) and tilted down and rolled, so they show from the side. They are bramble green (0x2e4a1c to 0x4a6026),
and one in seven is bronzed with the herb's own brown, so it still reads as the "tangled brown vine" of its description. Under the
canes, seven low leafy masses lie off the centre, wider than high. Picked, the outer leaves go. The masses and the leaves on each
cane's first two segments stay, so the cut-back plant is a mound, not a body with bare legs.

1,910 triangles (was 2,208), picked 1,094 (was 832). 0.27 high (was 0.30) and 1.22 across (was 1.30) in the kit's unit, ×1.661 on
the new look. The canes are three-sided now, which paid for the leaves.
