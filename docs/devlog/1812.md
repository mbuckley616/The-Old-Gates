## v80 — Session 1812 — The blue under a room's water
This session clears the systems builder's other note from Session 1806. Rooms swim by the sea's rules now, and in the sea grotto you
can dive, but the world's underwater look (`cameraUnderwater` feeding `atmosphere()` in `80-world-terrain.js`) answers only in the
world. Under the grotto's water the room went on looking exactly as it did above the surface: a dark cave, its fog out at 30 to
110 m, and no sign that the eye was under the water.

A room's fog belongs to its own scene, which `atmosphere()` never touches, so the fix is in the room's tick. `tickRoomUnderwater`
(`84-world-interiors.js`, run by `tickInterior` each frame) reads `roomWater()`, the systems builder's `INT_WATER` for the room you
are in, and asks whether the eye (`jumpY` plus the eye's current height) is more than 5 cm under its surface over water with a bed.
While it is, the room's fog and background turn to `ROOM_DEEP` (0x123a50) and the fog closes in to 0.5 to 14 m. `ROOM_DEEP` is the
open sea's underwater blue (0x1a4a6a) darkened for the rock over it, and 14 m is about where the world's 0.06 exponential fog
thickens. The room's own fog, near, far and background are kept on the scene's `userData` and put back the moment the eye comes up.
Only the view changes; the swim's rule is the systems builder's and is untouched. Today that covers the grotto's sea and the mine's
sump (0.6 m deep, waded, so its eye never goes under).
