**Why it needs a little of its own.** The kit drew the seal with two gaits, `swim` and `stand`. In the water it is a fish's case: it lies
along z, nose ahead, so the trout's path fits it nose to nose. But a seal hauls out, and on land nothing in the kit moved it. Its swim
waves the spine side to side (a true seal's stroke is its hind flippers sweeping sideways), which on a beach would read as a wriggle.

**What changed:**
- **The kit** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/creatures.js`, rebundled to `vendor/creatures.builder.js` by `scripts/creatures_bundle.py`): a
  `hump` gait, the seal's galumph. It is the swim's wave from head to tail, turned into the vertical plane (amplitude .3), with the chest
  lifting off the ground once a cycle and the fore-flippers pressing harder (.35 against the swim's .22). It is short: `strideBL` .25, a
  quarter of the body a cycle, where every other body-length gait keeps .7 (`strideOf` reads `strideBL` and falls back to .7, so no other
  plan changes). The seal's gaits are now `swim`, `hump`, `stand`.
- **The game** (`js/34-creatures.js`): `SWIMMER['Grey Seal']`, 2.0 m nose to tail on a round stand-in (`thin` 1.1, `wide` 1.7), `amph`.
  `hcFishAttach` records two fits for it: in the water its axis on the group's origin (the fish's fit), ashore its lowest point on it.
  `hcAshore` says which side of the water it is on: the foe's own `ashore` if the spawner sets it, else its group at or above `SEA_Y`.
  `hcAmph` moves the kit's body (and the stand-in) between the two fits when that changes. In the water it holds on the clock and swims by
  its group, as the fish do; ashore it lies still at the kit's `stand` and humps as its group moves, the phase by the ground covered.
  `HC_KINDS` cell .03: 5,488 triangles and 13 bones.

As with the rest of the sea line, nothing rolls it yet. Its spawns are the systems builder's: hauled out on the coast's rocks and sand in
groups, in the shallows off them.
