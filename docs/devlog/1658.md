## v80 — Session 1658 — the ladder's ring: an 8 m rope ring, three tiers of stands and the post with its names, at each of the ladder's sites (arenas session 2)

The build sheet's look session (`docs/design/arenas-build.md`, Michael's B on #914) asks for "the yard behind the hall, the 8 m ring
with stands and the post, in the Mark's kit". Session 1657 carried the yard; this session builds the ring. The systems builder's
sessions 3–5 left the whole ladder waiting on it: `ladderCall` is what a ring's master calls, and it defaulted to the town's
centre, since no ring stood anywhere. The ring's own reference is OG-089 (the yard). The arenas' references OG-079 and OG-080 are
the rings of the legends and give no shape beyond "the rings".

**The piece, `PKIT.mk.ring` (`38-kit.js`).** Trodden earth 9 m round, the yard's ragged disc in eight rings. Twenty ash stakes at 8 m
carry the yard's rope at 0.9 m, sagging 10 cm a span. The rope breaks at the gate, two taller posts (1.75 m) 1.6 m apart facing the
town. The stands are three arcs of 0.95 rad across the ring from the gate, with an aisle of 0.3 rad between each, so the gate's side
is open for 170°. Each arc has six segments, each segment three tiers 0.75 m deep. The seats are planks at 0.45, 0.9 and 1.35 m, with
a footboard under the upper two. A segment's seats are level whatever the ground does: the legs reach down to it. The post stands
beside the gate on the town's side. It is squared oak 3.1 m high with a cap and a finial, and six carved boards down its face for
the names. Everything is in the yard's two materials (rope, earth) and the hall's wood (beam, carve, two of the plank shades), so in
a town with a yard the ring adds no material. It is 4,684 tris in 6 draws.

**Where it stands (`83-world-generator.js`).** On every site `ladderSites` names: Caer Slige, the Mark's cities, padded with its largest
towns to four. On seed 1 that is six sites: Caer Slige (a town on this seed) and five cities. The ring reaches 12.4 m from its centre
to the back of the stands. No block inside the walls has that room (the yard's 6.2 m already did not fit behind the halls), so it
stands outside the walls, as Rowe's ring does. The search starts on the side of the town away from its main road. It steps out from
4 m to 24 m in 2 m steps and round in 0.2 rad steps. It uses the yard's test, now shared as `kitClearAt(x, z, out, reach)`: clear
of every road by 1.5 m, of every lot, plot and solid by 0.6 m, on ground that falls under 1.6 m. It also keeps 3 m clear of the
yard's ring and of Rowe's (east of Caer Slige's walls). The gate faces the town. A noFlat stamp clears the trees and leaves the
ground as it lies, and the 18 stand segments and the post are solids. `S.ring` holds its centre, the gate and the post.

**Its near and far.** The first pictures showed only the far disc, even standing on it. The kit town's near/far switch (S1073) is
measured from the town's centre less half its lots' reach. A ring 20 m past the walls of a city is always past the 70 m line, so the
town's far copy was all it ever had. The ring now has its own pair, `S.ringLod` (the piece, and a 20-sided earth disc), measured from
the ring by `houseLod`'s same rule. The yard had the same exposure, smaller, so a yard outside the walls now counts in the town's
reach (`S.kitLod.r`).

**Its use.** At the gate, E asks for a bout: `ladderCall({site, x, z})` with the ring's centre, so the bout is fought in this rope and
not at the town's centre. Until the quest writer's ring master stands there, the gate takes the call. At the post, E reads it: the
champion's name ("your own name, fresh in the wood" while you hold the title, else `rings.champion`), and the names of the fighters
killed after they yielded (`rings.by[site].dead`). The names are read, not drawn: six blank boards on the post stand for them.
