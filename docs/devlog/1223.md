## v80 — Session 1223 — a metre's catch-up is not a run: the people's stride on the new look (card F4, part eight)
Michael, 20 Oct 2026, in chat (carried to F4's card): fix the people stride failure (backlog I, `tests/people` on the new look) while
this card owns `js/32-people.js`. The check "walking: the stride tracks the ground, the standing foot holds still while the other
swings" failed on the new look every time the suite was run on its own here (3 of 3: the phase advanced 0.832 and 0.848 where 0.143
was expected, Niall at scale 1.307, the standing foot sliding 14–16 mm), and passed on the retry when run through `run.mjs` (FLAKY).
It is one of the suites #443 (the new look as every suite's default) waits on.

**What was wrong.** Not the walk. The card's note read the numbers as a cycle three times too fast; logging the suite's own loop tick by
tick showed the phase stepping 0.0043 a tick, a third of the walk's 0.0127, with `rig.run` on and the run's weight at 0.53 to 0.99
for the first 24 ticks. The run's cycle is the longer one (2.12 against the walk's 0.72), so the phase fell behind the ground and the
planted foot slid. The run was latched before the walk began: the suite moves the clock to noon and runs 120 world ticks in one
evaluate, with no `tickPeople` between, so the town's schedule carried Niall on by most of a metre; the suite's first `tickPeople`
read that as one step in 1/60 s, a pace of 5 m/s, and `rig.v` took half a second to fall under the run's threshold. On the old look
the browser drew frames between the two evaluates and each took a little of the jump at a real dt; the new look's frames are slower,
so none came between. In play the same thing happens after a hitch (the one-second stall when a builder body is built, a save
loading, a slow frame that the loop clamps to 50 ms while the world moves on): a townsperson breaks into a run for half a second.

**The change** (`tickPeople`, `js/32-people.js`): a move of more than 12 m/s in one tick is a teleport, as a jump over 1.5 m already
was: it adds nothing to the stride and nothing to the pace. No one in the game walks or runs that fast (the run starts at about
1.25 m/s, a foe's chase is a few metres a second, and a lunge moves the enemy's group, not its feet). The check is unchanged; a
new one beside it moves the walked person a metre in one tick and asserts no run.
