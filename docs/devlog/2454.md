**What was built** (`docs/prototypes/levelup/ruins.js` `stormwatch`, `places-stormwatch-view.js`, `places.html?place=stormwatch`; the
prototype only):
- **The cliff.** 88 m of grey limestone in beds, sheer to the sea, its edge wavering 6 m either way along 1.5 km of coast. The face
  steps in and out by bed, by up to 4.5 m over larger stretches, and is notched at the waves' line. It is wet and dark under 4 m and has
  lichen near the top. Ninety boulders lie at its foot, half in the sea, with foam along it.
- **The land.** It falls 3 m in 100 inland, stepped in low risers of the bedding. Turf covers it, with the bare pavement showing through in
  patches. The rock is bare along the edge (the salt burns the turf) and inside the cashel. The pavement is a texture of its own: clints
  between grikes that run one way and waver, 0.8 to 1.6 m apart, cut across by joints, drawn only where the ground is bare (a `pave`
  weight per vertex in the land's shader).
- **The inner wall (the cashel).** A half-ellipse 52 by 44 m about a heart on the edge. It is 4.2 m thick in three bands, the outer 5.4 m
  high and the two inside it 3.6 and 1.8 m, so its inner face is two terraces, with flights of steps up them at three places. The way in
  is a passage 1.3 m wide and 4.6 m long on the north-east, under lintels at 2 m. Inside is the bare rock, and by the edge a platform of
  one bed of it standing proud.
- **The middle wall.** 116 by 104 m, 3 m thick, about 3.2 m high. It has a gap on the way in and two breaches thrown down outward, their
  stones on the turf.
- **The set stones.** 453 slabs of the pavement set upright and leaning, 0.4 to 1.8 m high, in a band 14 m deep outside the middle
  wall on its west and north. Nothing leads through them.
- **The outer wall.** 236 by 200 m, 2.2 m thick. It is down to its footing for most of its run, standing to its 2.2 m in stretches,
  with a spill of its stone either side where it is down, and a gap on the way in.
- **All three walls stop at the cliff at both ends**, so the fort's seaward half is gone.
- **Dry stone.** The walls are a texture of their own, 4 m a tile: courses of uneven height (0.2 to 0.45 m), stones 0.25 to 0.95 m long,
  each its own grey, tight joints, lichen in patches, and the same canvas as a bump map. The kit's coursed `stone` and `lichen` both
  read as brickwork at this size.
- A 1.75 m post inside the passage's inner mouth is the scale. Nothing is lit, and there is no Weaver stone and no script.
- 83,090 triangles in 11 draws: land, cliff, sea, foam, walls, steps, lintels, set stones, fallen stones, the platform and the post.

### Decision
**DECISION #1815: Stormwatch — what was the fort, and is anyone in it?** (with the pictures)
- **A (recommended).** As built: the older people's fort, empty, a landmark on the Gatelands' cliffs. You walk the walls and the
  terraces for the view. What it was for stays a mystery, as the panel says, until the quest writer gives it a beat.
- **B.** Held by bandits (OG-121's "ruined forts taken by bandits"): a camp in the cashel and a way down a cleft to a sea cave, so
  it is a place that is not a gate (card F8), with foes.
- **C.** As the panel paints it: a tower raised in the cashel by a later people to watch the sea, itself a ruin, climbed for the
  view. This adds a structure the canon does not yet have.
A, because a Weaver-free ruin with no story yet is the quest writer's to fill, and B can be laid over it later without tearing anything
up.
