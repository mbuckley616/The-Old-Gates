## v80 — Session 2288 — the deck you walk: a bridge's setts underfoot, and rails in their own frame
The look builder's run of 16 Nov, on `auto/look-2285` from origin/main at s1633. Written as 2285; the bodies builder (2285) and the systems builder (2286–2287) landed while it was in work, so it is 2288. No `FREEZE:` issue was open. The last look branch
(2280, PR for Session 2281) had landed, and its last commit was two hours old. Section I holds no open item for this lane, so the run took
the item Session 2281 left owed to it: "on an intact deck you stand on the causeway, 0.8 m under the setts".

**What was wrong: three faults, one under the other.**
- **The deck was not your ground.** You walk on `worldH`, and under a bridge that is the road's causeway (the road's blend), not the
  setts. Measured on the nine bridges loaded at the start, the setts stood 0.53 to 0.83 m over the causeway at a shallow crossing,
  2 m over it at a deeper one, and 12.2 m over it at the deepest (`bridge_5,10_26`, a 16 m drop to the bed). You walked inside the bridge
  with the rails at your chest.
- **The rails lay across the deck.** `bridgeFit` lays each rail as a solid with its turn (`c`, `s`), but `solidAt` and `camSolid`
  (`js/81-world-cells.js`) tested every static solid as a box on the world's axes. A rail 0.4 m wide and 43 m long, on a bridge at 25°,
  became a box 0.4 m across x and 43 m along z, which lies across the deck. Walking `bridge_6,9_23` you met it 6 m short of the middle,
  slid along it off the side and swam. `bridge_5,9_21` stopped you dead at its middle. This has been so since the rails were made
  solids (S248); the causeway hid it on bridges near north–south. The caves', the sea caves' and the aqueduct's blocks carry a turn too
  and were tested the same way.
- **A redrawn bridge could lose its rails.** A loading cell tags every static solid pushed since its first step with its own key
  (`STATIC_SOL[n].cell=k` from `solStart`), in both the cell's steps and its statics. A bridge redrawn while another cell was loading
  (`tickBridges` does this at any minute; the suite did it after the drain) had its rails retagged to that cell. They then went with that
  cell's unload while the bridge stood, so six of the nine bridges had no rails at all during the walk. `bridgeFit`'s splice also
  shifted the indices under a cell that was loading.
- **A far cell's rails went for good.** This was the one that held the suite red after both fixes above. `dropStatics` (a loaded cell
  more than 900 m from you, `FAR_STATIC`) dropped every solid tagged with the cell. That took the solids the cell's own steps had laid
  as well as the statics': a bridge's rails, a quay's walls (`addQuayWalls`). Those steps do not run again while the cell stays loaded,
  so the meshes stood and their solids were gone until the cell unloaded. The suite's walk, from the bridges of cell 6,9 to those of 5,10
  and 5,9, took those cells past 900 m and back.

**What changed.**
- `bridgeWalk` (`js/82-world-structures.js`): each run of the deck (one whole, two either side of a gap) is a platform of the world's,
  as an aqueduct's channel is. Its top is the setts' (`BRIDGE_SETTS`, 0.28 over the mesh's origin), and it is 2.25 m either side of the
  line, inside the rails. The road meets the deck's ends at its own height, so the last 2.5 m of each end (`BRIDGE_RAMP`) ramp from the
  ground under you up to the setts. Where a bank stands over the deck's end, the ground wins. The player takes the deck when their feet
  are within 0.6 of its top, so a swimmer under the arches stays in the water. Anyone else takes it when the ground is within 0.6. Laid
  by `bridgeFit` (so a redraw, a fall and a mend relay it) and dropped with the cell (`bridgeWalkDrop`, from `unloadCell`).
- `solBoxHit` (`js/81-world-cells.js`): a static solid that carries a turn is tested in its own frame, as `impactInBox` (74) and the towns'
  own tests already did. `solidAt` and `camSolid` both use it. A solid without a turn is tested exactly as before.
- The loading cells' tag is written only on a solid that names no cell (`if(STATIC_SOL[n].cell==null)`, in `81` and `82`). A bridge's
  rails name theirs, and the wonders' `wonder:<name>` tags are now safe too. The statics' own solids are marked (`statics:true`), and
  `dropStatics` drops only those. The cell's own solids go with the cell, as their meshes do. `bridgeFit` rewrites a bridge's rails in place rather than
  splicing. A rail no longer wanted (mended, four to two) is emptied (`rx`/`rz` −1) and lets go of its bridge until its cell unloads.
- No change to any mesh: the bridges are the same triangles (5,780 to 11,316 on the nine loaded).
