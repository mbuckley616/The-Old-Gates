## v80 — Session 1293 — F5: a gate laid as spaces (card F5, FREEZE #583)
Card F5, session 2 of the build sheet `docs/design/gate-interiors-build.md` (Michael's B on #448: "Spaces first, rooms after, on one
grid"), on `auto/fable-f5-gates-2`, cut from main after session 1's #592 landed. Numbered 1284 when it began; the bodies builder's 1284
landed first and 1290–1292 are taken on other branches, so it is 1293.

**What was wrong.** After session 1 a gate was today's rooms and corridors at three times the size: rooms of 9 to 36 m joined by
3 m corridors under one flat 6 m roof, "a Skyrim ruin, roomy but still rooms and corridors". The brief and Michael (12 and 14 Oct)
want an old gate shockingly large: halls, not corridors.

**The space pass** (`52-dungeon-gen.js`). An old gate (a cave portal that is not a fort, a lair, a den or the crypt: those keep their
rooms, #421) is laid by `makeGate(size, seed)` instead of `makeDungeon`. Its grid is the sheet's: 48 cells (144 m) for a tiny or small
gate, 64 for medium and large, 90 for massive. It lays the spaces first, from the sheet's table, in cells of 3 m:
- a **nave** 9 cells (27 m) wide and 34 to 84 long by size (100 to 250 m), its barrel vault 40 to 50 m, two lines of piers 2 m round
  every 14 m with aisles of 2 cells outside them, and 2 to 4 side chapels of 3 × 4 cells open to the aisle (12 m roofs);
- a **crossing** 13 × 13 under a 45 m dome;
- a **chasm** 14 cells across and 20 to 40 long, its floor gone 25 m down between two ledges of 3 cells, crossed by a bridge 2 cells
  wide with a landing every 16 m span (the piers between spans); the open air is solid to walk into until session 4 builds falling;
- a **flooded hall** 10 × 27 under a 30 m vault, water standing in it, with 2 or 3 islands of slab 0.4 high (a step up);
- the **ring chamber** 20 × 20 under a 30 to 40 m dome, a dais 10 m square in its middle, always last.
How many: 1 space and the ring in a tiny or small gate, 2 to 3 in medium and large, 4 in massive. The layout is drawn from the size's
list (linear only for the small gates; linear, hub or ring of loops for medium and large; any of the six for massive, where the
labyrinth and the cave network are trees off a random earlier space, the ring of loops and the cave add up to two shortcuts, and the
vertical complex is linear until floors hold spaces). The first space faces a side of the grid, the way in a straight corridor to it;
each next space is set against its parent with exactly one cell of rock between, and the **threshold** is one cell of that rock opened,
3 m wide and 8 m high, with floor (never a pier or the chasm) on both sides. A hub's arms hang off its crossing; the ring hangs off
the deepest space, so it is always the farthest from the way in, with one way into it. Then **side rooms** (3 to 6, 6 to 12, 12 to 18;
3 to 12 cells, 9 to 36 m) are dropped in the rock left over, each joined by an L corridor to the nearest space's edge, refused if the
corridor would touch anything but its own room and that one space: no corridor ever joins two spaces. Every roll is the gate's own
stream (its seed, hashed), so one seed lays one gate on any machine.

What follows is makeDungeon's own code, cut out as `dunAfterLayout` and shared: treasure rooms off the side rooms, locked and unlocked
doors (never in a threshold), the well and floor two (massive only), the keys. makeDungeon calls it with the same stream in the same
order: 150 caves of every size laid by main's makeDungeon and by this branch's are byte-identical.

**The roof by point** (`dCeil`). `gen.ceilAt(c, r, X, Z)` is a space's vault or dome at a point, a chapel's 12, a threshold's 8, or the
dungeon's 6; `D_CEIL` holds it per floor and `dCeil(x, z)` reads it in metres. The head's stop (`headCeil`) and the third-person
camera's roof (`tpCeil`) read it instead of `FLOOR_HEIGHT`. `FLOOR_HEIGHT` stays the gate's 6: corridors, side rooms, doors and the
blades under it are as session 1 left them.

**The shell** (`56-dungeon-build.js`). `buildDunShell` takes the roof by point: each wall face rises to the roof of its open cell at
that point (a nave's end wall is a tympanum to its vault), the ceiling's grid follows the vault (finer, 4 a cell, under a roof that
curves), and where two open cells' roofs differ a face closes the step, facing the taller (a threshold's 8 m lintel up to a nave's
springing, a corridor's 6 into a hall). A chasm's cells have no floor; their walls go on down 25 m, a cliff faces the pit wherever the
floor ends, and a bridge's deck is 1.5 thick. A gate's walls have no cove (its vaults are the springing now). `buildGateSpaces` adds
what the shell does not draw, in one merged mesh stretched by the cell: the piers to the vault (and `DUNGEON_COLUMNS`, 2 m round),
the chasm's floor and the bridges' undersides, the islands and the dais (and their `FOOTHOLDS`), and the water. A gate is prewarmed
(`REN.compile`) behind the fade: the water's material is new, and `dunwarm` caught it compiling on first sight. The fog in a gate
reaches 90 to 150 m. The minimap draws the chasm black and a pier as rock. A sigil (`SIGIL_PLACEMENTS`) stands on the ring chamber's
wall on floor one in a gate laid as spaces ("a lesser gate's sigil", the sheet), since a medium or large gate has no floor two now.

**What changed for play beyond the shape.** A medium or large gate is one floor (the sheet's table: two floors only in a massive one),
so it holds its 14 or 20 foes once, not twice, and its tier-2 foes (wraiths, gargoyles, mimics) only in a massive gate's floor two. To
keep what a clear finds, a gate of one floor puts a chest in a room's corner twice as often (`lootpicks`, below). The beams of rooms are not hung in the spaces. Traps stay in corridors and may stand in a
threshold (the sheet: side rooms and thresholds).

**Left for the next session on this card**, and why: the **stair hall** is the way between two floors, and only a massive gate has two;
its floor two is still rooms and corridors under the well, and so a chasm is not rolled in a massive gate (its 25 m pit would cut through
floor two, 7.8 m down). Building floor two as spaces joined by a stair hall of 2 to 3 flights is the next session.
