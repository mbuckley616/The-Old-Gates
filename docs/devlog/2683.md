## v80 — Session 2683 — The light by the hour, tuned against P10b: Michael's night (#629) and islands (#719), night's colour, storms, painted clouds (deep work, light and grade)
The second session of the light-and-grade card. (Renumbered: written as Sessions 2660–2661, then 2676–2677; each pair was taken on main first, so the card's two sessions are 2682 and 2683.) Session 2682 built the curve, the haze, the grade, the sky and the clouds; this one

**The islands' air (#719).** `LIGHT_PAL` is now each island's `air.js` row as a ratio to the Gatelands' row, laid over the hour's
curve. The Gatelands soft and hazy (the haze ×1.1: about 2.5 km of sight at noon), a touch less saturated, green-grey water. The Mark
a harder, stronger sun (×1.2) with less fill (the hemisphere ×0.8), a deep blue sky (the zenith and middle ×0.62/0.82/1.02), crisp
air (a third of the haze: about 8 km), dark water, about half the clouds, and a cool, contrasty, less saturated grade (warmth −0.3,
saturation −0.14, contrast +0.12). Aurenne a fierce white sun (×1.2, a little blue in it), a bleached warm horizon and haze (about
4 km), a warm ground's bounce, turquoise water, a sky nearly bare of cloud (`cover` 0.3: each heap has a number, and a heap over the
island's share is not drawn, `aHeap`/`uCover`; an overcast brings every heap back), and a warm, saturated grade (+0.3, +0.14).
Session 2682's ochre Aurennais sun was the wrong way round: `air.js` makes it the whitest of the three. Still owed from #719: the sun's
height by island (`air.js` puts a mid-afternoon sun at 35° in the Gatelands, 21° in the Mark and 64° in Aurenne). The game's sun has
one path for the world, and changing it moves every shadow and the dome's checks (`f3land` reads the sun at noon); it is a small
follow-up, not done here.

**The night (#629's C).** The moon's own light is its lit share (Session 2682 kept a third of it under a new moon), and the floor keeps a fifth of a full moon's light on the land under a new one: the moon carries about two thirds of a full-moon night, so the fill (ambient and the hemisphere's sky) is scaled by `1 − 0.45 × (1 − lit)` and the sky and haze by `1 − 0.6 × (1 − lit)`, which keeps the sky lighter than the land, so the skyline and the roofs read against it and a lit window or a lamp carries across a dark field. A first try scaled everything by `nightRow`'s 0.2 + 0.8 × lit, but there the floor is the prototype's hemisphere, the bulk of its night; here it left a new moon near black (`night.jpg` shows the result). The exposure opens 6% at night. Tuned by the shots: Session 2682's first night (ambient 0.10, hemisphere 0.12,
moon 0.30) left the land black at 23h and the ground near black at 18h; the night is now moon 0.55, ambient 0.22, hemisphere 0.30
at a full moon, and every low-sun key has more fill (ambient 0.26–0.30, hemisphere 0.38–0.45) and exposure (1.08–1.10).

**Night's colour.** Under a blue moon a green leaf stays green while a white wall turns blue, so the town's trees stood out bright
green at 23h. Eyes lose colour in the dark, and P10b's night is painted so: a negative saturation in the grade now drains the dark
tones and spares the bright ones (`1 − smoothstep(.15, .55, luminance)`), and the night's is −0.6 (−0.4 at the blue hour, −0.25
before dawn). The moonlit land goes blue-grey while a lamp's pool on the road keeps its amber.

**Storms.** The sun's light under cloud now falls with it (×(1 − 0.65 × cloud), on top of the weather's own `WX.sunMul`), so a storm
casts no shadows (the land stood sunlit under a black sky); the clouds' lit colour turns to the storm's grey (`uHi` toward the sky's
middle and horizon by up to 0.85), and the sea under a lid is 30% duller.

**The clouds.** Session 2682's heaps shaded each puff as a ball and read as clusters of spheres. Each puff is now shaded by its
heap's roundness with only a little of its own (0.18), its edge is torn by two octaves of noise (`er`) instead of a smooth falloff,
the terminator is softer, and the puffs are larger (0.5–0.84 of the heap's size) so they merge: a heap reads as one cumulus with a
lit, cream top, a grey-blue or violet underside and a flat base. At night the lit side follows the sky's brightness, not the moon's
strength, so night clouds are dim silver, not white.

**Stars** wait for the blue hour (`sstep(.6, 1, night)`), so a sunset sky has none (Session 2682, in `skyBodies`).
