## v80 — Session 1299 — F5: a gate drawn by space, and lit by eight (card F5, FREEZE #583)
Card F5's goal 5, the build sheet's "Light and draws": only your space and the spaces joined to it are drawn and lit, at most 8 point
lights live, and a space with its neighbours stays under 200 draw calls, measured at five spots a gate size. On `auto/fable-f5-gates-4`,
cut from `auto/fable-f5-gates-3`'s head while its PR (#604) waits to land.

**What was wrong.** Session 1298 measured it: the most draws over four headings, at the way in and each space, ran 112 to 186 in a
small gate, 187 to 288 in a large and 336 to 578 in a massive one. Every gate held 22 live lights: the light pool every dungeon has
(`_sweepScenePool`, 20 lights moved to the nearest sources each frame) and the ambient. The sheet allows 200 draws and 8 lights.

**Zones** (`dZoneBuild`, `56-dungeon-build.js`, at the end of buildDungeon for a gate). Every open cell gets a zone: a space's own cells
(and its chapels, which `cellSpace` already gives it) are that space's zone; each side room is its own zone; everything else (a
threshold, the way in, a side room's corridor, a treasure room) takes the zone it is nearest to, by a breadth-first fill over the open
cells from all of them at once. Two zones that touch through an open side are joined, and so are the spaces the generator's `links`
and thresholds join (the stair hall is one zone on both of a massive gate's floors, joined to the space on each). `dZoneShown` gives
the zones drawn from your cell: in a space, that space and the spaces joined to it, and each of its side rooms that a sightline over
the map reaches from your cell (any cell of the room or its corridor; a quarter-cell step, rock stops it); in a side room, the room
and its space. It is worked out again only when you step into another cell.

**The frame** (`dRender`; the loop in `90-main.js` renders `dScene` through it, one line). Each top-level piece of the scene is measured
once (its bounds' middle as an offset from its place, its radius, and whether its bounds reach into two zones), then each frame read at
its place plus that offset, so a foe that walks into the next space goes with it. A piece in a zone not drawn is hidden for the render
and shown again straight after it (in a `finally`), so nothing that reads `.visible` (a foe, a chest, a door, the townsfolk's levels)
ever sees it hidden. A piece reaching into two zones, the shell, the lights and a skinned body's own bounds are left alone (a body is
read at its place, 3 m round). One more cut, because it is exact: a piece whose nearest depth along the view is past the fog's far
edge (90 to 150 m in a gate) is drawn there in the fog's colour, which is the background's, so it is not drawn. three's fog reads
depth along the view, not distance, so the test is the depth, not the distance.

**Lights** (`_sweepScenePool`, `68-dungeon-misc.js`). The pool's size is the scene's `userData.lightPool`, 20 as before, 8 in a gate;
the gate's pool is made at the end of the build so the compile behind the fade has its lights (a changed light count recompiles
every program). In a gate the 8 go to the nearest sources in the zones drawn, by their distance up and down as well as across, so
floor two's torches never take a light from floor one's.

Every other dungeon (forts, lairs, dens, the crypt) has no zones and its pool of 20, unchanged.

  - the cull hides nothing in view: each view rendered whole and culled under a flat white light differs by 0 pixels at all 100

### Needs eyes
- A gate is darker: 8 lights where there were 20. The sheet asks for it, but a nave was already black past its torches; the look
  builder's braziers and the script's glow (by emissive colour, not lights) are what lights it now.
- What pops: the sightline into a side room is taken from your cell's middle on the map, so stepping a cell along a space can show or
  hide a side room you see down its corridor at a slant. The pixel check found nothing at the 100 views it took; a playtest walking
  past the corridors is the real test. A side room of the next space, seen through a threshold and down its corridor, is not drawn.
- In third person the sightline is from your cell, not the camera's: a camera pulled back into a corridor sees what you see.
