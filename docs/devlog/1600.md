## v80 — Session 1600 — Say the Name at the Door (Asked in Passing, errand 4)
The quest writer's build run takes the oldest approval still unfinished: *Asked in Passing* (Michael's approval of 14 Oct 2026), whose
frame and errands 3 and 1 are on main (Sessions 1510 and 1554). Errand 4, *Say the Name at the Door*, is next. Errand 1's hand-off
waits on it: when the letter's addressee turns out to be dead and you tell the giver so, the giver asks for the name. It needs no
new system. A world gate's door already carries a prompt and an E of its own (the great gate's way back, Session 1354), and this
errand adds one more of the same kind ahead of it.

What it does. One errand in four of a week's errands (its own stream, `errand-name`, keyed by town, giver and week) is the name. A
Gatelander or Aurennais giver can ask it; a Markman can ask only if their bio has no spouse; an Old Blood giver never asks it. The
dead is named from the giver's people's bank, buried on a weekday in the past week (`calDay`). The gate is the nearest old gate
within 1,200 of the town, not counting forts, lairs and great gates (`erGate`; a great gate's door has its own prompt). The hint
follows First Words from the second meeting, as the other errands do. The ask is *Can I do anything for you?* in *About you*, and
the offer is the draft's, word for word, by people. At the gate's door, while the name is owed there, the prompt is *Press 'E' to
say the name* and E says it (`erNameSay`). The log and a message carry *You say the name at the door of {gate}: {name}.* For a
player who has stood on the Ashfeld (`story.choice`), who has met Varek at any of his fields (`varek.done`), or who is Old Blood,
the second line follows in `#a8b8c8`: *The stone is warm under your hand. Then it is not.* After that the door is a door again.
Back at the giver, the turn-in is *I said the name.* and the answer is the draft's. The pay is the draft's too: a Gatelander gives
a Loaf of Bread and 10 gold, an Aurennais the fee (the tier's base), a Markman nothing but the drink owed. Each also gives a point
of the town's favour. An Old Blood giver whose week rolls the name has only the one line to the ask (*We bury ours in the ground…*),
with no errand and no hint. On the truth of a death in errand 1, a giver who is not Old Blood offers the name at once, the player's
*Can I do anything for you?* following the truth line, for the one who died (`erNameAfter`).

Two plain choices where the draft and the code met. The draft has a Markman giver *only if a widow or widower (bio.spouse set and
the roll)*. But a townsperson with `bio.spouse` set answers *Who are you?* with *Married to {spouse}* and *Family?* with the
spouse's name, both alive, so a dead spouse would be contradicted in the same conversation. A Markman asks only with no spouse in
their bio: some of them already answer *Family?* with *Not anymore.* The draft's showMsg and its second line would overwrite each
other on the one message line. The first shows, and the second follows it 2.4 s later. Both are written to the record (`said`,
`felt`) before either is shown, so a dropped frame cannot change what happened.
