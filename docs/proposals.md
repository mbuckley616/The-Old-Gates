## A watch that walks at night, and guards who follow (2026-09-27)

The canon already asks for the other half: §12, *favour ≤ −2: guards follow*, which the build doesn't do.
**What.** (1) At night the town's guards walk a beat that passes the shop doors — a loop through the lots of type weapon/armor/potion/misc, a lantern in the left hand (the torch the S153 guard already carries), at the guard's pace; a town of prosperity ≥ 60 gets a third guard at night. (2) With favour ≤ −2 in a town, the nearest guard on duty trails you at six to eight units while you are inside its pad, and stops if you leave or go indoors. Witnessing is unchanged: the guard is simply there to see.
**Cost.** One Opus session: the guard schedule in `scheduleFor`/`tickNPCs` (a beat built from the settlement's house list), a follow state beside `_scared`, a test in the style of `crime2`. No story or quest text touched.

**Recommendation.** Do it before tuning any of the crime numbers: until someone can see at night, the balance questions S155–S158 left under *Needs eyes* can't be answered by play.

## Cargo goes by sea (2026-10-02)
**Why.** Sessions 390–399 made the hold, the blockade and the black sails who take half of it. In play on s360 none of it has to be met. A level-1 back carries 65–81 against a sloop's 40, the map's travel crosses to any port you have been to for no gold, and a foreign factor buys from your back: nine bales of wool from Portclare to Camuros made 72 clear without a ship. The shipwright's *Fetch* also brings your ship across the sea with her hold full, for at most 150. Canon §1 sets the islands' trade on the sea, and Michael's *sailing as a loop* (J) wants the voyage to be the game. As built, the voyage is the one way of trading that costs something.
**What.** (1) A factor's crate is too heavy to carry ashore: it goes in your hold or nowhere. `cargoBuy` refuses the bag path, which ends the back-and-map route at its root. (2) *Fetch* will not bring a ship with cargo aboard (*Not with a hold full. Sail her round yourself.*), or brings her and leaves the cargo where she lay. (3) The map's travel stays as it is for people. No other travel changes.
**Cost.** Under one Opus session for the systems builder: three guards in `js/80-world.js` (`cargoBuy`, the fetch topic in `upgradeTopics`) and changes to `cargo.test`/`piratehold.test`. No story, quest or canon touched.
**Risk / displaces.** It makes the trade slower: a crossing is about 26 minutes of play at a sloop's 7.5. The ×0.6/×1.4 margins were set by Session 390 for a route nobody had to sail, and may want raising with this. The sea tutorial's *crossing* step is unaffected. Portclare's reach (backlog I, s360) must be fixed first, or the home port could not load cargo at all.
**Recommendation.** Do (1) and (2) together, then sail one real crossing before touching the margins.

## The cellar's chest keeps what you leave in it (2026-10-05)
**Why.** In play on s418, a house at Carraig Mór costs 900. What it gives is a bed (an inn room is 10–40 a night), a door that never locks, and a cellar whose chest is set dressing: E beside it does nothing, and `intStashPos` is set only on a ship's cabin. The session that planned buying (devlog, Session 52's list: *a stash + your own bed*) wanted somewhere to keep things. The game already has one store, `stashBag` behind `openStash()`: Caldric's safehouse in Ironhaven, and the cabin's hold. Canon §12a counts `owned` among a town's states and §12 gives a deed at favour 5, so a house is meant to matter to the player. As built, a carried bag is the only place a player's things can be.
**What.** (1) In a cellar under a house you own, the kit's chest (at W/2, D−1.4) is the stash: `intStashPos` is set there and E opens the same panel. (2) It is one store shared with the safehouse and the cabin, as `stashBag` is today. No per-house inventories: that would be a new save field, and it would be the world's, not the character's, under the co-op rules. (3) The prompt reads *Press 'E' to open the chest*, as the safehouse's does.
**Cost.** Well under one Opus session: a line in the cellar branch of `buildInteriorFor` (`js/83-world-generator.js`) guarded by `parent.ownedByPlayer`, and a check in a new `house` suite that would also cover the three bugs filed today (buying twice, the seller at the door, the cellar's label). No story, quest or canon text touched.
**Risk / displaces.** One shared store reached from many houses lets a player move goods between towns for free. That already happens with the safehouse and the hold, and per-house stores would cost a save field and a co-op decision. Fix the double sale first, or a player may pay twice for the chest.
**Recommendation.** Do it with the fixes for the double sale, as one small session. Give each house its own store only if Michael wants houses to be places for storage.
